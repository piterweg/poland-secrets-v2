SMALL TOWNS

Small towns that
remember more.

Kazimierz Dolny, Zamość, Sandomierz, Kłodzko and other small towns where time flows slower and history is part of everyday life.

Why Small Towns?

Big cities attract tourists, but it is small towns that preserve the true soul of Poland. There are no crowds, no queues, no pressure to "see everything." There is a market square, a café, a church, a city wall — and there is time to really see them.

Many of these towns have preserved their original medieval or Renaissance urban layout. Zamość was designed with a ruler, Paczków has defensive walls almost untouched since the 14th century, and Kazimierz Dolny became a second home for generations of Polish artists.

Slow Travel in a Small Town

A perfect day in a small town looks like this: arrive in the morning, walk around the market square before the crowds, breakfast in a local bakery, visit one or two monuments, lunch in a family restaurant, afternoon coffee, a walk around the walls or by the river, depart at sunset.

Don't try to see everything. Choose one or two places, and leave the rest for next time. Small towns are meant to be returned to.

Where to Start?

For a first trip — Kazimierz Dolny on the Vistula, with loess ravines and a market square lined with Renaissance townhouses. For architecture lovers — Zamość, the pearl of the Renaissance and one of the few cities in the world designed entirely by a single architect.

For those looking for less obvious places — Chełmno with six Gothic churches, Paczków with its defensive walls, or Supraśl with an Orthodox monastery in the middle of a forest.

ALL SMALL TOWNS

Which one will you discover?

Kazimierz Dolny

LUBLIN REGION • 1–2 days

Artists' Town on the Vistula

A market square with historic townhouses, loess ravines, and artists' workshops. Perfect for a slow weekend.

Chełmno

KUYAVIA-POMERANIA • 1 day

Town of the Lovestruck

Medieval walls and six Gothic churches. A quiet little town with a view of the Vistula.

Kłodzko

LOWER SILESIA • 1–2 days

Fortress on the Nysa Kłodzka

A town on the Czech border with an impressive fortress and Gothic bridge. Gateway to the Sudetes.

Paczków

OPOLE REGION • 1 day

The Polish Carcassonne

One of the best-preserved fortified towns in Poland. Walls, towers, and a gate that remembers the Middle Ages.

Cieszyn

SILESIA • 1 day

A Town on the Border

Cieszyn Venice, the Three Brothers Well, and the Friendship Bridge. Coffee on one side of the Olza, dessert on the other.

Przemyśl

SUBCARPATHIA • 1–2 days

City on Seven Hills

The Fortress, cathedrals of three rites, and a panorama of the Carpathians. The eastern gate of Poland at a slow pace.

Sanok

SUBCARPATHIA • 1 day

Gateway to the Bieszczady

An open-air museum of wooden architecture and an Icon Museum. A perfect base for Bieszczady trips.

Krynica-Zdrój

LESSER POLAND • 2 days

Pearl of Polish Spas

Pump room, promenade, and Parkowa Hill with a funicular. Rest in the rhythm of an old spa town.

Szczawnica

LESSER POLAND • 2 days

Spa at the Foot of the Pieniny

Wooden architecture, mineral water, and views of Trzy Korony. A perfect base for a Dunajec raft trip.

Supraśl

PODLASIE • 1 day

Orthodox Monastery in the Forest

A monastery surrounded by the Knyszyn Forest. Iconostasis, chants, and a silence that soothes.
