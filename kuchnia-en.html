Flavors that

From Podlasie kibiny to Kashubian herring, from Podhale oscypek to Silesian roulade. A guide to Poland's regional cuisines.

Polish Cuisine Is Not One Cuisine

Polish regional cuisine is in fact a dozen different cuisines, each with its own traditions, ingredients, and flavors. Podlasie eats kibiny, Kashubia — herring, Podhale — oscypek, Silesia — roulade. All these dishes are rooted in the history and geography of the region.

Slow travel through Polish flavors is a journey through culinary regions where you can taste dishes prepared with methods passed down from generation to generation. It is a journey in which a restaurant is just one stop — the real flavors hide in small inns, agritourism farms, and the homes of local hosts.

From Podlasie to Kashubia

Podlasie is kibiny, kartacze, and potato babka — borderland cuisine where Polish, Tatar, and Orthodox traditions meet. Kashubia is fish from the Baltic and lakes, herring in every form, and Kashubian broth. Podhale is sheep's cheeses, kwaśnica, and bryjka — shepherd cuisine.

Silesia is roulade, dumplings, and modra kapusta — working-class cuisine, hearty and substantial. Roztocze is pierogi with bryndza and cebularze — Polish-Ukrainian-Jewish borderland cuisine. Kurpie is rejbaki and fafernuchy. Greater Poland — St. Martin's croissants. Warmia — vendace, marzipan, and kindziuk.

How to Eat Slowly?

The rule is one: eat slowly and locally. Instead of looking for dishes in chain restaurants, it is worth entering a small inn, a milk bar, or an agritourism farm where the cook prepares what they have known since childhood. It is worth ordering one dish and truly savoring it — tasting every bite, talking with the waiter about ingredients, asking about local traditions.

Many regions have their culinary festivals — from the grape harvest in Zielona Góra to the croissant festival in Poznań. It is worth planning trips to coincide with one of these events — the flavors of the region taste completely different then.

ALL CULINARY REGIONS

What will you eat first?

Podlasie

PODLASIE • 3–4 days

Kibiny, Kartacze and Potato Babka

Borderland cuisine — Tatar, Orthodox, and Polish in one. Hearty, simple, and deeply rooted in the land.

Kashubia

POMERANIA • 2–3 days

Herring, Eel and Kashubian Broth

Fish from the Baltic and lakes, potatoes, cabbage, and mushrooms. Kashubian cuisine is the taste of the sea meeting the forest.

Podhale

LESSER POLAND • 2–3 days

Oscypek, Kwaśnica and Bryjka

Shepherd cheeses, sauerkraut, and highland soups. Cuisine straight from the shepherd's hut — thick and warming.

Silesia

SILESIA • 2 days

Roulade, Dumplings and Modra Kapusta

Silesian cuisine is substance, heartiness, and respect for tradition. Roulade with dumplings is a classic that tastes like home.

Roztocze

LUBLIN REGION • 2–3 days

Pierogi with Bryndza and Cebularze

The cuisine of Roztocze is a mix of Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish traditions. Simple, earthy, and deeply filling.

Kurpie

MASOVIA • 2 days

Rejbak, Fafernuchy and Pierogi

Kurpie cuisine is dishes made of potatoes, flour, and mushrooms. Simple, rural, and still alive in the homes of the Kurpie people.

Greater Poland

GREATER POLAND • 2 days

St. Martin's Croissants and Pyry z Gzikiem

Greater Poland's cuisine is potatoes in every form, croissants with white poppy seeds, and goose for St. Martin's Day.

Warmia-Masuria

WARMIA-MASURIA • 2–3 days

Vendace, Marzipan and Kindziuk

The cuisine of Warmia and Masuria is fish from the lakes, game, mushrooms, and Olsztyn marzipan. Rural flavors of the land of a thousand lakes.

Kociewie

POMERANIA • 1–2 days

Kociewie Soups and Baltic Herring

Kociewie cuisine is simple, nourishing soups, fish from the Baltic, and dishes made with groats. A region where home cooking tastes best.

Lemko Region

LESSER POLAND • 2 days

Proziaki, Fuczki and Lemko Kwaśnica

Lemko cuisine is simple dishes made of flour, potatoes, and cabbage. The flavors of an old village, coming back into fashion.
