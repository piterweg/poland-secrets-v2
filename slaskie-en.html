SILESIA

The Beskids, industry, and Jura castles.

A region of contrasts — from mountain trails to industrial heritage and the largest concentration of castles in Poland.

A Region of Contrasts

Silesia is Poland's most industrial region — and at the same time one of the most mountainous. The Silesian Beskids, Żywiec Beskids, Little Beskids — all within one voivodeship.

Beside mines and steelworks, the most beautiful mountain trails, wooden churches, and pastoral traditions have survived for centuries.

Jura and Castles

The Kraków-Częstochowa Jura is a limestone plateau with white rocks, caves, and castle ruins. Ogrodzieniec, Ojców, Pieskowa Skała — all within 50 kilometers.

Pszczyna, Cieszyn, Tarnowskie Góry, Bielsko-Biała — cities where Silesian history meets modern Poland. Worth discovering slowly, one per day.

DESTINATIONS IN THIS REGION

Where to go?

Żywiec Beskids

SILESIA • 2–3 days

Mountains with Room to Rest

Lush greenery, scenic summits, and unhurried paths for a zero-pressure mountain holiday.

Ogrodzieniec

SILESIA • 1 day

Eagle's Nest on the Jura

The largest castle ruins of the Kraków-Częstochowa Jura. Limestone rocks, legends, and sunset over the valley.

Pszczyna

SILESIA • 1 day

Renaissance Pearl of Upper Silesia

A palace with preserved 19th-century interiors and an English-style park. A town with a postcard market square.

Tarnowskie Góry

SILESIA • 1 day

Underground of Silver and Lead

A historic mine with an underground boat route. The only such trail in Europe.

Cieszyn

SILESIA • 1 day

A Town on the Border

Cieszyn Venice, the Three Brothers Well, and the Friendship Bridge. Coffee on one side of the Olza, dessert on the other.

Silesia

SILESIA • 2 days

Roulade, Dumplings and Modra Kapusta

Silesian cuisine is substance, heartiness, and respect for tradition. Roulade with dumplings is a classic that tastes like home.
