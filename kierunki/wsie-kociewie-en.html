POMERANIA

Kociewie — Kociewie Villages with Embroidery

2 days
Home›Pomerania›Kociewie

Kociewie has no administrative borders but has its own dialect, embroidery, and landscape. Villages scattered across the Tuchola Forest, along the Wda and Wierzyca rivers. Slow travel through Kociewie.

Why it's worth it

Kociewie is one of those Polish regions that doesn't exist on an administrative map but exists in the minds of its inhabitants. It stretches between the Vistula and the Tuchola Forest — from Tczew and Pelplin in the north to Tuchola and Świecie in the south. The Kociewiacy have their own dialect, their own embroidery (three colours: red, blue, and black — rare today), their own dishes, and their own quiet pride. Kociewie villages don't compete with Kashubia for tourists — they have neither sea nor mountains — but that is exactly why they are authentic, calm, and cheap.

Slow travel through Kociewie is a journey through an agricultural-forest landscape: fields, meadows, pine forests, lakes, and rivers. The villages are small, often with 100–300 residents, and — rarely — still have shops, churches, sometimes a school. Stop longer in one village, walk the area, step into a church, eat lunch at a roadside inn. Kociewie offers no spectacular attractions — it offers an everydayness that no longer exists in cities.

What to see

Kociewie villages fall into three groups: villages in the Tuchola Forest, villages in the Wierzyca valley, and villages in the Wda valley. Exploring the region takes 2–3 days.

Villages in the Tuchola Forest

  • Wdecki Młyn — a hamlet on the Wda, with a historic water mill and a 1910 power plant. Starting point for kayak trips.
  • Wirty — a hamlet with an arboretum founded in 1869 by forest inspector Adam Putzer. The oldest arboretum in Poland, ~500 species of trees and shrubs. Free entry.
  • Osie — a village on the Wda, with a wooden church and numerous agritourism farms.

Villages in the Wierzyca valley

  • Pelplin — a small town with one of the largest cathedrals in Poland (13th-century, post-Cistercian). Worth it for architecture and the Gutenberg Bible in the diocesan museum.
  • Stocki Młyn — a village with a historic mill and water power plant, one of the oldest in the region.
  • Sumin — a village with an 18th-century wooden church and an Evangelical cemetery.

Villages in the Wda valley

  • Czarne — a village at the mouth of the Wda, starting point for kayak trips down the Wda.
  • Rytel — a village with an 1880 railway bridge and an old station, amid the Tuchola Forest.
  • Klosnowo — a village on the Wda, with a wooden church and numerous riverside campsites.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists. Worth it for walks in the forest and kayaking on the Wda.
  • July–August — kayaking season. The Tuchola Forest is busy, but the villages stay calm.
  • September–October — second best. Mushroom picking in the forest, autumn colours.
  • November–March — villages are sleepy in winter, many agritourism farms closed. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Gdańsk (A1 + DK22), ~1 hour from Bydgoszcz (S5), ~3.5 hours from Warsaw (S7 + A1).
  • By train: Tczew, Pelplin, Tuchola have railway stations. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS serves most larger villages.

Kociewie is best explored by car or bike — villages are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 villages in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Pelplin, Wirty, Wdecki Młyn.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Pelplin, Wirty, Wdecki Młyn, Osie, Czarne, Rytel.

A week lets you explore all of Kociewie — from Tczew to Tuchola, with numerous stops and kayak trips.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Zupa brzadowa — a Kociewie soup made from dried fruit (pears, plums, apples), served at Christmas and beyond.
  • Kociewie golce — dumplings made from flour and potatoes, served with cabbage or lard.
  • Smoked fish from the Wda — trout and trout smoked in home smokehouses.
  • Country bread — baked in bread ovens, available at inns.
  • Mead — from apiaries in the Tuchola Forest.

Accommodation: Tczew (town, more options), Pelplin (small town, guesthouses), Tuchola (town, more options), Osie and Czarne (village, agritourism), numerous agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — Kociewie's history is less known than Kashubia's, so take a guide in Pelplin.
  • Kayaking — the Wda and Wierzyca are among Poland's most beautiful kayak routes. Rentals in Czarne, Osie, Tleń.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Kociewie dialect — several villages still use the Kociewie dialect. Pay attention to local names.
  • Opening hours — many attractions (arboretum, museums) open only in season.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in the forest. Have an offline map.

What to avoid

  • Don't try to explore Kociewie in one day — the region is larger than it looks on a map.
  • Don't expect restaurants in every village — in many places you need to order food in advance at the agritourism farm.
  • Don't go to the Tuchola Forest on an August weekend without a reservation — campsites and agritourism farms are full.

Trip plan

  1. Villages with Kociewie embroidery around Tczew
  2. Wooden churches of Kociewie
  3. Farmsteads and open-air museums in the region

Tip

Head down to Wdecki Młyn on the Wda — the 1910 mill still works, and you can picnic by the river.
