GREATER POLAND

Poznań — The Great Rhythm of Poznań

2 days
Home›Greater Poland›Poznań

St. Martin's croissants, design shops, and an unhurried stroll through the Jeżyce district. Slow travel through Poznań — a city to know beyond the Old Market Square.

Why Poznań, differently

Poznań is often associated with trade fairs, business and the mechanical goats on the town hall tower. True — but that is only one face of the city. The other face of Poznań is its districts: Jeżyce with craft bakeries, Śródka with murals, Ostrów Tumski with the first cathedral of Poland, Cytadela with a park on a former fort. To feel Poznań, you have to step outside the Old Market Square.

Slow travel through Poznań is a journey through contrasts. Instead of racing from monument to monument, choose one district a day — Śródka, Jeżyce, Łazarz, Wilda — and get to know it deeply. This is a city that rewards the patient and curious. Coffee in a small café in Jeżyce tastes better than on the tourist promenade, and a walk among the murals of Śródka stays in memory longer than the standard tour of the Market Square.

What to see

Poznań splits into several parts: the Old Town (with the Market Square and Ostrów Tumski), Śródka (east, with murals), Jeżyce (west, with cafés), Cytadela (a park on a former fort), Malta (lake, sport and recreation). Two to three days are enough to see it all.

Old Town and Ostrów Tumski

  • Old Market Square — one of the most beautiful market squares in Poland, with the Renaissance Town Hall, where every day at 12:00 the mechanical goats butt heads (mechanism from 1551). Worth seeing, but best early in the morning when the square is almost empty.
  • Poznań Town Hall — a masterpiece of the Renaissance, with a richly decorated facade and an arcaded courtyard. Today the seat of the Museum of the History of Poznań.
  • Ostrów Tumski — the oldest part of the city, on an island between the Warta and Cybina rivers. Poznań Cathedral, where the baptism of Poland probably took place in 966. Worth seeing the Church of Our Lady and the Lubrański Academy.
  • Brama Poznania — a modern heritage interpretation centre on Ostrów Tumski. Multimedia exhibition about the beginnings of Poland. Worth 2–3 hours.
  • Śródka — a district just behind Ostrów Tumski, with Poznań's most famous mural (illustration of the tale of the goats), cafés and the atmosphere of a small town within a city.

Jeżyce, Łazarz, Wilda — districts with soul

  • Jeżyce — the district that has become a symbol of Poznań slow life. Craft bakeries, cafés, independent design shops, early 20th-century townhouses. Worth walking around Jeżyce Market and side streets.
  • Łazarz — the neighbouring district, quieter and less known. Wilson Park, Łazarski Market, 19th-century townhouses. A good place for a walk away from the crowds.
  • Wilda — a working-class district, today with the most interesting food and art scene. Worth visiting small bistros and galleries.
  • Cytadela Park — the largest park in Poznań, on the site of a former Prussian fort. Sculptures by Magdalena Abakanowicz, war cemeteries, walking alleys. Perfect for a morning or afternoon.

Museums and attractions outside the centre

  • National Museum in Poznań — one of the most important art collections in Poland. Polish painting gallery, European collections, temporary exhibitions.
  • Imperial Castle — a monumental building from the partition era, today a cultural centre (including Muza cinema, Polish Theatre).
  • Malta Thermal Baths — a complex of thermal and sports pools on Lake Malta. Perfect for an evening after a day of sightseeing.
  • New Zoo and Malta Ski — for families with children. Chairlift to Malta in summer, ski slope in winter.
  • Rogalowe Museum of Poznań — shows how St. Martin's croissants are baked. You can also try it yourself.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time. The city blooms, fewer crowds, long days. Worth combining with a spring walk through Cytadela.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, warm evenings, fewer tourists.
  • July and August — peak season. Market Square crowded, but the city has summer energy. Malta buzzes with life.
  • November — the season of St. Martin's croissants (11 November). The city celebrates, but prices and queues in pastry shops are high. Worth reserving croissants in advance.
  • December — Christmas market on the Market Square (one of the largest in Poland). Beautiful, but very crowded — especially on weekends.

Getting there

  • By train: Poznań Główny is one of the largest railway stations in Poland, with direct connections from Warsaw (~2.5 hours), Wrocław (~2 hours), Gdańsk (~3.5 hours), Kraków (~4 hours), Berlin (~3 hours).
  • By car: ~3 hours from Warsaw (S8 and A2), ~3 hours from Berlin (A2), ~2 hours from Wrocław (S5 and S11).
  • By bus: Flixbus and other operators serve most Polish and European cities.
  • By plane: Poznań-Ławica airport (POZ), ~7 km from the centre. From the airport to the centre by bus (~30 min).

Within the city, trams and buses are well developed. The centre is compact and most sights are within walking distance, but districts (Jeżyce, Śródka, Wilda) are worth exploring by tram or bike.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Old Market Square, Ostrów Tumski, Śródka and a walk through Jeżyce. Enough for a first impression.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for the Old Town and Ostrów Tumski, one for the districts (Jeżyce, Łazarz, Wilda), one for Cytadela, Malta and museums.

Four to five days lets you add trips outside Poznań — Rogalin (~20 km), Kórnik (~20 km), Puszczykowo (~15 km) and Wielkopolski National Park.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • St. Martin's croissant — Poznań's most recognisable pastry. A croissant with white poppy seeds, dried fruit and almonds, baked only on 11 November (though today available in pastry shops throughout the year).
  • Pyry z gzikiem — potatoes in their jackets with curd cheese, onion and sour cream. A Greater Poland classic.
  • Goose for St. Martin's Day — roast goose with modra kapusta and dumplings. Seasonally, from November.
  • Szneki z glancem — sweet yeast buns with streusel and icing. Popular for breakfast throughout Greater Poland.
  • Potato pancakes — in the Greater Poland version with marjoram and bacon.
  • Specialty coffee — Poznań has an excellent coffee scene, especially in Jeżyce and Wilda (Stragan Kawiarnia, Weranda Cafe, Kofi Brand).

Accommodation: Old Town (most convenient, but expensive and busy), Jeżyce (atmosphere, close to cafés), Wilda (cheaper, artistic), Ostrów Tumski (quiet, but less infrastructure), Malta area (for active travellers). Poznań has some of the best hostels in Poland — worth considering, especially for a weekend.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — Rogalowe Museum of Poznań, Brama Poznania and Malta Thermal Baths require online booking in season.
  • Goats — they butt heads every day at 12:00 on the Town Hall tower. Arrive 5 minutes early to get a good spot.
  • Public transport — time-based tickets (15, 30, 60 min, 24h) from machines and apps. Night trams run 24/7.
  • Bike — Poznań has a well-developed cycling path network and city bike rentals (Poznań City Bike).
  • Crowds — Old Market Square is crowded in season and during markets. Rise early and start sightseeing before the crowds.
  • St. Martin's croissants — buy authentic croissants in certified pastry shops. Avoid cheap imitations.
  • Parking — the centre has a paid parking zone. Large car parks at the station and at Galeria Malta. Leave the car at a P+R and take a tram.

Trip plan

  1. Old Market Square in the morning
  2. Śródka and Cathedral Island (Ostrów Tumski)
  3. Dinner in artistic Jeżyce

Tip

Take a walk through Śródka and Ostrów Tumski. These are the oldest parts of the city, where silence and greenery allow a moment of breath from the urban pace.
