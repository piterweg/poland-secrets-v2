LUBLIN REGION

Janów Forests — Serenity in Eastern Poland

1–2 days
Home›Lublin Region›Janów Forests

Flat trails, uncrowded paths, and an idyllic atmosphere for a quiet woodland stay. Slow travel through the Janów Forests — one of the largest forest complexes in Poland.

Why the Janów Forests, differently

The Janów Forests are one of the largest forest complexes in Poland — they stretch over 1,000 km², between the Vistula and San rivers, on the border of the Lublin region and Subcarpathia. This is a land of gentle landscapes, where the forest does not overwhelm but invites you to slowly discover its secrets. It is a place for those who seek peace and lack of haste in nature above all.

Slow travel through the Janów Forests is a celebration of simplicity. Morning forest trails are a ritual of cleansing. Walking through dew while the sun breaks through the leaves, you can feel tension disappearing and the mind filling with clarity and peace. It is an ideal place to escape the noise of the city. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: animal tracks, hidden ponds, rare plants.

What to see

The Janów Forests split into several parts: Janów Forests Reserve (centre), around Janów Lubelski (east), around Biłgoraj (south), around Stalowa Wola (west). Two to three days are best to visit.

Janów Forests Reserve

  • Janów Forests Reserve — a reserve protecting the most valuable fragments of the Janów Forests, with pine forests and peat bogs. Entry only on marked trails.
  • Nature trail in the reserve — an educational trail, leading through the most interesting fragments of the reserve. Good for families with children.
  • Observation tower in the reserve — a tower from which you can watch birds and animals. Come at dawn.
  • Ecological Education Centre in Janów Lubelski — worth it for understanding the nature of the region.

Hiking and cycling trails

  • Hiking trail through the Janów Forests — several hiking trails, leading through the most interesting fragments of the forests. Worth a walk.
  • Cycling routes — several cycling routes, leading through the forests and surroundings. Good for those who want to see more in a short time.
  • San and Vistula kayak trails — kayak trails on the San and Vistula rivers, leading through the Janów Forests. Worth it for those who want to see the forests from the water.
  • Horse riding trails — several horse trails in the Janów Forests. Worth it for equestrian enthusiasts.

Janów Lubelski and surroundings

  • Janów Lubelski — a small town in the centre of the Janów Forests, with a market square and accommodation base. Worth a walk and shopping.
  • Biłgoraj — a town in the south of the Janów Forests, with a historic market square and church. Worth it for architecture.
  • Stalowa Wola — an industrial town in the west of the region, with museums and accommodation base.
  • Zamość — the ideal Renaissance town, ~60 km from the Janów Forests. Worth a day trip.
  • Roztocze — a region in the southeast, ~80 km from the Janów Forests. Worth it for contrast.

Lesser-known corners

  • Solska Forest Reserve — a reserve in the southern part of the Janów Forests, with pine forests. Worth a walk.
  • Villages of Momoty Górne and Dolne — villages deep in the Janów Forests, with wooden architecture. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Ponds around Janów — several smaller ponds, less known than those in the Barycz Valley. Worth it for birdwatching.
  • In the Footsteps of Partisans Trail — a historical trail, leading through places associated with partisan activity in the Janów Forests. Worth it for history.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Forests bloom, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk.
  • July and August — peak season. Forests full of mushroom pickers, but still fewer tourists than in other regions. Worth it for walks.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, mushrooms in the forests, fewer people. September is a good time for a walk.
  • November–March — winter in the Janów Forests is quiet and harsh. Worth it for those who seek solitude.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Lublin (DK19), ~1.5 hours from Rzeszów (DK19 and DK74), ~3.5 hours from Warsaw (S17 and DK19).
  • By train: station in Janów Lubelski, with connections from Lublin (~1.5 hours), Rzeszów (~2.5 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Lublin, Rzeszów, Stalowa Wola.

Public transport in the region is sparse — a car or bike makes moving between attractions much easier.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one day for a walk in the forest, one for cycling or kayaking.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for a walk, one for cycling, one for Janów Lubelski and surroundings.

A week lets you explore the whole region — Janów Forests, Roztocze, Zamość, Biłgoraj and surroundings.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Lublin cuisine — Lublin cebularz, pierogi with bryndza, krupnik. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Fish from the San and Vistula — pike, bream, pike-perch. Smoked or fried, available at local fish shops.
  • Mushrooms — in season (August–October) local restaurants offer dishes with mushrooms collected in the forests.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Janów Lubelski has several good cafés.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Janów Lubelski (largest choice, centre of the Forests), Biłgoraj (town, more options), Stalowa Wola (town, more options), villages in the Forests (agritourism, silence), Zamość (~60 km, more options). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — book campsites in the forests in advance in summer season.
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — forests are paradise for mosquitoes and ticks. Repellent and mosquito net — essential.
  • Bike — cycling trails in the Janów Forests are well-marked. Rent a bike and explore the area.
  • Mushrooms — the Janów Forests are a paradise for mushroom pickers. In season, bring a basket and a knife.
  • Signal — good in Janów Lubelski, weaker deep in the forests. Have an offline map.
  • Car parks — paid car parks at reserves and trails. They fill quickly in season.

Trip plan

  1. Early morning forest trails
  2. Picnic among old trees and ponds
  3. Unhurried return in the late afternoon

Tip

Visit local agritourism farms in the vicinity of the forests. There you will find the most authentic hospitality and feel the true spirit of eastern Poland.
