Kashubia — Kashubian Villages with Chëcze

3 days
Home›Pomerania›Kashubia

Thatched wooden cottages, Kashubian embroidery, and bilingual village signs. Villages where Kashubian is still spoken. Slow travel through Kashubian villages.

Why Kashubian villages, differently

Kashubian villages are one of the most characteristic elements of the Pomeranian landscape. Wooden thatched cottages — called chëcze — are rare today, but in some villages you can still see them. Kashubian villages also have a unique naming system — bilingual signs (Polish-Kashubian) stand at the entrances to many towns. This is a sign that Kashubian is still a living language, not just a language of folklore.

Slow travel through Kashubian villages is a journey through culture and language. Instead of rushing from village to village, stop in one longer — talk to residents (even if they speak Kashubian, they are often happy to talk about their culture), enter a cottage, see Kashubian embroidery, listen to how the village lives. Each village has its own history, its own cottages, its own inhabitants. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly understand about Kashubian culture.

What to see

Kashubian villages split into several groups: villages with chëcze (Wdzydze Kiszewskie, Chmielno), villages with Kashubian embroidery (Żukowo, Kartuzy), villages with bilingual signs (whole region), villages in the Kashubian Switzerland (around Kartuzy). Exploring the region takes 2–3 days.

Villages with chëcze

  • Wdzydze Kiszewskie — a village with the Kashubian Ethnographic Park, one of the oldest open-air museums in Poland. Here you can see traditional chëcze, craft workshops, and agricultural tools.
  • Chmielno — a village on a lake, with a Kashubian pottery tradition. Ceramics workshops in Chmielno and surroundings.
  • Sianowo village — a village with a wooden church and Marian sanctuary, one of the more important pilgrimage sites in Kashubia.
  • Wygoda Łączyńska village — a village with thatched chëcze, one of the best-preserved in the region.

Villages with Kashubian embroidery

  • Kashubian embroidery — colorful patterns (seven characteristic colors, each with its own meaning) decorate today not only folk costumes but also everyday objects. Workshops in Żukowo, Kartuzy and other towns.
  • Żukowo — a small town with a Kashubian embroidery tradition. There are several workshops where you can see the embroidery process.
  • Kartuzy — a small town with a Kashubian embroidery tradition and a regional museum. Worth it for context.
  • Villages with embroidery — several villages in the region have preserved the Kashubian embroidery tradition. Pay attention to cottages with embroidered elements.

Kashubian Switzerland and surroundings

  • Kashubian Switzerland — a region around Kartuzy, named for its hilly landscape. Villages scattered across the hills, with wooden architecture and numerous lakes.
  • Ostrzyce village — a village in Kashubian Switzerland, with a view over Lake Ostrzyckie. Worth it for views.
  • Wieżyca village — a village with the highest hill in Kashubia (329 m), with a viewing tower. Worth it for views.
  • Stężyca village — a village in Kashubian Switzerland, with wooden architecture and lakes. Worth it for the atmosphere.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk around the villages.
  • July–September — season for craft festivals and fairs. Worth it for cultural events.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people. September is a good time for mushroom picking in Kashubian forests.
  • November–March — villages in winter are less accessible (some workshops closed). Worth it for those who want to see the villages without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~30 minutes from Gdańsk (DK20), ~40 minutes from Gdynia, ~4 hours from Warsaw (S7).
  • By train: Kartuzy, Kościerzyna, Żukowo have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most villages.

Kashubian villages are best explored by car or bike — they are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 villages in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Wdzydze Kiszewskie, Chmielno, Kartuzy.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Wdzydze Kiszewskie, Chmielno, Kartuzy, Żukowo, Kashubian Switzerland.

A week lets you explore all Kashubian villages — from Gdańsk to Kościerzyna, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Herring in oil — a Kashubian classic, available at fish shops.
  • Smoked eel — a delicacy from Kashubian lakes, available at smokehouses.
  • Kashubian broth — chicken broth with dried mushrooms, available in local restaurants.
  • Golce — the Kashubian version of dumplings, available at village inns.
  • Szneki z glancem — sweet yeast buns, available at bakeries in Kartuzy.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region (Browar PG4, Browar Puck).

Accommodation: Kartuzy (town, more options), Kościerzyna (town, more options), Żukowo (small town, smaller choice), Wdzydze Kiszewskie (village, agritourism farms), Chmielno (village, agritourism farms), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the history of Kashubian villages is complex, and without context many sites look similar. Take a guide at selected places.
  • Craft workshops — many workshops offer courses (Kashubian embroidery, pottery, cutouts). Book ahead.
  • Shopping — buy directly from craftsmen. It is not only a souvenir but also support for vanishing trades.
  • Kashubian language — several villages in the region have bilingual signs. Pay attention to Kashubian names.
  • Opening hours — many workshops are open only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Open-air museum in Wdzydze Kiszewskie
  2. Villages with bilingual signs
  3. Kashubian embroidery in Żukowo

Tip

Visit the open-air museum in Wdzydze Kiszewskie — the largest ethnographic museum in Pomerania and the best place to see a traditional Kashubian village.
