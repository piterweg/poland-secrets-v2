Pszczyna — Renaissance Pearl of Upper Silesia

1 day
Home›Silesia›Pszczyna

A palace with preserved 19th-century interiors and an English-style park. A town with a postcard market square. Slow travel through Pszczyna — one of the most beautiful towns of Upper Silesia.

Why Pszczyna, differently

Pszczyna is a town where the history of Silesia meets the elegance of European aristocracy. The palace, which for centuries belonged to the Hochberg family, delights with interiors preserved in the style of the turn of the 19th and 20th centuries. Each room is a separate story about the daily life of one of the wealthiest families in the region. This is one of the few places in Poland where you can see how the aristocracy really lived.

Slow travel through Pszczyna is a celebration of details. Instead of rushing through the palace in an hour, choose a few rooms and give them full attention. The palace and park form a coherent whole — combine touring the interiors with a walk in the park, where old oaks remember the Hochberg times. Pszczyna is a land that rewards the patient — the longer you are here, the more details you notice.

What to see

Pszczyna is compact — the main sights are within walking distance. One day is enough to see it, but it is worth staying longer to feel the atmosphere.

Pszczyna Palace

  • Pszczyna Palace — a Renaissance palace, rebuilt in the 19th century in Neo-Baroque style. The interiors delight with original furnishings, furniture, paintings and everyday objects. Guided tours take ~2 hours.
  • Hochberg Apartments — the palace's ceremonial halls, where guests were received and the most important decisions were made. Note the salon, library, dining room.
  • Palace Chapel — a Neo-Gothic chapel from the 19th century, where palace residents prayed. Worth visiting for architecture and atmosphere.
  • Treasury — a collection of objects connected with the history of the Hochberg family. Worth it for understanding how an aristocratic estate functioned.

Pszczyna Park and Bison Show Enclosure

  • Pszczyna Park — one of the most beautiful parks in the region, surrounding the palace. Extensive alleys, old trees, ponds — all this creates a space ideal for a walk. Especially beautiful in autumn, when leaves change colour to gold and red.
  • Bison Show Enclosure — a demonstration reserve where bison, fallow deer, mouflons and other animals live. Paid entry, but worth it — especially for families with children.
  • Wild Promenade — a path in the park leading through the wildest fragments. Worth a walk and contact with nature.

Market square and town monuments

  • Market square in Pszczyna — the heart of the town, surrounded by townhouses from different eras. In the centre, the town hall with a clock. Look at the townhouses — each has a different story.
  • Evangelical Church — a Neo-Gothic church from the 19th century, one of the most important monuments of the town. Worth seeing from the outside and visiting the interior.
  • All Saints' Church — a Gothic church from the 14th century, one of the oldest monuments of Pszczyna. The interior delights with vaults and altars.
  • Town Hall — a Classicist building from the 19th century, in the centre of the market square. Worth seeing from the outside.
  • Museum of the Silesian Press — one of the few museums in Poland dedicated to the history of the press. Worth it for those interested.

Surroundings (day trips)

  • Moszna Castle — a Neo-Baroque castle with 99 towers, ~60 km from Pszczyna. Worth it for architecture and atmosphere.
  • Historic Silver Mine in Tarnowskie Góry — one of the most interesting attractions of the region, ~60 km from Pszczyna.
  • Oświęcim and Auschwitz-Birkenau Museum — ~30 km from Pszczyna. A difficult but essential visit for understanding the region's history.
  • Kraków — ~70 km from Pszczyna. Worth a day trip.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time. The town blooms, the park is green, fewer tourists. Worth a walk in the park.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours in the park, warm days, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. Palace and park crowded, but all attractions open. Come on a weekday.
  • November–March — the town empties out, but is wintry. The palace is open, the park looks fairy-tale under snow. Worth it for those who want to see Pszczyna without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~40 minutes from Katowice (A4), ~1 hour from Kraków (A4), ~2 hours from Wrocław (A4).
  • By train: station in Pszczyna, with direct connections from Katowice (~30 minutes), Kraków (~1.5 hours), Wrocław (~2.5 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Katowice, Kraków and other cities.

Within the town, the centre is compact and most sights are within walking distance. But to see the surroundings (Moszna, Tarnowskie Góry), a car is useful.

How much time you need

A weekend (1 day) is the minimum — palace, park and a walk around the market square.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for the town itself, one for the park and Bison Show Enclosure, one for a trip (Moszna or Tarnowskie Góry).

A week lets you explore the whole region — Pszczyna, Moszna, Tarnowskie Góry, Oświęcim and Kraków.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Silesian roulade — beef rolled with bacon, onion and cucumber, with Silesian dumplings and modra kapusta. A Silesian cuisine classic.
  • Silesian dumplings — round potato dumplings with a hole, served with sauce and meat.
  • Duck with apples — a Silesian classic, available in elegant restaurants.
  • Silesian kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Worth it for dessert.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — the centre of Pszczyna has several good cafés, ideal for an afternoon break.

Accommodation: Pszczyna centre (most convenient, but expensive), around the park (quiet, close to nature), surrounding towns (cheaper, but further), Katowice (~40 km, more options). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — tickets for the palace and the Bison Show Enclosure are worth booking in advance, especially in season.
  • Guided tours — the palace's history is complex, and without context many rooms look similar. Worth going with a guide.
  • Pszczyna Park — walking in the park is free and available all year. Bring comfortable shoes, especially in spring and autumn, when alleys can be wet.
  • Crowds — the palace and park are crowded in season (May–September). Rise early and start sightseeing before the crowds.
  • Parking — central Pszczyna has a paid parking zone. Large car parks at the park, then a 5–10 min walk.
  • Signal — good throughout the centre and in the park.

Trip plan

  1. Guided palace tour
  2. Pszczyna Park and European Bison Show Farm
  3. Walk around the market square

Tip

Start your day by touring the palace, then move on to the park and the Bison Show Enclosure. This way you avoid the largest crowds and use the best daylight for a walk.
