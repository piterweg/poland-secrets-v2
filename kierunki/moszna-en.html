Moszna — A Fairytale Castle with 99 Towers

1 day
Home›Opole Region›Moszna

A neo-Baroque residence surrounded by an English park. Perfect for a lazy afternoon among old oaks. Slow travel through Moszna Castle.

Why Moszna, differently

Moszna is a castle that looks like it came straight out of a fairy tale. A neo-Baroque residence with 99 towers, surrounded by an extensive English park, delights with harmony and architectural diversity. Each tower has a different shape, each detail — a different story. It is one of the most recognisable castles in Poland, though still less known than Malbork or Książ.

Slow travel through Moszna is a celebration of details. Touring the castle chambers is a journey through several eras — you can see both historic interiors and contemporary exhibitions. But the park itself leaves the greatest impression — old oaks, ponds, bridges and hidden alleys that invite a walk without haste. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: park sculptures, interesting tree specimens, hidden corners. Moszna is a place for those who want to rest from the bustle and feel like they are in a fairy tale.

What to see

Moszna is not just the castle itself — it is a whole complex of attractions: chambers, park, ponds, chapel, restaurant. Half a day or a full day is enough to see it.

Castle and chambers

  • Moszna Castle — a neo-Baroque residence from the 18th century, rebuilt in the 20th century. Known for its 99 towers and 365 rooms. A guided tour takes ~1.5 hours.
  • Ballroom — the castle's ceremonial hall, with richly decorated vault and chandeliers. Worth it for architecture.
  • Castle chapel — a Neo-Gothic chapel from the 19th century, with stained glass and rich decoration. Worth it for architecture.
  • Library — the former castle library, today an exhibition hall. Worth it for book lovers.
  • Ceremonial chambers — halls where guests were received. Worth it for interiors.

Park and ponds

  • Castle Park — an English-style park around the castle, ~200 ha. Alleys, old oaks, ponds, bridges — all this creates a space perfect for a walk. Worth a walk at any time of day.
  • Ponds — several ponds in the park, with swans and ducks. Worth a walk and contact with nature.
  • Bridges and footbridges — bridges and footbridges over the ponds, with a view over the castle. Worth it for photography.
  • Avenue of old oaks — an avenue of old oaks in the park, some over 300 years old. Worth a walk.
  • Park sculptures — sculptures and monuments in the park, scattered in various corners. Worth it for details.

Surroundings

  • Prószków — a small town with a palace and park, ~20 km from Moszna. Worth it for architecture.
  • Opole — the regional capital, ~30 km from Moszna. Worth it for accommodation base and culture.
  • St. Anne's Mountain — a hill with a sanctuary and geological reserve, ~30 km from Moszna. Worth it for views.
  • Krapkowice — a small town on the Oder, ~15 km from Moszna. Worth a walk.
  • Moszna Castle — hotel — the castle operates a hotel and restaurant. Worth it for accommodation in a unique place.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time. The park blooms, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk in the park.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours in the park, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. Castle crowded, car parks full. If you must — pick a weekday and come in the morning.
  • November–March — the castle in winter is less crowded, but the park is grey. Worth it for those who want to see the castle without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1.5 hours from Wrocław (A4), ~30 minutes from Opole (DK45), ~1.5 hours from Katowice (A4). Car park at the castle (paid, in season full from 9 a.m.).
  • By train: station in Prószków or Opole, with connections from Wrocław (~2 hours), Katowice (~2 hours). Then bus or taxi.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Opole and Wrocław.

In Moszna itself there is not much — the castle and park are the main attractions. A car is useful to explore the surroundings (Opole, St. Anne's Mountain).

How much time you need

Half a day (3–4 hours) is the minimum for a visit to the castle and a walk in the park.

A full day lets you add a walk through the whole park and lunch at the castle restaurant.

A weekend (2 days) lets you combine Moszna with Opole and St. Anne's Mountain.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Silesian cuisine — roulade, Silesian dumplings, modra kapusta. Worth trying at the castle restaurant or local inns.
  • Duck with apples — a Silesian classic, available at the castle restaurant.
  • Silesian kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Worth it for dessert.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Opole has several good cafés.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Moszna Castle (hotel in the castle, expensive but atmospheric), Opole (town, more options), Prószków (small town, smaller choice), Krapkowice (small town, smaller choice). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — tickets for the castle are worth booking in advance, especially in season. The castle hotel must be booked well ahead.
  • Guided tours — the castle's history is interesting, and without context many chambers look similar. Worth going with a guide.
  • Park — walking in the park is free and available all year. Wear comfortable shoes.
  • Castle restaurant — the castle has a restaurant where you can have lunch. Book a table in season.
  • Crowds — the castle is crowded in season (May–September). Come in the morning.
  • Parking — the car park at the castle is paid. In season it fills from 9 a.m.
  • Signal — good throughout the castle complex and the area.

Trip plan

  1. Chambers and chapel tour
  2. Walk through the English park
  3. Afternoon at the castle café

Tip

Plan your visit to Moszna on a weekday. On weekends the park can be crowded, but on weekdays you can truly feel its peace and harmony.
