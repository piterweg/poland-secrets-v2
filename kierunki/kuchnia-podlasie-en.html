PODLASIE

Podlasie — Kibiny, Kartacze and Potato Babka

3–4 days
Home›Podlasie›Podlasie

Borderland cuisine — Tatar, Orthodox, and Polish in one. Hearty, simple, and deeply rooted in the land. Slow travel through Podlasie cuisine.

Why Podlasie cuisine, differently

Podlasie is a region where cuisine is a record of history. Poles, Tatars, Russians, Lithuanians, and Jews met here for centuries — and each of these cultures brought something to the local cuisine. Kibiny, Tatar meat pastries, are now one of the most recognizable dishes of the region. Kartacze — large potato dumplings stuffed with meat — is a dish that appears at every wedding and every holiday.

Slow travel through Podlasie cuisine is a celebration of taste and history. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a milk bar, in a village inn, in an agritourism farm. Each dish has its own story, its own cook, its own region. Ask where the ingredients come from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Podlasie cuisine splits into several categories: Tatar dishes (kibiny, pierogi), rural dishes (kartacze, potato babka, potato sausage), Orthodox dishes (pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms, kutia), Jewish dishes (gefilte fish, cholent). Exploring the culinary region of Podlasie takes 2–3 days.

Tatar dishes

  • Kibiny — Tatar meat pastries, baked or fried. A classic of Podlasie cuisine that has survived for centuries. The dough is thin, the filling is meat with onion, seasoned with pepper. Served warm, sometimes with broth.
  • Tatar pierogi — pierogi with meat filling, seasoned with herbs. Similar to kibiny, but in pierogi form.
  • Pelmeni — Tatar dumplings with meat, similar to Russian pelmeni. Served with broth or sour cream.

Rural dishes

  • Kartacze — large potato dumplings stuffed with meat. Hearty, substantial, rural. Served with cracklings or sour cream. In Podlasie, they appear at every wedding and every major holiday.
  • Potato babka — a baked dish of grated potatoes, bacon, onion, and eggs. Served for dinner with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Potato sausage (kiszka ziemniaczana) — sausage made of potato mass with bacon, baked in the oven or on a sheet. Served with onion and mustard.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a traditional dish on every Podlasie table. Podlasie is famous for pierogi with mushroom filling — mushrooms gathered in the Białowieża Forest are the best in Poland.
  • Sękacz — yeast cake baked on a rotating spit, layer by layer. Sękacz is a Podlasie dessert, baked for holidays and celebrations.

Orthodox and Jewish dishes

  • Kutia — a traditional Christmas Eve dish of wheat, poppy seeds, honey, and dried fruit. Served at Orthodox Christmas Eve.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a traditional Lenten dish, served at Christmas Eve. Podlasie is famous for pierogi with mushroom filling.
  • Gefilte fish — a Jewish fish dish, served cold with horseradish. Worth trying in restaurants in Białystok.
  • Cholent — a Jewish Sabbath dish of meat, beans, and groats. Served hot with bread.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal produce. Worth a walk through markets.
  • July–September — season for fresh vegetables, fruit, mushrooms. Worth it for markets and culinary festivals.
  • September–October — second best. Mushrooms, pumpkins, plums. September is a good time for mushroom picking in the Białowieża Forest.
  • November–March — season for bigos, kwaśnica, kutia. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Podlasie cuisine is from the eastern region of Poland — best to travel by car or train, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Białystok, Łomża, Suwałki, Augustów have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~2.5 hours from Warsaw (S8), ~4 hours from Gdańsk (S7 and DK8), ~3 hours from Lublin (DK19).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Białystok (milk bars, kibiny, kartacze) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Białystok, Supraśl, Tykocin, Białowieża Forest.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of Podlasie — all the most important dishes and places.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose milk bars and village inns. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Kibiny — Tatar meat pastries, available at milk bars in Białystok.
  • Kartacze — potato dumplings with meat filling, available at village inns.
  • Potato babka — a baked dish of grated potatoes, available at agritourism farms.
  • Potato sausage (kiszka ziemniaczana) — sausage made of potato mass, available in local shops.
  • Sękacz — yeast cake, available at pastry shops in Białystok.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region (Browar Baba Jaga, Browar Waszczukowe).

Accommodation: Białystok (largest choice, more options), Supraśl (monastery, quiet), Tykocin (small town, smaller choice), Augustów (resort, more options), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Milk bars — usually open from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., often closed on weekends. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants in city centres.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (baking bread, making pierogi, smoking fish). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Podlasie cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Markets — the best place to buy local products (cheeses, honey, cold cuts, mushrooms).
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Kibiny at a milk bar in Białystok
  2. Kartacze in a village inn
  3. Potato babka in Supraśl

Tip

Order kibiny at a milk bar in Białystok — the most authentic place to try the Tatar specialty in its everyday, not holiday, version.
