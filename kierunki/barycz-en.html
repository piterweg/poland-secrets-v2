LOWER SILESIA

Barycz Valley — Birdwatching Weekend

2 days
Home›Lower Silesia›Barycz Valley

Milicz Ponds, cycling trails, and lively water bird mornings on Europe's largest carp ponds. Slow travel through the Barycz Valley.

Why the Barycz Valley, differently

The Barycz Valley is one of the most extraordinary places in Poland — an area where water and birds create an extraordinary symphony. It is a land where humans learned to live in harmony with nature, creating an enormous system of ponds that has become a paradise for birds from all over Europe. The Milicz Ponds are the largest carp pond complex in Europe — over 100 ponds with a total area of ~77 km².

Slow travel through the Barycz Valley is a celebration of details. Instead of rushing from attraction to attraction, sit on an observation tower, look at the ponds, listen to the call of cranes. As you cycle along the pond banks and herons and cormorants circle overhead, you can feel stress disappearing with every turn of the pedals and the mind filling with clarity. This is a land that rewards those who slow down.

What to see

The Barycz Valley splits into several parts: Milicz Ponds (centre), Milicz Ponds Reserve (with observation towers), around Milicz and Żmigród (north), around Twardogóra (east). Two to three days are best to visit.

Milicz Ponds and reserve

  • Milicz Ponds Reserve — the largest ornithological reserve in Poland, covering a complex of carp ponds. In spring and autumn, the ponds become a resting place for thousands of migrating birds — cranes, geese, ducks, wading birds.
  • Observation towers — the reserve has several observation towers from which you can watch birds. Come at dawn when birds are most active.
  • Nature trail Milicz Ponds — an educational trail, leading through the most valuable fragments of the reserve. Good for families with children.
  • Carp Breeding Centre in Milicz — worth it for understanding how carp farming works in the Barycz Valley.

By bike through the Barycz Valley

  • Barycz Valley Cycling Loop — a cycling route around the Barycz Valley, ~150 km long. Leads through ponds, villages, forests and meadows. Worth a multi-day cycling trip.
  • Barycz Valley Trail — a cycling trail leading through the most interesting fragments of the valley. Good for those who want to see the most in a short time.
  • Routes around Milicz — several shorter cycling routes around Milicz, with views over the ponds.
  • Barycz kayak trail — a kayak trail on the Barycz river, leading through ponds and forests. Worth it for those who want to see the valley from the water.

Milicz, Żmigród and surroundings

  • Milicz — a small town in the centre of the Barycz Valley, with a market square, church and accommodation base. Worth a walk and shopping.
  • Żmigród — a small town in the north of the valley, with a castle and park. Worth it for architecture and atmosphere.
  • Krośnice — a village with a palace and park, ~10 km from Milicz. Worth it for architecture.
  • Twardogóra — a small town in the east of the valley, with a church and market square.
  • Joanna's Hill Reserve — a steppe reserve east of Milicz, with unique vegetation. Worth a walk.

Lesser-known corners

  • Olszyny Niezgodzkie Reserve — a forest reserve in the Barycz valley, with rare tree species. Worth a walk.
  • Village of Niezgodzkie — a village with a wooden church, in the middle of the Barycz Valley.
  • Radziądz and Jankowa Ponds — smaller ponds, less known than the Milicz Ponds. Worth it for less crowded places.
  • Milicz Forest District — worth it for those who want to learn about the region's forest management.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time for ornithologists. Birds in full migration, ponds full of life. Come with binoculars.
  • July and August — peak season. Ponds full of tourists, but still fewer than by the sea. Worth it for walks and cycling.
  • September and October — second ornithological peak. Autumn bird migrations, autumn colours on the ponds. Worth it for birdwatchers.
  • November–March — winter on the ponds can be harsh, but quiet. Worth it for those who seek solitude.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Wrocław (DK15), ~2 hours from Poznań (S5 and DK15), ~2.5 hours from Łódź.
  • By train: station in Milicz, with connections from Wrocław (~1.5 hours), Poznań (~2.5 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Wrocław, Poznań, Ostrów Wielkopolski.

Public transport in the region is sparse — a car or bike makes moving between ponds and villages much easier.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one day for a walk around the ponds and observation towers, one for cycling around the valley.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for the ponds, one for cycling, one for Milicz and surroundings.

A week lets you explore the entire Barycz Valley — all ponds, villages, reserves, cycling and kayak trails.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Carp from the Barycz Valley — fresh or smoked, available in local fish shops and restaurants.
  • Lower Silesian cuisine — duck with apples, Silesian dumplings, kwaśnica. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Freshwater fish — pike, bream, tench. Smoked or fried, available at fish shops by the ponds.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Milicz has several good cafés.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Milicz (largest choice, valley centre), Żmigród (small town, more options), villages in the valley (agritourism, silence), around Wrocław (~50 km, more options). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Binoculars — without binoculars you will not see most birds. Rent or buy before your trip.
  • Bookings — observation towers and viewpoints may be limited in number. Book in advance.
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — ponds and forests are paradise for mosquitoes and ticks. Repellent and mosquito net — essential.
  • Bike — cycling trails around the Barycz Valley are well-marked. Rent a bike and explore the valley.
  • Signal — good in Milicz and Żmigród, weaker deep in the valley.
  • Car parks — paid car parks at ponds and towers. They fill quickly in season.

Trip plan

  1. Milicz Ponds Nature Reserve
  2. Barycz Valley cycle loop
  3. Wildlife observation tower

Tip

Take the Barycz Valley cycling loop in early spring, when nature wakes up and the ponds fill with migrating birds. It is the time of greatest energy and wonder at nature.
