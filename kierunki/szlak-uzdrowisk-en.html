ALL OF POLAND

Polish Spa Trail — Waters, Air, and Peace

7–14 days
Home›Themed journeys›Polish Spa Trail

A route through the oldest Polish spas — from Krynica to Kudowa, from Szczawnica to Nałęczów. Pump rooms, spa parks, and walks at a slow pace. Slow travel along the Polish Spa Trail.

Why the Polish Spa Trail, differently

Poland has several dozen spas — places where mineral waters, air, and peace have been healing for centuries. Many have preserved their old character — wooden guesthouses, spa parks, pump rooms, promenades. All this makes spas the perfect place for slow travel. These are places where time flows differently — slower, calmer, more reflective.

Slow travel along the Spa Trail is a celebration of calm. Instead of rushing from attraction to attraction, sit in the pump room, drink mineral water, listen to a concert. Walk through the spa park, read a book, talk to other patients. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly rest. Spas are places where not only the body but also the soul is healed.

What to see

The Spa Trail splits into several sections: Lesser Poland (Krynica-Zdrój, Szczawnica, Piwniczna), Lower Silesia (Kudowa-Zdrój, Lądek-Zdrój, Polanica-Zdrój, Duszniki-Zdrój, Szczawno-Zdrój), Lublin region (Nałęczów), Holy Cross (Busko-Zdrój, Solec-Zdrój), Pomerania (Ustka, Sopot), Subcarpathia (Iwonicz-Zdrój, Rymanów-Zdrój). Riding the whole route takes 7–14 days.

Lesser Poland

  • Krynica-Zdrój — the largest spa in Lesser Poland, with a promenade, pump room and Parkowa Hill with a funicular. Worth it for the atmosphere and walks.
  • Szczawnica — a spa at the foot of the Pieniny, with wooden architecture, parks and Dunajec rafting. Worth it for walks and contact with nature.
  • Piwniczna-Zdrój — a smaller spa in the Beskid Sądecki, with mineral waters and peace. Worth it for silence.
  • Wysowa-Zdrój — a spa in the Low Beskids, with mineral waters and wooden architecture. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Muszyna-Zdrój — a spa in the Beskid Sądecki, with sensory gardens and mineral waters. Worth it for walks.

Lower Silesia

  • Kudowa-Zdrój — a spa at the foot of the Table Mountains, with a Spa Park, pump room and numerous guesthouses. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Lądek-Zdrój — one of the oldest spas in Poland, with thermal waters and a spa park. Worth it for history and relaxation.
  • Polanica-Zdrój — a spa with a spa park and pump room. Worth it for walks.
  • Duszniki-Zdrój — a spa with traditions, with a pump room and the Museum of Papermaking. Worth it for culture.
  • Szczawno-Zdrój — a spa near Wałbrzych, with a spa park and pump room. Worth it for walks.

Other regions

  • Nałęczów — a spa in the Lublin region, with a spa park, pump room and promenade. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Busko-Zdrój — a spa in the Holy Cross region, with sulphide waters and a spa park. Worth it for relaxation.
  • Iwonicz-Zdrój — a spa in the Low Beskids, with mineral waters and wooden architecture. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Ustka — a spa on the Baltic, with mineral waters and a maritime climate. Worth it for walks.
  • Sopot — a resort on the Baltic, with mineral waters and a maritime climate. Worth it for walks.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer patients, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk in the parks.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, lower prices.
  • July–August — peak season. Spas crowded, prices high, but all attractions open. Worth it for concerts and cultural events.
  • November–March — winter in spas is quiet and peaceful. Worth it for those who seek solitude.

Getting there

The Spa Trail runs through the whole country — you can start anywhere. Best to travel by car or train, connecting several spas in one trip.

  • By train: main spas (Krynica, Szczawnica, Kudowa-Zdrój, Nałęczów, Busko-Zdrój) have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: the trail runs through main national roads. Plan the route to connect several spas.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most spas.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one spa (e.g. Krynica-Zdrój, Kudowa-Zdrój or Nałęczów).

A week is realistic for a first visit. You can see 3–4 spas in one region (e.g. Lower Silesia or Lesser Poland).

Two weeks is time for the whole trail — all Polish spas. This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

Three weeks — if you want to travel truly slow, with numerous treatments, walks and rest.

What to eat and where to sleep

Spas are famous for local dishes and regional products — try them in guesthouses and restaurants.

  • Regional cuisine — in each spa you can try local dishes: oscypek (Krynica, Szczawnica), duck with apples (Kudowa-Zdrój), pierogi (Nałęczów).
  • Mineral waters — in every pump room you can drink mineral water from local springs. Try different waters — each has a different taste.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in spa regions.
  • Coffee and cake — every spa has several good cafés where you can spend an afternoon.
  • Local products — cheeses, honey, cold cuts, available in regional shops.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in spas (various price categories), sanatoria (for those who want to combine rest with treatments), agritourism farms in surrounding villages (quiet, cheaper), apartments (for those who want to cook themselves). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Treatments — spas offer various treatments (mineral baths, inhalations, massages). Check the offer before your trip.
  • Pump rooms — usually open from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Drink mineral water on an empty stomach, as recommended by a doctor.
  • Concerts — many spas hold concerts in pump rooms and spa parks. Check the schedule.
  • Bookings — in summer season (July–August) guesthouses and sanatoria must be booked in advance.
  • Weather — Polish weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Walks — spa parks and promenades are ideal for walks. Wear comfortable shoes.
  • Signal — good in spas, weaker in surrounding villages.

Trip plan

  1. Krynica-Zdrój and Szczawnica
  2. Kudowa-Zdrój and Lądek-Zdrój
  3. Nałęczów and Busko-Zdrój

Tip

Plan the route for spring or autumn — that is when spas are calmer, prices lower, and the air crisp. Summer is peak season, when crowds are larger.
