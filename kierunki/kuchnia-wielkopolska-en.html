Greater Poland — St. Martin's Croissants and Pyry z Gzikiem

2 days
Home›Greater Poland›Greater Poland

Greater Poland cuisine means pyry z gzikiem, duck with apples, and St Martin's croissants. Slow travel through Greater Poland through your stomach.

Why it's worth it

Greater Poland cuisine is one of the most distinctive regional cuisines in Poland — hearty, potato-based, built on local produce. It was shaped in a region dominated by farms and livestock — hence the abundance of potatoes (called “pyry” here), cabbage, meat, and dairy. Greater Poland cuisine also has its legends: St Martin's croissants, which are on the EU protected products list, and pyry z gzikiem — potatoes with curd cheese, a regional classic. This is a cuisine that is not refined, but honest and filling.

Slow travel through Greater Poland through cuisine is a journey through flavours that are hard to find elsewhere. The region has restaurants, inns, milk bars, and market stalls — everything that lets you explore Greater Poland cuisine in different settings. It is worth spending 2–3 days here to truly feel this flavour. The best way to explore Greater Poland is by car — restaurants and inns are scattered across the region.

What to eat

Greater Poland cuisine has several dishes you must try. Here are the most important ones.

Main courses

  • Pyry z gzikiem — potatoes (pyry) with curd cheese (gzik), a Greater Poland classic. Available at milk bars and inns.
  • Duck with apples — roast duck with apples, a Greater Poland classic. Available at restaurants.
  • Greater Poland pork knuckle — roast pork knuckle with cabbage and potatoes. Available at inns.

Soups

  • Greater Poland żurek — rye sour soup with white sausage and egg. Available at milk bars.
  • Greater Poland krupnik — barley and potato soup. Available at inns.
  • Pickle soup — sour cucumber soup, a Polish classic. Available at milk bars.

Sweets

  • St Martin's croissants — croissants of puff pastry filled with white poppy seeds, dried fruit, and almonds. On the EU protected products list. Available at pastry shops in Poznań.
  • Szneki z glancem — sweet yeast buns with crumble. Available at bakeries in Poznań.
  • Szagówki — potato dough dumplings, served with cabbage. Available at inns.

Where to eat

Greater Poland has many places where you can try regional cuisine. Here are the most important ones.

Poznań

  • Milk bars — several milk bars in central Poznań, with Greater Poland classics. Worth it for cheap and good meals.
  • Regional restaurants — several restaurants in Poznań specialise in Greater Poland cuisine. Worth it for higher standards.
  • Pastry shops — numerous pastry shops in Poznań offer St Martin's croissants and szneki z glancem. Worth it for sweets.

Region

  • Country inns — several country inns in the region offer Greater Poland cuisine in a home-style version. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Agritourism farms — several agritourism farms offer meals from local produce. Worth it for authenticity.
  • Market stalls — numerous market stalls at Poznań markets offer local products. Worth it for shopping.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — food festival season. Worth it for events.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people.
  • November–March — St Martin's croissant season (November). Worth it for sweets.

Getting there

  • By car: ~3 hours from Warsaw (A2), ~3 hours from Berlin (A2), ~2 hours from Wrocław (S5).
  • By train: Poznań has a railway station, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By bus: PKS and private carriers serve most towns and small towns.

Greater Poland cuisine is best explored in Poznań — the city has the most restaurants, bars, and pastry shops. The region is best explored by car — restaurants and inns are scattered across the area.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Poznań, pyry z gzikiem, St Martin's croissants.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Poznań, pyry z gzikiem, St Martin's croissants, duck with apples, pork knuckle.

A week lets you explore all of Greater Poland cuisine — from Poznań to Kalisz, with numerous stops.

Practical tips

  • Milk bars — the cheapest way to try Greater Poland cuisine. Look for them in city centres.
  • St Martin's croissants — original croissants have an EU certificate. Buy only at certified pastry shops.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Food festivals — Poznań hosts numerous food festivals (e.g. the Good Taste Festival). Check the calendar.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — good throughout the region, including in smaller towns.

Tip

Try St Martin's croissants only at pastry shops with the EU certificate — a guarantee they are made to the original recipe.
