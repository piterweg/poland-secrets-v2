POMERANIA

Kashubia — Herring, Eel and Kashubian Broth

2–3 days
Home›Pomerania›Kashubia

Fish from the Baltic and lakes, potatoes, cabbage, and mushrooms. Kashubian cuisine is the taste of the sea meeting the forest. Slow travel through Kashubian cuisine.

Why Kashubian cuisine, differently

Kashubian cuisine is a combination of two elements: sea and forest. On one hand, Baltic fish — herring, cod, flounder — on the other, lake fish, mushrooms, berries, and game. All this makes Kashubian cuisine one of the most diverse in Poland. For centuries Kashubians lived on what the sea and forest gave them — and this philosophy has survived to this day.

Slow travel through Kashubian cuisine is a celebration of taste and simplicity. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a village inn, in an agritourism farm, in a small fish shop by a lake. Each dish has its own story, its own cook, its own village. Ask where the fish comes from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Kashubian cuisine splits into several categories: Baltic fish (herring, cod, flounder), lake fish (vendace, whitefish, eel), potato dishes (golce, pancakes), cabbage dishes (cabbage with mushrooms), soups (Kashubian broth, fish soup), baked goods (Kashubian bread, szneki z glancem). Exploring the culinary region of Kashubia takes 2–3 days.

Baltic fish

  • Herring in oil — a classic of Kashubian cuisine. Marinated herring, served with onion, oil, and potatoes in their jackets. There are many variations — in sour cream, in vinegar, with mustard. The best in small fish shops by the sea.
  • Fresh cod — Baltic cod, fried or baked, with garlic sauce or butter. Worth it in small fish shops in Gdynia, Hel, Władysławowo.
  • Fried flounder — a flat Baltic fish, fried in a pan, served with fries, salad, and lemon. A classic of seaside fish shops.
  • Smoked halibut — increasingly available on the Kashubian coast. Traditionally smoked in home smokehouses.

Lake fish and seafood

  • Smoked eel — eel from Kashubian lakes, a delicacy hard to find outside the region. Traditionally smoked in home smokehouses, it has a deep, smoky flavor and delicate texture.
  • Smoked vendace — a delicate fish from Kashubian lakes, traditionally smoked. Worth trying in small smokehouses by the lakes.
  • Smoked whitefish — similar to vendace but larger. Smoked, served with bread and butter.
  • Kashubian carp — carp from Kashubian lakes, prepared in various ways: fried, baked, in jelly. Served at holidays and family celebrations.

Potato, cabbage, and mushroom dishes

  • Golce — the Kashubian version of Silesian dumplings, potato balls with flour, boiled in water. Served with onion, lard, or mushroom sauce.
  • Potato pancakes — in the Kashubian version with marjoram and bacon. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Kashubian cabbage with mushrooms — braised cabbage with dried mushrooms, onion, and caraway. Served with meats, especially game and roasts.
  • Mushroom soup — soup from dried mushrooms with groats or noodles. Warms you on cold days.
  • Kashubian broth — chicken broth with dried mushrooms, with noodles or dumplings. In the Kashubian version — with root vegetables and herbs.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal fish, long days. Worth a walk through fish shops.
  • July–September — season for fresh fish, fruit, mushrooms. Worth it for fish markets in Gdynia and Hel.
  • September–October — second best. Mushrooms, herring, fewer people. September is a good time for mushroom picking in Kashubian forests.
  • November–March — season for herring, smoked eel, soups. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Kashubian cuisine is from the northern region of Poland — best to travel by car or train, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Gdańsk, Gdynia, Kartuzy, Kościerzyna have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~30 minutes from Gdańsk (DK20), ~4 hours from Warsaw (S7), ~3 hours from Poznań (S5 and DK20).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Gdańsk or Gdynia (fish shops, herring, cod) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Gdańsk, Gdynia, Kartuzy, Kościerzyna.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of Kashubia — all the most important dishes and places.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose fish shops and village inns. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Herring in oil — a Kashubian classic, available at every fish shop.
  • Smoked eel — a regional delicacy, available at smokehouses in Kartuzy and Kościerzyna.
  • Golce — the Kashubian version of dumplings, available at village inns.
  • Kashubian broth — chicken broth with dried mushrooms, available in local restaurants.
  • Szneki z glancem — sweet yeast buns, available at bakeries in Kartuzy.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region (Browar PG4, Browar Puck).

Accommodation: Gdańsk (largest choice, more options), Sopot (resort, more options), Gdynia (town, more options), Kartuzy (small town, smaller choice), Kościerzyna (small town, smaller choice), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Fish shops — usually open from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m., longer in season. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants in city centres.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (smoking fish, baking bread, making pierogi). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Kashubian cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Fish markets — the best place to buy fresh fish. Held regularly in Gdynia and Hel.
  • Weather — coastal weather changes fast. Even in July, bring a windproof jacket and hat.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Herring in oil in Kościerzyna
  2. Smoked eel from the lake
  3. Kashubian broth in a local inn

Tip

Try Kashubian broth in Kościerzyna or Kartuzy — the most traditional versions of this dish, made with local ingredients.
