Lake Solina — Waterside Relaxation in Bieszczady

1–2 days
Home›Subcarpathia›Lake Solina

Deep blue water, green hills and quiet coves. Slow travel at Lake Solina — where water meets the silence of the Bieszczady mountains.

Why Solina, differently

Lake Solina is sometimes called the Bieszczady Sea — and not without reason. It is Poland's largest artificial reservoir (~22 km², ~27 km long), ringed by the hills of the Bieszczady. The water has a deep sapphire hue rarely found in Polish lakes, and the shoreline is so jagged that its coves and headlands can hardly be counted.

Slow travel at Solina is a celebration of idleness. It is hours on a boat, drifting into hidden bays, watching the sun disappear behind the horizon and paint the water orange. Instead of chasing viewpoints, find your own place — on grass, by a jetty, in a quiet fishing village. This is a lake that rewards those who slow down.

What to see

Lake Solina divides into several zones: the dam area (most touristy), Polańczyk (resort and sanatoria), the middle stretch around Wołkowyja and Bukowiec (quieter), and the eastern end at Myczkowce (a smaller reservoir, less known).

The dam and Solina village

  • Solina Dam — the tallest dam in Poland (82 m high, 664 m long). The view from the dam crest over the lake and the San valley is one of the most recognisable vistas in the country. Walk the crest and look down.
  • Solina Gondola Lift — new (2022), with two stations and a viewing tower at the summit of Jawor. Alpine-style gondolas lift you to ~600 m a.s.l. for a panorama of the lake and the Bieszczady. Book online in season.
  • Viewing tower above the dam — an alternative to the gondola, less known. A viewpoint with a panorama of the reservoir and dam.
  • Lake cruises — the White Fleet Solina runs cruises from Solina and Polańczyk. The route typically covers the western part of the lake, with views of the dams and coves. A cruise lasts 1–2 hours.

Polańczyk — the lakeside resort

  • Polańczyk — the largest resort on Solina, with sanatoria, guesthouses and numerous water equipment rentals. Beaches and jetties along the shore, plenty of restaurants.
  • Main beach in Polańczyk — lifeguarded in season, with kayak, pedal boat and boat rentals. Clear water, sandy bottom.
  • Polańczyk viewpoint — from the viewpoint on the road to Solina, a panorama of the lake with the peninsula and Skalisty Island.
  • Skalisty Island — the only island on Lake Solina, near Polańczyk. Reachable by kayak or small boat. Also called the Island of Love.

Villages around the lake

  • Wołkowyja — a village on the southern shore, with many farm stays and jetties. A quiet base, less commercial than Polańczyk.
  • Bukowiec — a small settlement with jetties and wild coves. Ideal for those seeking absolute silence.
  • Terka and Zatwarnica — villages on the San above the lake. Worth visiting for their churches and former Lemko atmosphere.
  • Myczkowce — a smaller reservoir below Solina, with a hydroelectric plant. Less known, with quiet coves, popular with anglers.
  • Churches at Bóbrka and Wołkowyja — 19th-century wooden Orthodox churches, part of the Wooden Architecture Route. Worth a stop on the way to the lake.

Day trips into the Bieszczady

  • Połonina Wetlińska and Caryńska — if you want a break from the water, walk a meadow ridge. Entry from Wetlina or Brzegi Górne.
  • Bieszczady Forest Railway — narrow-gauge railway in Cisna, running from May to October.
  • Sanok and the Folk Architecture Museum — open-air museum and Icon Museum, an hour's drive from Solina.
  • Lesko and its Jewish cemetery — a small town with a historic Jewish cemetery and synagogue, on the way from Sanok to Solina.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Lake calm, crowds not yet arrived, water cold but clear. Perfect for renting a kayak or sailing boat.
  • July and August — peak season. Polańczyk full of tourists, prices high, but the water is warmest (up to 24°C on hot days) and all attractions operate.
  • September — the best compromise. Water still warm, fewer crowds, autumn colours in the Bieszczady around the lake.
  • October — end of the water season. Rentals close, but the lake is absolutely calm. Views of autumn beeches — spectacular.
  • Winter — the reservoir rarely freezes entirely, but the shores are ice-bound. The area empties out, beautiful for photographers. Note: many guesthouses are closed.

Getting there

  • By car: ~4–5 hours from Kraków (via Nowy Sącz and Krosno), ~1.5 hours from Rzeszów, ~6–7 hours from Warsaw. Route 84 from Sanok to Solina runs through scenic countryside.
  • By train: nearest stations are Sanok and Zagórz. Then PKS buses to Polańczyk or Solina (sparse services, check timetables).
  • By bus: from Rzeszów and Sanok to Polańczyk; seasonal services in summer. Off-season transport is very limited.
  • By plane: Rzeszów-Jasionka airport (RZE); from the airport to Polańczyk ~2 hours by car.

Public transport around Solina is poor. A car or bike makes moving between villages and viewpoints far easier.

How much time you need

A weekend (2–3 days) is the minimum — see the dam, take a cruise, spend a day on the beach at Polańczyk.

A week is realistic for a first visit. Three days on the water (kayak, sailing, cruise), two days in the Bieszczady (Połonina Wetlińska, Cisna), two days exploring the area (Sanok, Lesko, wooden churches).

Two weeks lets you combine Solina with the whole Bieszczady and the Lower Beskid. An ideal plan for a family holiday or a longer digital detox.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Grilled trout — from local farms in the San and smaller streams. A Solina classic.
  • Whitefish (sielawa and sieja) — lake fish, smoked or fried. Buy at the smokehouse in Polańczyk or Solina.
  • Bieszczady kwaśnica — sour cabbage soup with meat. Warming after a day on the water.
  • Proziaki — soda bread pancakes, served with garlic butter or lard. A Bieszczady classic.
  • Fuczki — cabbage fritters, a Lemko dish. Worth trying in local taverns.
  • Local craft beer — Sanok has a craft brewery (Browar Sanok), and there are several small ones nearby.

Accommodation bases: Polańczyk (largest choice — sanatoria, hotels, guesthouses — but pricey in season), Solina (small village by the dam, guesthouses), Wołkowyja and Bukowiec (farm stays, quiet), Myczkowce (peaceful, less touristy). Campsites by the lake — cheap in season, mostly open June–September only.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — the Solina gondola and lake cruises are worth booking online in July and August. Kayak and sailing boat rentals in Polańczyk — available on site, but come early in peak season.
  • Sailing licence — for a boat with sail area up to 10 m², a short course at the rental is enough. Above that — a sailing licence. Kayaking requires nothing.
  • Water safety — the lake is large and deep; wind can pick up suddenly. Life jackets are mandatory for children. Do not paddle far from shore in strong wind.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — by the shore, in the reeds, troublesome in summer. Always carry repellent.
  • Prices — in season Polańczyk is expensive (restaurants, guesthouses). Villages outside Polańczyk are noticeably cheaper.
  • Mobile signal — good across the lake, but fades deep in the Bieszczady.
  • Fuel and shopping — petrol stations in Lesko and Sanok. Smaller villages have poorly stocked shops — do a bigger shop on the way.

Trip plan

  1. Lakeside walk and dam viewpoint
  2. Picnic and sunbathing in quiet bays
  3. Sunset boat cruise over the water

Tip

Rent a boat early in the morning. When the lake is still smooth as a mirror and mists hover over the water, you can feel absolute unity with nature.
