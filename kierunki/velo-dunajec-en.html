Velo Dunajec — By Bike Along the Dunajec

3–4 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Velo Dunajec

240 km through the Pieniny, Beskid Sądecki, and Podhale. A route for those who love water and mountains. Slow travel along Velo Dunajec.

Why Velo Dunajec, differently

Velo Dunajec is a cycling route that runs along one of the most beautiful rivers in Poland — the Dunajec. 240 km through Podhale, the Pieniny, and the Beskid Sądecki is not just a cycling trail but a journey through the most interesting landscapes of southern Poland. The Dunajec is a river that connects regions — from the Tatras, through the Pieniny, to the Beskid Sądecki and the Sądecczyzna.

Slow travel on Velo Dunajec is above all about paying attention to details. A chapel along the route, old wooden cottages, the view of Trzy Korony — all this makes the route not just a physical challenge but also a cultural experience. Adjust your pace to your own abilities — 40–60 km per day is the optimal distance that lets you really see the region. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are on the route, the more details you notice: wooden architecture, churches, shrines, landscapes that change with every kilometre.

What to see

Velo Dunajec splits into several sections: Podhale (from Nowy Targ), the Pieniny (from Czorsztyn to Szczawnica), the Beskid Sądecki (from Szczawnica to Nowy Sącz), Sądecczyzna (from Nowy Sącz to the border). Riding the whole route takes 3–4 days.

Podhale

  • Nowy Targ — a town in Podhale, with a market square and accommodation base. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Czorsztyn and Niedzica — two castles over Lake Czorsztyn. Worth it for architecture and views.
  • Lake Czorsztyn — an artificial reservoir on the Dunajec, with dams in Niedzica and Czorsztyn. Worth it for views.
  • Dębno — a village with the Gothic Church of St. Michael the Archangel (UNESCO). Worth it for architecture.
  • Frydman — a village with a castle and wooden church. Worth it for architecture.

Pieniny

  • Czorsztyn — a village with castle ruins. Worth it for history and views.
  • Sromowce Niżne and Wyżne — villages on the Dunajec, the start of the rafting. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Dunajec rafting — raft trips, one of the region's greatest attractions. Worth it for the experience.
  • Trzy Korony — the highest peak of the Pieniny (982 m), accessible on foot. Worth it for views.
  • Sokolica — a peak with a famous pine, with a view over the Dunajec gorge. Worth it for photography.
  • Szczawnica — a spa at the foot of the Pieniny, with a promenade and mineral water pump room. Worth it for the atmosphere.

Beskid Sądecki and Sądecczyzna

  • Krościenko on the Dunajec — a small town at the foot of the Pieniny, with accommodation base. Worth a walk.
  • Łącko — a village famous for plums and plum brandy. Worth it for local products.
  • Stary Sącz — a small town with a Poor Clares monastery, one of the oldest in Poland. Worth it for architecture.
  • Nowy Sącz — the regional capital, with a market square and castle. Worth it for culture.
  • Grybów — a small town with a market square and church. Worth a walk.
  • Beskid Sądecki — a mountain range around the Dunajec, with numerous trails. Worth it for walks.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth cycling.
  • September — second best. Warm days, fewer people, autumn colours in the Pieniny and Beskid Sądecki.
  • July and August — peak season. Route crowded, Dunajec rafting needs booking, prices higher. Worth it for swimming in the Dunajec.
  • October — end of the season. Still beautiful autumn colours, but the weather can be capricious. Worth it for those with a flexible plan.
  • November–April — the route in winter is less accessible (snow, ice). Worth it for experienced cyclists.

Getting there

  • By train: Nowy Targ and Nowy Sącz have railway stations, with connections from Kraków (~2 hours), Zakopane (~1 hour), Rzeszów (~2.5 hours). You can reach any point on the route by train.
  • By car: ~1.5 hours from Kraków (via Rabka), ~45 minutes from Zakopane, ~2.5 hours from Rzeszów.
  • By bus: PKS and Szwagropol from Kraków and Zakopane to Nowy Targ, Szczawnica, Krościenko and Nowy Sącz.

Velo Dunajec is well connected to the rest of Poland — you can start anywhere, arrive by train, ride a chosen stage and return by train. Plan the route so that the start and end of the stage are near railway stations.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one stage of 40–60 km per day, around a chosen town (e.g. Nowy Targ, Szczawnica, Nowy Sącz).

Three days is realistic for a first visit. You can ride 120–180 km, completing one region (e.g. Podhale, Pieniny or Beskid Sądecki).

Four to five days is time for the whole route — from Nowy Targ to the Ukrainian border. This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

A week — if you want to ride truly slow, with numerous stops, sightseeing and rest. Combine cycling with Dunajec rafting or a visit to a spa.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Podhale cuisine — oscypek, kwaśnica, moskole. Worth trying at highland taverns.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms in the Dunajec and smaller streams.
  • Duck with apples — a regional classic, available in elegant restaurants.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Nowy Targ, Szczawnica and Nowy Sącz have several good cafés.
  • Śliwowica łącka — a local plum brandy from Łącko, available in regional shops.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), campsites (for those who want to sleep in a tent), PTTK mountain huts (in the mountains). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bike — a trekking or gravel bike is best for Velo Dunajec. The route runs mainly on asphalt and gravel roads. In the Beskid Sądecki there are harder climbs.
  • Bookings — book accommodation on the route in advance, especially in summer season (July–August). In May and September it is easier.
  • Signage — the route is well marked (Velo Dunajec logo, information boards). GPS navigation is not necessary, but have an app with the route.
  • Dunajec rafting — book the rafting in advance. In season spots run out quickly.
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Transport — Polish trains have bike carriages. Use them if you want to transport bikes between stages.
  • Bike service — Nowy Targ, Szczawnica and Nowy Sącz have bike workshops.

Trip plan

  1. Nowy Targ — Czorsztyn
  2. Pieniny and Szczawnica
  3. Finale in Nowy Sącz

Tip

Plan the route so that you devote one day just to the Pieniny. It is worth leaving the bike in Szczawnica and walking up to Trzy Korony or Sokolica.
