POMERANIA

Hel Peninsula — Between Two Shores

2 days
Home›Pomerania›Hel Peninsula

Bay breezes, dedicated coastal bike paths, and beaches on both sides of the narrow spit. Slow travel through the Hel Peninsula — the edge of the land.

Why Hel, differently

The Hel Peninsula is a special place — a narrow strip of land that boldly stretches into the sea, dividing the Baltic from the calm waters of the Puck Bay. It is between a few hundred metres and just 200 metres wide, about 35 km long, and one of the most distinctive landscapes of the Polish coast. This is a place that, with every minute of the journey, takes you further from the noise of everyday life.

Slow travel through Hel is a celebration of the horizon and the wind. By bike from Władysławowo to Hel is a lesson in humility before the elements. When on one side we have wild dunes and on the other the calm surface of the bay, you can feel the world shrinking to that one narrow path, and the only goal becomes the movement itself and the rhythm of breathing. This is a land that rewards those who slow down.

What to see

The Hel Peninsula splits into several parts: Władysławowo (entrance to the peninsula), Chałupy, Jastarnia, Jurata, Kuźnica and Hel (at the end). Two to three days are best to visit.

Hel and the seal sanctuary

  • Hel — a town at the end of the peninsula, with a fishing harbour, lighthouse and famous seal sanctuary. Worth seeing the seals at the Seal Sanctuary of the Marine Station of the Institute of Oceanography of the University of Gdańsk — one of the few places in Poland where you can see these animals up close.
  • Hel Lighthouse — from the 19th century, one of the oldest on the Polish coast. Worth climbing to the top — a view over the open sea and the Puck Bay.
  • Fishing harbour — cutters, nets, fishermen at work. Worth seeing in the morning when they return from fishing.
  • Museum of Fisheries — in a former Evangelical church, an exhibition on the history of fishing in the Baltic.
  • Beach at the tip — at the end of the peninsula, where two waters meet. Worth sitting at sunset.

Jastarnia, Jurata and Kuźnica

  • Jastarnia — a fishing town with a harbour and numerous guesthouses. A good base for kitesurfers — the best conditions in Poland are in this area.
  • Jurata — a pre-war resort with wooden villas from the 1930s. Worth seeing for the architecture and atmosphere.
  • Kuźnica — a fishing village with a harbour and beach. Less known but pleasant.
  • Chłapowo and Chałupy — smaller towns at the start of the peninsula, with campsites and beaches.

Władysławowo and surroundings

  • Władysławowo — a town at the entrance to the peninsula, with a harbour, beach and avenue of sports stars. Worth a walk and for accommodation base.
  • Chłapowo Ravine — a loess ravine west of Władysławowo, with interesting vegetation and paths.
  • Star of the North — a rock at the tip of Władysławowo, a viewpoint.
  • Słone Łąki Reserve — a halophytic reserve in the area, with unique halophilic vegetation.

Lesser-known corners

  • Cycling path through the peninsula — one of the most beautiful cycling routes in Poland, running through the entire peninsula. Rent a bike and ride from Władysławowo to Hel (~35 km).
  • Wild beaches on the bay side — warmer, calmer and less crowded than the beaches on the open sea side.
  • Fishing cottages in Kuźnica — wooden fishing architecture, still present in smaller villages.
  • WWII bunkers — remnants of German fortifications, scattered across the peninsula. Worth it for history.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, beaches almost empty. Weather can be capricious, but evenings stay light until 10 p.m.
  • July and August — peak season. Peninsula crowded, prices high, but all attractions open. Come on a weekday.
  • September — second best. Warm sea, fewer crowds, autumn colours over the bay.
  • October and November — end of the season, but the peninsula is quiet and beautiful. Worth it for those who seek solitude.
  • Winter — Hel in winter can be harsh, but empty. Worth it for those who want to see the sea without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Gdańsk (DW216), ~50 minutes from Gdynia. In season, the road to the peninsula can be crowded — better to come by train.
  • By train: railway line from Gdynia to Hel, through the entire peninsula. Services hourly, journey takes ~2 hours. Convenient way to reach the peninsula without a car.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Gdańsk and Gdynia.
  • By bike: cycling path from Władysławowo to Hel (~35 km), one of the most beautiful routes in Poland.

On the peninsula itself you can walk, bike or take the train. A car is not necessary — and in season it can be troublesome (traffic jams, parking difficulties).

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — cycling through the peninsula, Hel with the seal sanctuary, a walk on the beach.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for Hel, one for Jastarnia and Jurata, one for Władysławowo and surroundings.

A week lets you explore the whole peninsula, the surroundings (Puck, Gdynia) and spend a few days in one of the towns.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Baltic fish — herring, flounder, cod, smoked salmon. Best in small fry shops at the harbour.
  • Herring in oil — a coastal cuisine classic, available at every fish shop.
  • Smoked eel — a regional delicacy, available at smokehouses in Hel and Kuźnica.
  • Kashubian cuisine — in the region, try ruchanki, kiszka z kaszy i krwi.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Hel and Jurata have several good cafés, ideal for a break.

Accommodation: Hel (largest choice, but expensive), Jastarnia (kitesurfing, harbour), Jurata (pre-war resort, quiet), Kuźnica (smaller town, quiet), Chałupy (campsites, cheaper), Władysławowo (entrance to the peninsula). Book ahead in summer season (2–3 weeks).

Practical tips

  • Bookings — book seal sanctuary tickets in advance. Hotels and guesthouses in season (July–August) must be booked 2–3 weeks ahead.
  • Weather — the peninsula is windy (hence kitesurfing popularity). Even in July, bring a windproof jacket.
  • Beaches — beaches on the bay side are warmer and calmer than those on the open sea side. Choose the former.
  • Bike — the cycling path through the peninsula is one of the best routes in Poland. Rent a bike and ride the whole route.
  • Seal sanctuary — seal feeding takes place at specific times. Check the schedule before your visit.
  • Parking — in season car parks are paid and crowded. Leave the car in Władysławowo and travel by train or bike.
  • Signal — good in most towns, weaker on wild beaches.

Trip plan

  1. Cycling from Władysławowo to Hel
  2. Historic Hel Lighthouse
  3. Puck Bay at sunset

Tip

Walk to the very end of the spit, where the land definitively ends and the open sea begins. It is the perfect place for reflection on the power of the elements.
