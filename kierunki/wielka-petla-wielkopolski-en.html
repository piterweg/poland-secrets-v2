GREATER POLAND

Greater Poland Loop — The Great Greater Poland Loop

4–5 days
Home›Greater Poland›Greater Poland Loop

600 km through Wielkopolski National Park, the Warta Valley, and the surroundings of Poznań. A route for patient cyclists. Slow travel along the Great Greater Poland Loop.

Why the Great Greater Poland Loop, differently

The Great Greater Poland Loop is a cycling route that circles Greater Poland — through Wielkopolski National Park, the Warta Valley, the surroundings of Poznań, and numerous smaller towns of the region. 600 km of route is a journey through the region where Poland was born. This is not a route for those seeking mountain challenges — it is a route for those who want to truly get to know the region, its villages, towns and people.

Slow travel on the Great Loop is above all about paying attention to the details of the Greater Poland landscape — fields, lakes, villages, palaces, wooden churches. It is worth stopping at each of these elements and giving it a moment. The route is gentle — it is a flat land, ideal for cyclists who are not looking for mountain challenges but above all for contact with nature and regional culture. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are on the route, the more details you notice: traces of old estates, interesting architectural solutions, people who have lived here for generations.

What to see

The Great Greater Poland Loop splits into several sections: northern (around Piła and Chodzież), eastern (around Gniezno and Wągrowiec), southern (around Kalisz and Ostrów Wielkopolski), western (around Leszno and Wolsztyn). Riding the whole route takes 4–5 days.

Poznań and surroundings

  • Poznań — the regional capital, with a market square, Ostrów Tumski and Jeżyce. Worth it for culture and atmosphere.
  • Wielkopolski National Park — a park with glacial lakes and moraine hills. Worth it for nature.
  • Puszczykowo — a small town near Poznań, with the Arkady Fiedler Museum. Worth it for culture.
  • Rogalin — the Raczyński Palace with park and Rogalin oaks. Worth it for architecture and nature.
  • Kórnik — a castle with a dendrological park, one of the most beautiful in Poland. Worth it for architecture and nature.

Northern and eastern Greater Poland

  • Gniezno — the first capital of Poland, with a cathedral and the Gniezno Doors. Worth it for history.
  • Wągrowiec — a small town with a Cistercian monastery and market square. Worth it for architecture.
  • Piła — a town in northern Greater Poland, with a market square and park. Worth a walk.
  • Chodzież — a small town with a porcelain factory, one of the oldest in Poland. Worth it for craft.
  • Szamotuły — a small town with the Górka Castle and collegiate church. Worth it for architecture.

Southern and western Greater Poland

  • Kalisz — one of the oldest towns in Poland, with a market square and cathedral. Worth it for history.
  • Ostrów Wielkopolski — a town with a market square and accommodation base. Worth a walk.
  • Leszno — a town with a market square and Baroque townhouses. Worth it for architecture.
  • Wolsztyn — a small town with a steam locomotive depot, one of the oldest in Europe. Worth it for railway enthusiasts.
  • Rydzyna — a small town with a Baroque castle, one of the most beautiful in Poland. Worth it for architecture.
  • Gołuchów — a castle with a park and art collection. Worth it for architecture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth cycling.
  • September — second best. Warm days, fewer people, autumn colours in the parks.
  • July and August — peak season. Route crowded, prices higher, but all attractions open. Worth it for swimming in the lakes.
  • October — end of the season. Still beautiful autumn colours, but the weather can be capricious. Worth it for those with a flexible plan.
  • November–April — the route in winter is less accessible (snow, ice). Worth it for experienced cyclists.

Getting there

  • By train: Poznań, Gniezno, Kalisz, Leszno and Piła have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities. You can reach any point on the route by train.
  • By car: ~3 hours from Warsaw to Poznań (S8 and A2), ~3 hours from Berlin (A2), ~2 hours from Wrocław (S5 and S11).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

The Great Greater Poland Loop is well connected to the rest of Poland — you can start anywhere, arrive by train, ride a chosen stage and return by train.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one stage of 60–80 km per day, around a chosen town (e.g. Poznań, Gniezno, Kalisz).

A week is realistic for a first visit. You can ride 300–400 km, completing one region (e.g. northern or southern Greater Poland).

Two weeks is time for the whole loop — 600 km through all regions of Greater Poland. This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

Three weeks — if you want to ride truly slow, with numerous stops, sightseeing and rest.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • St. Martin's croissant — the most recognisable pastry of Greater Poland, with white poppy seeds, dried fruit and almonds. Baked only on 11 November, but today available in pastry shops throughout the year.
  • Pyry z gzikiem — potatoes in their jackets with curd cheese, onion and sour cream. A Greater Poland classic.
  • Goose for St. Martin's Day — roast goose with modra kapusta and dumplings. Seasonally, from November.
  • Szneki z glancem — sweet yeast buns with streusel and icing. Popular for breakfast throughout Greater Poland.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — each larger town on the route has several good cafés.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), campsites (for those who want to sleep in a tent), palaces and manor houses (for those who want to sleep in a unique place — Rogalin, Kórnik, Gołuchów). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bike — a trekking or gravel bike is best for the Great Greater Poland Loop. The route runs mainly on asphalt and gravel roads. The terrain is flat — a mountain bike is not necessary.
  • Bookings — book accommodation on the route in advance, especially in summer season (July–August). In May and September it is easier.
  • Signage — the route is well marked (Great Loop logo, information boards). GPS navigation is not necessary, but have an app with the route.
  • Weather — Polish weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Transport — Polish trains have bike carriages. Use them if you want to transport bikes between stages.
  • Bike service — Poznań, Gniezno, Kalisz and Leszno have bike workshops.
  • Cuisine — Greater Poland is famous for the St. Martin's croissant and goose. Plan the route to hit a local holiday or festival.

Trip plan

  1. Poznań — Puszczykowo
  2. Wielkopolski National Park
  3. Rogalin, Kórnik and surroundings

Tip

Don't try to ride the entire loop in one trip. Divide it into 2–3 stages of 200–300 km each and complete them over successive years — each offers a different experience.
