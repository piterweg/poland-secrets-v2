POMERANIA

Kociewie — Kociewie Soups and Baltic Herring

1–2 days
Home›Pomerania›Kociewie

Kociewie cuisine is simple, nourishing soups, fish from the Baltic, and dishes made with groats. A region where home cooking tastes best. Slow travel through Kociewie cuisine.

Why Kociewie cuisine, differently

Kociewie cuisine is home cuisine — simple, nourishing soups, fish from the Baltic, dishes made with groats and potatoes. Without fireworks, without exoticism, but with a deep flavor that is remembered for a long time. Kociewie is an ethnographic region in Pomerania, next to Kashubia, but less known. Kociewie cuisine is the cuisine of working people — farmers, fishermen, craftsmen — who needed filling meals to survive a hard day.

Slow travel through Kociewie cuisine is a celebration of taste and simplicity. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a village inn, in a milk bar, in an agritourism farm. Each dish has its own story, its own cook, its own village. Ask where the ingredients come from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Kociewie cuisine splits into several categories: soups (Kociewie soup, żur, krupnik), fish (herring, cod, flounder), groat dishes (groats with goulash, krupnioki), potato dishes (pancakes, kopytka), baked goods (Kociewie bread, kołocz). Exploring the culinary region of Kociewie takes 1–2 days.

Kociewie soups

  • Kociewie soup — a regional classic. A thick soup with vegetables, groats, and meat, served with bread. There are many variations, depending on the household and season. All share one thing: they fill you up, warm you, and taste like home.
  • Kociewie żur — sour rye soup with white sausage and egg. A Kociewie classic.
  • Kociewie krupnik — barley groat soup with vegetables and meat. Hearty, warming.
  • Fish soup — soup based on Baltic fish broth, with vegetables and sour cream. Served with croutons or bread.

Fish and groat dishes

  • Baltic herring in oil — one of the most characteristic dishes of the region. Marinated, in oil, in sour cream — served with potatoes in their jackets and onion.
  • Fresh cod — Baltic cod, fried or baked, with garlic sauce or butter. Worth it in small fish shops in Tczew and Starogard.
  • Fried flounder — a flat Baltic fish, fried in a pan, served with fries, salad, and lemon. A classic of seaside fish shops.
  • Kaszanka with onion — buckwheat groat and blood sausage, fried with onion. Served with potatoes, bread, or as a snack.
  • Kociewie krupnioki — buckwheat groat and blood sausage, similar to kaszanka. Served hot, with mustard or onion.

Potato dishes and baked goods

  • Potato pancakes — in the Kociewie version with marjoram and bacon. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Kopytka — potato dumplings in an elongated shape, served with sauce, meat, or as a standalone dish.
  • Kociewie bread — sourdough bread made from rye and wheat flour. Served with all dishes, especially with butter and local honey.
  • Kociewie kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Baked for weddings and family celebrations.
  • Kociewie cheesecake — cheesecake with curd cheese, raisins, and orange peel. Available at pastry shops in Tczew.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal fish, long days. Worth a walk around fish shops.
  • July–September — season for fresh fish, fruit, mushrooms. Worth it for fish markets in Tczew and Starogard.
  • September–October — second best. Mushrooms, herring, fewer people. September is a good time for mushroom picking in Kociewie forests.
  • November–March — season for żur, krupnik, kaszanka. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Kociewie cuisine is from the northern region of Poland — best to travel by car or train, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Tczew, Starogard Gdański, Pelplin have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~40 minutes from Gdańsk (A1), ~1.5 hours from Bydgoszcz (DK91), ~3.5 hours from Warsaw (S7 and A1).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (1–2 days) is the minimum — Tczew or Starogard Gdański (fish shops, herring, Kociewie soup) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Tczew, Starogard Gdański, Pelplin, Gniew.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of Kociewie — all the most important dishes and places, and neighbouring Kashubia.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose fish shops and village inns. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Kociewie soup — a regional classic, available at village inns.
  • Baltic herring in oil — a coastal cuisine classic, available at fish shops.
  • Kaszanka with onion — a simple, filling dish, available at inns and markets.
  • Kociewie krupnioki — groat sausage, available in local shops.
  • Kociewie bread — sourdough bread, available at bakeries in Tczew and Starogard.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Tczew (town, more options), Starogard Gdański (town, more options), Pelplin (small town, smaller choice), Gniew (small town, smaller choice), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Fish shops — usually open from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m., longer in season. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants in city centres.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (baking bread, making kaszanka, smoking fish). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Kociewie cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Fish markets — the best place to buy fresh fish. Held regularly in Tczew and Starogard.
  • Weather — coastal weather changes fast. Even in July, bring a windproof jacket and hat.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Kociewie soup in Tczew
  2. Baltic herring in Starogard
  3. Kaszanka with onions for lunch

Tip

Order Kociewie soup at one of the local restaurants in Tczew or Starogard Gdański — preferably the version with buckwheat groats and fresh bread.
