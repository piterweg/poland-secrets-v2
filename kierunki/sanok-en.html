Sanok — Gateway to the Bieszczady

1 day
Home›Subcarpathia›Sanok

Sanok is the gateway to the Bieszczady and one of the oldest cities in Poland. Open-air museum, icons, castle. Slow travel through the Subcarpathian region.

Why it's worth it

Sanok is a city in the Subcarpathian voivodeship, on the San river, at the foot of the Bieszczady. It is one of the oldest cities in Poland — it received city rights in 1339, but settlement here dates back to the early Middle Ages. Sanok is a city that has everything a city on the border of cultures should have: a castle, an open-air museum, Orthodox churches, Roman Catholic churches, a synagogue, and museums. Add to that unique cultural heritage: the Historical Museum in Sanok with the largest collection of icons in Poland, the Museum of Folk Architecture (open-air museum), and works by Zdzisław Beksiński — one of the most outstanding Polish contemporary painters.

Slow travel through Sanok is a journey through a landscape that surprises with its diversity. The city has a castle, an open-air museum, museums, and Orthodox churches — all within a few kilometres. It is worth spending 2–3 days here to truly feel this landscape. The best way to explore Sanok is on foot, and the Bieszczady by car — public transport from Rzeszów and Kraków is good (trains, buses).

What to see

Sanok is divided into three main parts: the centre (Market Square and castle), the open-air museum (Museum of Folk Architecture), and the Bieszczady (surroundings). Exploring the city and surroundings takes 2–3 days.

Centre

  • Market Square — the main square of the city, with townhouses and a town hall. Worth it for architecture.
  • Royal Castle — a 14th-century castle, now home to the Historical Museum. Worth it for architecture.
  • Historical Museum — a museum with the largest collection of icons in Poland (about 700). Worth it for culture.

Open-air museum

  • Museum of Folk Architecture — one of the largest open-air museums in Poland, with buildings of the Pogórzanie, Lemkos, and Boykos. Worth it for culture.
  • Open-air Orthodox churches — several Orthodox churches moved to the museum from the area. Worth it for architecture.
  • Farmsteads — numerous peasant farmsteads from the 19th and 20th centuries. Worth it for culture.

Bieszczady

  • Bieszczady — mountains south of Sanok, among the wildest in Poland. Worth it for hiking.
  • Solina — a reservoir lake in the Bieszczady, with numerous harbours. Worth it for sailing.
  • Lesko — a small town with a synagogue and castle, on the way to the Bieszczady. Worth it for architecture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — Bieszczady and open-air museum season. Worth it for hiking, but crowded in Solina.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, mushroom picking in the forests.
  • November–March — the city is sleepy in winter, but museums are open. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1.5 hours from Rzeszów (DK28), ~3.5 hours from Kraków (DK28), ~5.5 hours from Warsaw (S7 + DK28).
  • By train: Sanok has a railway station, with connections from Kraków and Rzeszów.
  • By bus: PKS serves Sanok, Lesko, Ustrzyki Dolne, Solina.

Sanok is best explored on foot — the city is small, and attractions are close together. The Bieszczady is best explored by car or on foot.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Market Square, Castle, Open-air museum.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Market Square, Castle, Open-air museum, Historical Museum, Bieszczady.

A week lets you explore all of Sanok and the Bieszczady — from Lesko to Ustrzyki Dolne.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pierogi with bryndza — regional pierogi with sheep cheese, available at inns.
  • Proziaki — flatbreads of flour and sour milk, served with butter or lard.
  • Trout from the San — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Goat cheeses — from local farms, available in village shops.
  • Beer from Subcarpathian breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Sanok (city, more options), Lesko (small town, guesthouses), Solina (village, guesthouses), Ustrzyki Dolne (small town, guesthouses).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — Sanok's history is rich, take a guide at the castle and open-air museum.
  • Icons — the Historical Museum has about 700 icons. Allow at least 2 hours for them.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Open-air museum — in season, craft demonstrations and folklore events take place. Check the calendar.
  • Weather — Bieszczady weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — good in the city, weaker in the Bieszczady. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Sanok Skansen (Folk Architecture Museum)
  2. Icon collection at the Historical Museum
  3. Walk around the market square and along the San

Tip

Visit the Beksiński studio at the Historical Museum — one of the most important collections of this painter in Poland, with his paintings and photographs.
