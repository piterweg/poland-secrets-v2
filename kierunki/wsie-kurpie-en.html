Kurpie — Kurpie Wooden Villages

2–3 days
Home›Villages & rural regions›Kurpie

White cottages with ornate shutters, paper cutouts, and wood carvings. Villages where folklore is still alive. Slow travel through Kurpie villages.

Why Kurpie villages, differently

Kurpie is one of the most characteristic ethnographic regions in Poland. Villages scattered in the Green Forest and White Forest have preserved folk traditions that disappeared long ago in other parts of Poland. White cottages with ornate shutters, wooden sculptures, paper cutouts — all this is still a living part of the daily life of residents. This is one of the few places in Poland where folklore is not a tourist attraction but everyday life.

Slow travel through Kurpie villages is a journey through culture and tradition. Instead of rushing from village to village, stop in one longer — talk to residents, enter a cottage, see how cutouts are made, listen to how the village lives. Each village has its own history, its own cottages, its own inhabitants. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly understand about Kurpie culture.

What to see

Kurpie villages split into several groups: villages with white cottages (Kadzidło, Myszyniec, Łyse), villages with cutouts (Kadzidło, Łyse), villages with sculpture (Myszyniec, Dylewo), villages with beekeeping tradition (Green Forest). Exploring the region takes 1–2 days.

Villages with white cottages

  • Kadzidło — a village with numerous white cottages with ornate shutters. There is also a Kurpie open-air museum, presenting the traditional architecture of the region.
  • Myszyniec — a small town in the centre of Kurpie, with numerous Kurpie cottages and an open-air museum. Worth it for the atmosphere and craft shopping.
  • Łyse — a village with white cottages and a Kurpie cutout tradition. Worth it for craft.
  • Dylewo — a village with a wood carving tradition. Worth it for craft.

Kurpie cutouts and sculpture

  • Kurpie cutout — colorful paper compositions (flowers, birds, genre scenes). Workshops in Kadzidło, Łyse and other villages.
  • Wood carving — traditional Kurpie craft, including folk saints, shrines, figures of saints. Workshops in Myszyniec and Dylewo.
  • Kurpie embroidery — traditional embroidery craft, including embroidery on folk costumes. Workshops in Kadzidło and Łyse.
  • Open-air museum in Kadzidło — an ethnographic museum presenting the traditional architecture and craft of the region.
  • Kurpie farmsteads in Myszyniec — a complex of rural farmsteads, presenting the traditional architecture of the region.

Green Forest and beekeeping tradition

  • Green Forest — a forest complex around Kurpie, with numerous paths and trails. Worth a walk and bike ride.
  • Beekeeping tradition — Kurpie have been involved in beekeeping (forest apiculture) for centuries. In some villages you can still see barcie — hives in hollow tree trunks.
  • White Forest — a forest complex south of Kurpie, with numerous paths and trails. Worth a walk.
  • Villages with barcie — several villages in the Green Forest have preserved the beekeeping tradition. Pay attention to barcie in the forests.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk around the villages.
  • July–September — season for craft festivals and fairs. Worth it for cultural events.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people. September is a good time for mushroom picking in the Green Forest.
  • November–March — villages in winter are less accessible (some workshops closed). Worth it for those who want to see the villages without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Warsaw (S8 and DK61), ~2.5 hours from Olsztyn, ~2.5 hours from Białystok.
  • By train: Ostrołęka has a railway station, with connections from most Polish cities. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most villages.

Kurpie villages are best explored by car or bike — they are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 villages in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (1–2 days) is the minimum — Kadzidło, Myszyniec, Łyse.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Kadzidło, Myszyniec, Łyse, Dylewo, Green Forest.

A week lets you explore all Kurpie villages — from Ostrołęka to the White Forest, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Rejbak — potato pancakes with flour and cracklings, available at Kurpie farmsteads.
  • Fafernuchy — sweet cookies made of flour, honey, and spices, available at shops with local products.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a Kurpie classic, available at every farmstead.
  • Mushroom soup — soup from dried mushrooms, available at farmsteads and inns.
  • Kurpie liqueurs — krupnik, herbal liqueurs, available at Kurpie farmsteads.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Kadzidło (small town, smaller choice), Myszyniec (small town, smaller choice), Ostrołęka (town, more options), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Kurpie farmsteads — usually open from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., longer in season. Check hours before your trip.
  • Craft workshops — many farmsteads and agritourism businesses offer workshops (Kurpie cutout, wood carving, embroidery). Book ahead.
  • Shopping — buy directly from craftsmen. It is not only a souvenir but also support for vanishing trades.
  • Opening hours — many workshops are open only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Open-air museum in Kadzidło
  2. Kurpie paper-cut workshops
  3. Kurpie farmsteads in Myszyniec area

Tip

Visit Kurpie during one of the local holidays or festivals — that is when you can see folklore in its most authentic form.
