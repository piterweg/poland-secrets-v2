SILESIA

Żywiec Beskids — Mountains with Room to Rest

2–3 days
Home›Silesia›Żywiec Beskids

Lush greenery, scenic summits, and unhurried paths for a zero-pressure mountain holiday. Slow travel through the Żywiec Beskids.

Why the Żywiec Beskids, differently

The Żywiec Beskids are the second-highest mountain range in Poland (after the Tatras) — but touristically completely different. There are no queues, cable cars and crowds here. Instead, there are lush green meadows, extensive forests, wooden shepherd huts and trails where you can walk for hours without meeting a human. These are mountains for those who seek space, not attractions.

Slow travel through the Żywiec Beskids means giving up the race in favour of contemplation. Instead of conquering as many peaks as possible, choose one path and walk it attentively — stopping at shrines, shepherd huts, clearings. Here, in the rhythm of the mountain wind, you can remember that the most beautiful thing about travel is the unplanned. The Żywiec Beskids is a land that rewards the patient.

What to see

The Żywiec Beskids splits into several mountain groups: Pilsko and Babia Góra (west), the Polica and Jałowiec ranges (centre), the Wielka Racza and Rycerzowa ranges (east). Two to four days are best to visit.

Pilsko and surroundings

  • Pilsko (1,557 m) — the second-highest peak of the Żywiec Beskids (after Babia Góra). A papal cross at the summit, commemorating John Paul II's visit in 1998. From Pilsko, views over the Tatras, Babia Góra and Slovak mountains.
  • Hala Miziowa — a pastoral meadow on the slopes of Pilsko, with a PTTK shelter. Good base for the summit and a place to rest.
  • Pilsko Reserve — a nature reserve on the slopes of Pilsko, protecting old forests and rare plant sites. Entry only on marked trails.
  • Korbielów — a village at the foot of Pilsko, with ski lifts and accommodation base. Good starting point.

Polica and Jałowiec range

  • Polica (1,369 m) — a peak in the Polica range, with views over Babia Góra and the Tatras. Ascent from Zawoja (~3 hours) or Kojszówka.
  • Jałowiec (1,111 m) — a peak in the central part of the Żywiec Beskids, with a panorama of nearby ranges. Less known but worth attention.
  • Hala Krupowa — a pastoral meadow on the slopes of Jałowiec, with a student shelter. Good base and resting place.
  • Shelter at Hala Krupowa — one of the most interesting shelters in the Żywiec Beskids, known for its atmosphere and home cooking.

Wielka Racza and Rycerzowa range

  • Wielka Racza (1,236 m) — the highest peak of the Wielka Racza range, on the Polish-Slovak border. Views over the Tatras, Lesser Fatra and Silesian Beskids. Ascent from Rycerka Dolna (~3.5 hours).
  • Wielka Rycerzowa (1,226 m) — a peak with a distinctive shelter on top. Worth it for the views and shelter atmosphere.
  • Wielka Racza Reserve — a reserve protecting fragments of old beech-fir forests on the slopes of Wielka Racza.
  • Rycerka Dolna and Górna — villages at the foot of Wielka Racza, with accommodation base and trailheads.

Culture and tradition

  • Żywiec Ethnographic Park in Ślemień — an open-air museum presenting the traditional culture of the Żywiec highlanders. Wooden cottages, tools, folk costumes.
  • Regional tavern in Korbielów — worth trying local dishes: kwaśnica, oscypek, grilled trout.
  • Shrines and roadside crosses — there are many in the Żywiec Beskids, often in picturesque spots. Pay attention on your hikes.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Meadows bloom, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk among the meadows.
  • July and August — peak pastoral season. Sheep graze on the meadows, you can see shepherds at work. More tourists, but still fewer than the Tatras.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people. September is red deer rutting season.
  • November–March — winter in the Żywiec Beskids is harsh but beautiful. Shelters are open, but trails require proper equipment. For experienced hikers.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Kraków (via Rabka and Zawoja), ~1.5 hours from Katowice, ~1 hour from Bielsko-Biała.
  • By train: stations in Żywiec and Sucha Beskidzka, with connections from Kraków (~2.5 hours) and Katowice (~2 hours). Then bus or taxi.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Kraków, Katowice and Bielsko-Biała to Korbielów, Zawoja and other villages in the region.

Public transport in the Żywiec Beskids is sparse — a car or bike makes moving between trailheads much easier.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one day for Pilsko or Wielka Racza, one for a walk through the meadows.

Three to four days is realistic for a first visit. Pilsko, the Polica range, Wielka Racza, a walk through the meadows, a visit to the open-air museum in Ślemień.

A week lets you explore the entire Żywiec Beskids — all ranges, the most interesting shelters, pastoral tradition and surrounding towns (Żywiec, Zawoja, Korbielów).

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Grilled oscypek — smoked sheep cheese with cranberry, available at shepherd huts on the meadows.
  • Kwaśnica — highland sour cabbage soup with meat. A Beskid cuisine classic.
  • Moskole — pancakes of flour and water with additions, served with bryndza or cracklings.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms, available in regional taverns.
  • Karpielówka and śliwowica — local liqueurs, available in regional taverns.
  • Beer from the Żywiec Brewery — one of the oldest breweries in Poland (since 1856), brewing in traditional styles.

Accommodation: Korbielów (close to Pilsko), Zawoja (close to Babia Góra and Polica), Rycerka Dolna (close to Wielka Racza), Żywiec (larger town, more options), shelters on the meadows (for those who want to sleep in the mountains), agritourism farms in surrounding villages. Book ahead in season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — book accommodation at meadow shelters in advance, especially on summer weekends.
  • Weather — the Żywiec Beskids are lower than the Tatras, but the weather changes quickly. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Shoes — sturdy trekking boots are essential on longer trails. For short walks through the meadows, comfortable sports shoes are enough.
  • Water — there are springs and streams on the trails, but not all are drinkable. Bring a supply of water.
  • Car parks — paid car parks in Korbielów, Zawoja and at trailheads. In season they fill by 8 a.m.
  • Signal — good in Korbielów and Zawoja, weaker on trails. Have an offline map.
  • Slovak border — some trails cross the border. Carry an identity document.

Trip plan

  1. Morning walk along the slopes
  2. Vistas from high mountain ridges
  3. Evening savoring regional highland cuisine

Tip

Take a hike on a weekday. The lack of crowds makes the Żywiec Beskids a true sanctuary of silence where you can fully regenerate your strength.
