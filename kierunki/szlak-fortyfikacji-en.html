ALL OF POLAND

Fortification Trail — Fortresses, Bunkers, Shelters

5–8 days
Home›Themed journeys›Fortification Trail

A route through the greatest military works of Poland — from Przemyśl Fortress to the bunkers of the Pomeranian Wall. Military history at a slow pace. Slow travel along the Fortification Trail.

Why the Fortification Trail, differently

Poland has hundreds of fortresses, forts, bunkers, and shelters — remnants of centuries of armed conflict. The Fortification Trail is a proposal for those who want to see the most interesting of them — from medieval defensive castles to 20th-century bunkers. Many of these structures are open to tourists and offer a fascinating journey into military history.

Slow travel along the Fortification Trail is a celebration of details of defensive architecture. Instead of rushing from fort to fort, stop in one longer — tour with a guide, understand how the structure worked, listen to the stories. Each fortress has its own history, its own secrets, its own dramas. Ask, learn, try to understand. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly understand about military history.

What to see

The Fortification Trail splits into several sections: Przemyśl Fortress (largest 19th-century fortress in Europe), Kłodzko Fortress (largest fortress in the Sudetes), Owl Mountains bunkers (mysterious underground), Międzyrzecz Fortified Region (largest system of underground tunnels in Poland), Pomeranian Wall (bunkers on the Baltic), Modlin Fortress (one of the largest in Poland). Riding the whole route takes 5–8 days.

Przemyśl Fortress

  • Przemyśl Fortress — one of the largest 19th-century fortresses in Europe, built by Austria-Hungary. A system of forts and fortifications surrounding the city. Worth it for history and architecture.
  • Fort XIII San Rideau — one of the best-preserved forts of the fortress, open to tourists. Worth visiting with a guide.
  • Fort XV Borek — a fort near the city centre, with an exhibition about the fortress's history. Worth it for understanding the context.
  • Przemyśl Fortress Museum — an exhibition about the history of the fortress and region. Worth it for historical context.

Kłodzko Fortress and Owl Mountains bunkers

  • Kłodzko Fortress — one of the largest and best-preserved fortresses in Poland. It rises on a hill above the town. Worth it for architecture and views.
  • Underground tourist route in Kłodzko — a route through a labyrinth of underground passages and chambers, ~600 m long. Worth it for unusual experiences.
  • Owl Mountains bunkers — mysterious underground passages from World War II, partly open to tourists. Worth it for history.
  • Grodno Castle — ruins of a castle in Zagórze Śląskie, one of the more interesting defensive structures in the region. Worth it for architecture.

Międzyrzecz Fortified Region and Pomeranian Wall

  • Międzyrzecz Fortified Region — the largest system of underground tunnels in Poland, built by Germany in the 1930s. Bats hibernate in the tunnels. Worth visiting with a guide.
  • Pomeranian Wall bunkers — a system of fortifications from World War II, in northwestern Poland. Worth it for history.
  • Gerhard's Fort in Świnoujście — a Prussian fort from the 19th century, today a museum. Worth it for architecture and history.
  • Modlin Fortress — one of the largest fortresses in Poland, built by Napoleon and expanded by the Russians. Worth it for architecture.
  • Warsaw Fortress — remnants of forts around Warsaw, from the 19th century. Worth it for history.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk around the forts.
  • July–September — peak season. Forts crowded, but all attractions open. Worth it for cultural events.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people. Worth a walk around the fortifications.
  • November–March — forts in winter are less crowded, but the underground may be closed (due to humidity). Check opening hours before your trip.

Getting there

The Fortification Trail runs through the whole country — you can start anywhere. Best to travel by car or train, connecting several forts in one trip.

  • By train: Przemyśl, Kłodzko, Modlin, Świnoujście have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: the trail runs through main national roads. Plan the route to connect several forts.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one fortification complex (e.g. Przemyśl Fortress, Kłodzko Fortress or Międzyrzecz Fortified Region).

A week is realistic for a first visit. You can see 3–4 fortification complexes in different regions.

Two weeks is time for the whole trail — all the most important fortifications of Poland. This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

Three weeks — if you want to travel truly slow, with numerous guided tours and rest.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Regional cuisine — in each region you can try local dishes: duck with apples (Lower Silesia), pierogi (Lesser Poland), kartacze (Podlasie).
  • Pierogi — at every regional inn, with various fillings.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in each region.
  • Coffee and cake — every larger town on the route has several good cafés.
  • Local products — cheeses, honey, cold cuts, available at agritourism farms and regional shops.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), campsites (for those who want to sleep in a tent), PTTK mountain huts (on mountain sections).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the history of fortifications is complex, and without context many forts look similar. Worth going with a guide.
  • Opening hours — many forts are open only in season (May–October) and only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Temperature — the underground has a constant temperature (~10°C). Dress warmly, even in summer.
  • Shoes — sturdy shoes are essential on underground routes (uneven surfaces, wet stones).
  • Bookings — visiting some forts requires booking (especially in season). Check before your trip.
  • Weather — Polish weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Przemyśl Fortress and Fort Salis Soglio
  2. Kłodzko Fortress and Owl Mountains bunkers
  3. Pomeranian Wall and Międzyrzecz Fortified Region

Tip

Visit the Międzyrzecz Fortified Region with a guide — it is the largest system of underground tunnels in Poland, where bats hibernate. Book the tour in advance.
