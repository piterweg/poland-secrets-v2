POMERANIA

Amber Trail — In the Footsteps of Baltic Amber

4–6 days
Home›Pomerania›Amber Trail

A route through amber workshops and amber-related sites — from Gdańsk to Kashubia. History, craft, and workshops. Slow travel along the Amber Trail.

Why the Amber Trail, differently

Baltic amber is one of the most characteristic treasures of Poland — and one of the most recognizable craft materials in the world. Extracted on the Baltic coast for centuries, today it is the basis of local craft that attracts tourists from all over the world. The Amber Trail is a route through the most important amber-related sites — from Gdańsk, through Kashubia, to the Baltic coast.

Slow travel along the Amber Trail is a celebration of craft and history. Instead of rushing from workshop to workshop, stop in one longer — talk to the craftsman, see the amber processing, try it yourself. Each workshop has its own history, its own techniques, its own secrets. Ask, learn, try. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly understand about amber.

What to see

The Amber Trail splits into several sections: Gdańsk (the centre of Polish amber craft), Kashubia (workshops and former mines), the Baltic coast (beaches where you can find amber), the surroundings of Elbląg (historic amber routes). Riding the whole route takes 4–6 days.

Gdańsk — centre of amber craft

  • Amber Museum in Gdańsk — one of the largest amber museums in the world, with a collection of specimens from various eras. Worth it for understanding amber history.
  • Mariacka Street in Gdańsk — a street with numerous amber workshops. Worth it for atmosphere and shopping.
  • Amber workshops in Gdańsk — several dozen workshops offering amber products, courses and demonstrations. Worth it for craft.
  • Amber fairs in Gdańsk — held regularly, especially in summer. Worth it for shopping and comparing prices.
  • Granary Island — a former warehouse district, today with workshops and amber shops. Worth a walk.

Kashubia — workshops and mines

  • Amber workshops in Kashubia — in Żukowo, Kartuzy, Kościerzyna and other towns. Worth it for craft.
  • Former amber mines — remnants of old amber mines in the region. Worth it for history.
  • Open-air museum in Wdzydze Kiszewskie — an ethnographic museum with an exhibition about amber and Kashubian craft. Worth it for context.
  • Kashubian Ethnographic Park — a museum with an exhibition about Kashubian culture, including amber. Worth it for context.
  • Amber workshops in Kashubia — many workshops offer courses for tourists (grinding, polishing, setting amber). Worth it for the experience.

Baltic coast and surroundings of Elbląg

  • Baltic beaches — you can find amber on the beaches, especially after a storm. Worth it for the experience.
  • Vistula Spit — the region with the most amber. Worth it for walks and searching.
  • Stegna and Jantar — villages on the Vistula Spit, famous for amber. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Elbląg — a city with a history connected to amber, with an Amber Museum. Worth it for history.
  • Frombork — the town of Copernicus, on the route of the old amber trail. Worth it for history.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk on the beach.
  • September and October — second best. Warm sea, fewer people, storms after which you can find amber.
  • July and August — peak season. Workshops and fairs crowded, prices higher, but all attractions open. Worth it for cultural events.
  • November–March — season for storms, after which you can find amber on the beaches. Worth it for searching.

Getting there

The Amber Trail runs through northern Poland — from Gdańsk to Elbląg. Best to travel by car or train, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Gdańsk, Elbląg, Frombork have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: the trail runs through main national roads (S7, DK22). Plan the route to connect several towns.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Gdańsk (Amber Museum, workshops, fairs) and surroundings.

Three to four days is realistic for a first visit. Gdańsk, Kashubia (workshops, courses), Baltic coast (amber hunting).

A week lets you explore the whole trail — Gdańsk, Kashubia, coast, Elbląg, Frombork. You can also add amber workshops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pomeranian cuisine — Baltic fish, herring, eel. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Kashubian cuisine — ruchanki, kiszka z kaszy i krwi, Kashubian broth. Worth trying in local restaurants.
  • Buckwheat pierogi — a variant popular in Pomerania, less known in the rest of Poland.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region (Browar PG4, Browar Puck).
  • Coffee and cake — Gdańsk, Sopot and Gdynia have several good cafés.
  • Local products — cheeses, honey, cold cuts, available in regional shops.

Accommodation: Gdańsk (largest choice, but expensive), Sopot (resort, more options), Gdynia (town, more options), Kashubia (agritourism farms, quiet), Vistula Spit (guesthouses, close to the beach). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Amber — prices vary widely. Genuine Baltic amber starts at several dozen złoty for a small piece. If the price is suspiciously low — it is probably fake.
  • Workshops — buy directly from workshops, not from stalls. In workshops you can also see the processing and talk to the craftsman.
  • Courses — many workshops offer courses (grinding, polishing, setting amber). Book in advance.
  • Amber hunting — the best time is the first hours after a storm, especially on the Vistula Spit and around Ustka. It takes patience, but beautiful pieces do turn up.
  • Opening hours — many workshops are open only in season (May–September) and only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Weather — coastal weather changes fast. Even in July, bring a windproof jacket and hat.
  • Transport — Polish trains have bike carriages. Use them if you want to transport bikes between stages.

Trip plan

  1. Amber workshops in Gdańsk
  2. Amber Museum and fairs in Gdynia
  3. Amber workshops in Kashubia

Tip

Sign up for an amber workshop at one of the studios in Gdańsk or Gdynia — the best way to understand how amber jewelry is made.
