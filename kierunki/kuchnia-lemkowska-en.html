Lemko Region — Proziaki, Fuczki and Lemko Kwaśnica

2 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Lemko Region

Lemko cuisine is simple dishes made of flour, potatoes, and cabbage. The flavors of an old village, coming back into fashion. Slow travel through Lemko cuisine.

Why Lemko cuisine, differently

Lemko cuisine is the cuisine of old Beskid villages — simple, based on flour, potatoes, cabbage, and milk. Dishes once eaten in every Lemko cottage are now returning to fashion — especially in the Low Beskids and Sądecki Beskids, where the culinary traditions of ancestors are cultivated. The Lemkos are an ethnic group inhabiting the Low and Sądecki Beskids, who over the centuries developed their own, distinct culinary culture.

Slow travel through Lemko cuisine is a celebration of taste and tradition. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a village inn, in an agritourism farm, in a small restaurant in the Low Beskids. Each dish has its own story, its own cook, its own village. Ask where the ingredients come from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Lemko cuisine splits into several categories: flour dishes (proziaki, hreczanyki, pierogi), cabbage dishes (kwaśnica, fuczki), potato dishes (pancakes, dumplings), meat dishes (duck, lamb), baked goods (kołocz). Exploring the culinary region of the Lemko Region takes 1–2 days.

Flour dishes

  • Proziaki — the simplest of Lemko flatbreads — made of flour, sour milk, and baking soda, baked on a sheet, served with garlic butter or lard. A classic of Lemko cuisine, available at inns in the Low Beskids.
  • Hreczanyki — buckwheat groat pancakes with curd cheese and eggs, fried in a pan. Characteristic of Lemko cuisine — especially in the version with mushroom sauce.
  • Pierogi with bryndza — delicate pierogi with sheep cheese, a specialty of the Polish-Ukrainian borderland. Served with cracklings or sour cream.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a traditional Lenten dish, served at Christmas Eve. In the Lemko version — with marjoram and pepper.

Cabbage and potato dishes

  • Lemko kwaśnica — soup based on sauerkraut, with potatoes and mushrooms, cooked long over low heat. On winter days it warms from the first sip.
  • Fuczki — cabbage fritters, fried in a pan, also known in a potato version. A Lemko dish, available in small bars in the Lemko Region.
  • Potato pancakes — in the Lemko version with marjoram and bacon. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Lemko dumplings — dumplings made of flour and water, served with mushroom or meat sauce.

Meat dishes and baked goods

  • Duck with apples — roast duck with apples and marjoram, a Lemko classic. Available in elegant restaurants in the Low Beskids.
  • Lamb — roast or stewed lamb, a classic of shepherd cuisine. Served with potatoes and cabbage.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms in the Low Beskids, available at taverns.
  • Lemko kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Baked for weddings and family celebrations.
  • Herbal liqueurs — local liqueurs from mountain herbs, available at inns.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal produce, long days. Worth a walk through the villages.
  • July–September — season for fresh vegetables, fruit, mushrooms. Worth it for markets and culinary festivals.
  • September–October — season for mushrooms, pumpkins, plums. September and October are good times for mushroom picking in the Low Beskids.
  • November–March — season for kwaśnica, bigos, krupnik. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Lemko cuisine is from the southeastern region of Poland — best to travel by car, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Nowy Sącz, Gorlice, Krosno have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~2 hours from Kraków (DK75), ~2 hours from Rzeszów (DK28), ~1 hour from Nowy Sącz.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (1–2 days) is the minimum — Nowy Sącz or Gorlice (Lemko restaurants, proziaki, fuczki) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Nowy Sącz, Gorlice, Krynica-Zdrój, Hańczowa.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of the Lemko Region — all the most important dishes and places, and neighbouring Bieszczady.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose village inns and agritourism farms. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Proziaki — a Lemko classic, available at inns in the Low Beskids.
  • Fuczki — cabbage fritters, available in small bars in the Lemko Region.
  • Lemko kwaśnica — sauerkraut soup, available at every inn.
  • Hreczanyki — buckwheat groat pancakes, available at agritourism farms.
  • Lemko kołocz — sweet yeast cake, available at pastry shops in Nowy Sącz.
  • Herbal liqueurs — local liqueurs, available at inns in the Low Beskids.

Accommodation: Nowy Sącz (town, more options), Gorlice (town, more options), Krynica-Zdrój (spa, more options), Hańczowa (village, quiet), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Village inns — usually open from 12 p.m. to 9 p.m., longer on weekends. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants in city centres.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (baking proziaki, making fuczki, baking bread). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Lemko cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Markets — the best place to buy local products (cheeses, honey, cold cuts, mushrooms).
  • Weather — weather in the Low Beskids can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Proziaki with garlic butter
  2. Fuczki with cabbage in a Beskid inn
  3. Lemko kwaśnica on a cold day

Tip

Order proziaki with garlic butter at one of the Lemko homesteads in the Low Beskids — preferably the version with curd cheese or bryndza.
