PODLASIE AND THE CARPATHIANS

Orthodox Churches Trail — Wooden and Brick Orthodox Churches

5–7 days
Home›Podlasie›Orthodox Churches Trail

A route through the most beautiful Orthodox and Greek Catholic churches — from Podlasie to the Bieszczady. Iconostases, frescoes, and multicultural heritage. Slow travel along the Orthodox Churches Trail.

Why the Orthodox Churches Trail, differently

For centuries Poland was a multicultural country — alongside Catholics lived Orthodox Christians, Greek Catholics, Jews, and Tatars. Orthodox and Greek Catholic churches are today one of the most valuable testimonies of this multiculturalism, especially in the eastern and south-eastern regions. The Orthodox Churches Trail is a route through the most beautiful sites of this type — from Podlasie to the Bieszczady, from the Chełm region to the Lemko Region.

Slow travel along the Orthodox Churches Trail is a journey through history, art, and spirituality. The interiors of Orthodox churches delight with iconostases — walls of icons separating the nave from the sanctuary. Some iconostases are several hundred years old and are true masterpieces of sacred art. Frescoes, polychromes, old icons — all this creates a space where you can truly feel the sacred. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: symbols, colors, influences of different cultures, the history of people who built these churches.

What to see

The Orthodox Churches Trail splits into several sections: Podlasie (from Supraśl to Białowieża), Chełm-Lublin (from Chełm to Włodawa), Lemko (from Krynica to the Bieszczady), Bieszczady (from Sanok to Ustrzyki). Riding the whole route takes 5–7 days.

Podlasie

  • Monastery in Supraśl — one of the most important Orthodox monasteries in Poland, from the 16th century. Worth it for architecture and art.
  • Church in Puchły — a wooden church from the 19th century, with a unique iconostasis. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Trześcianka — a wooden church from the 19th century, with a unique interior. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Sołki — a wooden church from the 19th century, less known. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Monastery in Zwierki — a women's Orthodox monastery, ~20 km from Białystok. Worth it for the sacred.

Chełm region and Lublin region

  • Church in Hrubieszów — a Baroque church from the 18th century, one of the town's symbols. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Włodawa — a Baroque church from the 18th century, one of the town's symbols. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Chełm — a Baroque church from the 18th century, with a unique interior. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Hola — a wooden church from the 18th century, on the edge of Polesie. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Uhrusk — a wooden church from the 18th century, on the edge of Polesie. Worth it for architecture.

Lemko Region and Bieszczady

  • Church in Kwiatoń — a Lemko church from the 17th century, inscribed on the UNESCO list. Worth it for architecture and art.
  • Church in Owczary — a Lemko church from the 17th century, inscribed on the UNESCO list. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Bartne — a Lemko church from the 19th century, in the Low Beskids. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Smolnik — a Lemko church from the 18th century, in the Bieszczady. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Turzańsk — a Lemko church from the 19th century, inscribed on the UNESCO list. Worth it for architecture.
  • Church in Radoszyce — a Lemko church from the 19th century, in the Bieszczady. Worth it for architecture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk through the villages.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. Sites crowded, car parks full. If you must — pick a weekday and come in the morning.
  • November–March — sites in winter are less accessible (some closed). Worth it for those who want to see the churches without crowds, but check opening hours.
  • Orthodox holidays — during holidays the churches are full of worshippers. Worth it for a spiritual experience, but the crowds are larger.

Getting there

  • By car: the trail runs through many Polish regions. Plan the route so that it connects several sites in one day.
  • By train: many sites are close to railway stations (Białystok, Chełm, Sanok). You can arrive by train and rent a bike or car on site.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

The Orthodox Churches Trail is best explored by car or bike — the sites are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 sites in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — 5–6 sites in one region (e.g. Podlasie or the Lemko Region).

A week is realistic for a first visit. You can see 15–20 sites, covering one or two regions.

Two weeks is time for the whole trail — 30–40 sites across all Polish regions. This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

Three weeks — if you want to ride truly slow, with numerous stops, sightseeing of villages, museums and nearby attractions.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Regional cuisine — in each region you can try local dishes: pierogi with bryndza (Subcarpathia), proziaki (Lesser Poland), kibiny (Podlasie), kartacze (Podlasie).
  • Sheep cheeses — oscypek, bryndza, bundz, available at shepherd huts in mountain regions.
  • Fish — grilled trout in mountain and foothill regions.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — every larger town on the route has several good cafés.
  • Local products — cheeses, honey, cold cuts, available at agritourism farms and regional shops.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), campsites (for those who want to sleep in a tent), monasteries and retreat houses (for those seeking a unique place — especially near monasteries). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Car — the Orthodox Churches Trail is best explored by car. Sites are scattered over a large area, and public transport can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 sites in one day.
  • Bike — a bike will also work, but only in selected regions (e.g. Podlasie, Lemko Region). Cycling routes around the sites are well-marked.
  • Opening hours — many sites are open only in season (May–October) and only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Services — during services, keep quiet and do not photograph. Come to a service to hear the liturgical chants.
  • Clothing — modest dress is required in the church. Have a scarf or shawl for shoulders.
  • Guided tours — the history of the churches is complex, and without context many sites look similar. Take a guide at selected places.
  • Weather — Polish weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Podlasie churches in Supraśl and Puchły
  2. Lemko churches in Kwiatoń and Bartne
  3. Supraśl Monastery and Grabarka Sanctuary

Tip

Visit Orthodox churches during services — that is when you can hear the liturgical chants that are an integral part of the Orthodox tradition.
