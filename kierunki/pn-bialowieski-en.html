PODLASIE

Białowieża National Park — Europe's Last Primeval Forest

2 days
Home›Podlasie›Białowieża National Park

The oldest national park in Poland. Bison, oaks over 400 years old, and a strict reserve where nature rules by its own laws. Slow travel through Białowieża National Park.

Why Białowieża National Park, differently

Białowieża National Park is the oldest national park in Poland — established in 1932, but protected since the 15th century as a royal forest. It protects a fragment of primeval lowland forest that has never been cut down by humans. It is the last such area in Europe — which is why it is inscribed on the UNESCO list.

Slow travel through Białowieża is a journey to times when state borders did not exist and the only law was the cycle of life and death. A walk through the strict reserve with a licensed guide is a lesson in humility. When you look at fallen trunks that become home to thousands of organisms, you can understand that death in this forest is only the beginning of new life. This is a place that demands time and attention — it cannot be visited in haste.

What to see

The park splits into several zones: the strict reserve (entry only with a guide), the Orłówka Protective District (with bison), areas around Białowieża (educational trails), and hiking and cycling trails. Two to three days are best to visit.

Strict reserve

  • Strict reserve — the heart of the park, an area of ~47 km², entry only with a licensed guide. The route has several variants (2–6 km), and guides talk about the ecosystem that functions without human intervention.
  • Wolf Trail — a longer route through the reserve, leading through the wildest fragments of the forest. Requires fitness but gives a chance to encounter bison, deer or boar in their natural habitat.
  • Royal Oaks — a group of old oaks up to 400 years old, growing near Białowieża. Worth seeing these monumental trees that remember the times of kings.

Bison and animals

  • Bison Breeding Centre — a place where you can see bison up close. The bison breeding and reintroduction programme was launched here, saving the species from extinction. Worth it for understanding the history of this animal's protection.
  • Bison Show Reserve — a demonstration reserve where bison live, along with other animals (Polish Koniks, wolves, lynxes). Paid entry, but worth it — especially for families with children.
  • Wildlife observation — early in the morning or at dusk, with a licensed guide, you can see bison, deer, boar and roe deer in their natural habitat. Requires patience and silence.

Trails and paths

  • Bison Trail — a walking route through the forest, ~4 km long, leading through different forest types. Good for a first encounter with the park.
  • Royal Oaks Path — a short route (~2.5 km), leading through forest with monumental oaks. Ideal for a walk with children.
  • Cycling route — around the park and through the Białowieża Forest you can cycle. Rentals in Białowieża. Routes are well-marked.
  • Bison's Rib Path — an educational path around Białowieża, leading through various habitats. Good for understanding the park's ecosystem.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time. Birds in full song, forest green, fewer crowds. Visit in the morning.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer tourists. Red deer rutting season in September.
  • July and August — peak season. Trails crowded, but all attractions open and bison can be seen in full glory.
  • November and December — the forest is quiet and still. Bring warm clothes — humidity is high.
  • Winter — the forest under snow looks fairy-tale. Trails are accessible, but require warm clothing and sturdy boots.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Białystok (route 19), ~4 hours from Warsaw (S8 and route 19), ~3 hours from Lublin.
  • By train: nearest station is Hajnówka (~20 km from Białowieża). Then bus or taxi.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Białystok, Hajnówka and other cities in the region.

Within Białowieża you can walk or cycle — the village is small and the main attractions are within walking distance.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one day for the strict reserve, one for bison and trails around Białowieża.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Reserve, wildlife observation at dawn, trails, cycling, and a quiet walk around Białowieża.

A week lets you explore the entire Białowieża Forest — Polish and Belarusian sides (visa required) — and the surroundings (Hajnówka, Białystok).

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Podlasie cuisine — kibiny, kartacze, potato babka. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Berry pierogi — seasonally, in restaurants in Białowieża.
  • Mushrooms — in season (August–October) local restaurants offer dishes with mushrooms collected in the forest.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries operate in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Białowieża has several cafés, ideal for an afternoon break.

Accommodation: Białowieża (several hotels, guesthouses and agritourism farms), Hajnówka (cheaper, but further from the park), surrounding villages (agritourism, quiet, close to nature). Book ahead in season — especially on May and holiday weekends.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — visiting the strict reserve requires booking a guide in advance (Białowieża NP, bpn.com.pl). Spots sell out quickly in season.
  • Tickets — entry to the strict reserve, bison enclosure and other attractions is paid. Buy tickets online or at ticket offices.
  • Guide — the strict reserve can only be visited with a licensed guide. This is not optional — it is a park requirement.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — the forest is damp, mosquitoes and ticks are active from May to October. Repellent, long trousers and socks — essential.
  • Clothing — humidity in the forest is high, even summer can be cool. Rain jacket and sturdy shoes — essential.
  • Signal — good in Białowieża, fades deep in the forest.
  • Fuel and shopping — petrol stations in Hajnówka and Białowieża. Larger shopping in Białystok.

Trip plan

  1. Strict Reserve with a guide
  2. European Bison Breeding Centre
  3. Bison Trail in Białowieża

Tip

Go to the strict reserve with a guide in the early morning. The chance of encountering bison is highest then, and the forest looks most magical.
