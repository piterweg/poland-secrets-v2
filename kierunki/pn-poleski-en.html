LUBLIN REGION

Polesie National Park — Land of Marshes and Cranes

1–2 days
Home›Lublin Region›Polesie National Park

Polesie National Park is a land of marshes, peat bogs, and cranes. A landscape straight out of Polesie. Slow travel through the Lublin region.

Why it's worth it

Polesie National Park was established in 1990 to protect the Polesie marshes — one of the most valuable wetland areas in Poland. It lies in the Lublin Polesie, on the border with Ukraine and Belarus. The park's landscape is a mosaic of peat bogs, marshes, meadows, forests, and karst lakes. This is a land that looks completely different from the rest of Poland — flat, humid, dominated by water. Cranes, herons, beavers, and otters live in the park, along with numerous species of wetland birds. Add to that endemic plant species, such as the bog willow.

Slow travel through Polesie National Park is a journey through a landscape that requires patience. The park has no peaks or waterfalls — there are marshes, peat bogs, and birds. It is worth spending 1–2 days here to truly feel this landscape. The best way to explore the park is on foot or by bike — there are about 60 km of hiking and cycling trails, including nature paths. Public transport is weak, so rent a car.

What to see

Polesie National Park is divided into three main parts: marshes in the centre, forests in the northern part, and karst lakes in the southern part. Exploring the park takes 1–2 days.

Marshes and peat bogs

  • Bubnów marsh — the most valuable ecosystem in the park, a refuge for wetland birds. Worth it for nature.
  • “Spławy” trail — a nature trail through the marshes, with a viewing tower. Worth it for views.
  • “Dąb Dominik” trail — a trail around a karst lake. Worth it for nature.

Northern forests

  • Polesie forests — a mosaic of pine forests, oak-hornbeam forests, and alder carrs. Worth it for mushroom picking.
  • Wola Uhruska village — a village with a wooden church. Worth it for architecture.
  • Sobibór village — a village with a museum of the former extermination camp. Worth it for history.

Karst lakes

  • Lake Moszne — a karst lake in the middle of the forest. Worth it for silence.
  • Lake Długie — a karst lake with crystal-clear water. Worth it for swimming.
  • Lake Karaśne — a karst lake with rare plant species. Worth it for nature.

When to go

  • April–May — the best time for birds. Spring floods, birds at peak nesting. Worth it for nature.
  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — warm, but fewer birds (post-breeding period).
  • September–October — second best. Autumn bird migrations, autumn colours.
  • November–March — the park is accessible in winter, but less interesting for nature. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Lublin (DK12), ~40 minutes from Chełm, ~3 hours from Warsaw (S17 + DK12).
  • By train: Chełm, Włodawa have railway stations. Then rent a car.
  • By bus: PKS serves most larger villages, but connections are sparse.

Polesie National Park is best explored by car or bike — the park is vast, and public transport is weak.

How much time you need

Half a day is the minimum — the “Spławy” trail and a viewpoint.

A weekend (2 days) is realistic for a first visit. “Spławy” trail, “Dąb Dominik” trail, karst lakes.

Three days lets you explore the whole park and surroundings — from Wola Uhruska to Sobibór.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pierogi with potatoes — a Lublin classic, available at inns.
  • Potato kiszka — a Lublin speciality, available at inns.
  • Trout from the lakes — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Goat cheeses — from local farms, available in village shops.
  • Mead — from Lublin apiaries.

Accommodation: Chełm (town, more options), Włodawa (small town, guesthouses), Wola Uhruska (village, agritourism), numerous agritourism farms in villages.

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the park has rich birdlife, take a guide.
  • Binoculars — essential equipment. Without binoculars you miss half the experience.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Opening hours — trails are open year-round, but sections may be closed during the breeding season.
  • Weather — Polesie weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — weak in many parts of the park. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Dąb Dominik educational trail
  2. Observation tower over the lake
  3. Peat bog reserve

Tip

Go to the “Spławy” trail early in the morning — the best time to hear and see cranes on the marshes.
