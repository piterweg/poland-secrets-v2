POMERANIA

Velo Baltica — Baltic Coastal Cycling Route

5–7 days
Home›Pomerania›Velo Baltica

Along the Baltic coast, through seaside towns and Słowiński National Park. Slow travel along Velo Baltica — the most beautiful cycling route on the Polish coast.

Why Velo Baltica, differently

Velo Baltica is a cycling route that runs along the Baltic coast — through the most beautiful seaside towns, lighthouses, cliffs and beaches. The route lets you discover the Polish coast from a completely different perspective than by car or train. It is part of the European R10 route, which circles the entire Baltic.

Slow travel along Velo Baltica is above all about the rhythm of the sea. Stop at every beach, climb every lighthouse, eat fresh fish at every seaside fish shop. The sea teaches patience and lets you notice things that escape in everyday haste. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are on the route, the more details you notice: dunes, cliffs, birds, fishing harbours, people who live from the sea. Velo Baltica is the best way to truly get to know the Polish coast.

What to see

Velo Baltica splits into several sections: western (from Świnoujście), central (from Kołobrzeg to Łeba), eastern (from Łeba to Gdańsk). Riding the whole route takes 5–7 days.

Western Coast

  • Świnoujście — a city on 44 islands, with Poland's tallest lighthouse (68 m). Worth it for views and atmosphere.
  • Międzyzdroje — a resort with a promenade, pier and Wolin NP nearby. Worth a walk.
  • Wolin National Park — a park with cliffs, bison enclosure and a viewpoint over the Szczecin Lagoon. Worth it for nature.
  • Dziwnów — a small town on the Dziwna strait, with a harbour and beach. Worth a walk.
  • Kamień Pomorski — a town with a cathedral and market square, ~10 km from the coast. Worth it for architecture.

Central Coast

  • Kołobrzeg — a large spa resort with a lighthouse and harbour. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Darłowo — a small town with a Pomeranian dukes' castle and a fishing port. Worth it for history and architecture.
  • Ustka — a town with a lighthouse and fishing harbour. Worth a walk along the promenade.
  • Łeba — a small town with the shifting dunes of Słowiński NP. Worth it for the dunes and walks.
  • Słowiński National Park — a park with shifting dunes, coastal lakes and coastal pine forests. Worth it for nature.

Eastern Coast

  • Hel — a town at the tip of the Hel Peninsula, with a seal sanctuary and fishing harbour. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Jastarnia, Jurata, Kuźnica — towns on the Hel Peninsula, with beaches and harbours. Worth it for rest.
  • Władysławowo — a town at the entrance to the Hel Peninsula. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Gdynia — a port city, with Kościuszko Square and Kamienna Góra. Worth it for architecture.
  • Sopot — a resort with the longest wooden pier in Europe (511 m). Worth a walk.
  • Gdańsk — a city with a Hanseatic Old Town (UNESCO). Worth it for history and architecture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth cycling.
  • September — second best. Warm sea, fewer people, autumn colours over the coast.
  • July and August — peak season. Route crowded, prices higher, but all attractions open. Worth it for swimming in the sea.
  • October — end of the season. Still beautiful autumn colours, but the weather can be capricious. Worth it for those with a flexible plan.
  • November–April — the route in winter is less accessible (snow, ice). Worth it for experienced cyclists.

Getting there

  • By train: Świnoujście, Kołobrzeg, Darłowo, Ustka, Łeba, Gdynia, Sopot and Gdańsk have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities. You can reach any point on the route by train.
  • By car: from Gdańsk to Świnoujście ~6 hours (S6 and DK3), from Warsaw ~5 hours (S7). Plan the route so that the start and end of the stage are near railway stations.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

Velo Baltica is well connected to the rest of Poland — you can start anywhere, arrive by train, ride a chosen stage and return by train.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one stage of 50–70 km per day, around a chosen town (e.g. Gdańsk, Kołobrzeg, Łeba).

A week is realistic for a first visit. You can ride 300–400 km, completing one region (e.g. Central or Eastern Coast).

Two weeks is time for the whole coast — from Świnoujście to Gdańsk (through all seaside towns).

Three weeks — if you want to ride truly slow, with numerous stops, sightseeing and rest.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Baltic fish — herring, flounder, cod, smoked salmon. Best in small fish shops at the harbour.
  • Herring in oil — a coastal cuisine classic, available at every fish shop.
  • Smoked eel — a regional delicacy, available at smokehouses.
  • Kashubian cuisine — in the Gdańsk region, try ruchanki, kiszka z kaszy i krwi, goose soup.
  • Buckwheat pierogi — a variant popular in Pomerania, less known in the rest of Poland.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region (Browar Ustka, Browar Puck, Browar PG4).

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), campsites (for those who want to sleep in a tent), hostels (for those seeking cheaper options). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season (July–August).

Practical tips

  • Bike — a trekking or gravel bike is best for Velo Baltica. The route runs mainly on asphalt and gravel roads. A mountain bike will also work but will be slower on asphalt.
  • Bookings — book accommodation on the route in advance, especially in summer season (July–August). In May and September it is easier.
  • Signage — the route is well marked (Velo Baltica logo, information boards). GPS navigation is not necessary, but have an app with the route.
  • Weather — coastal weather changes fast. Even in July, bring a windproof jacket and hat. Summer storms happen but are spectacular.
  • Crowds — in summer season the route is crowded. Ride early in the morning or on weekdays.
  • Transport — Polish trains have bike carriages. Use them if you want to transport bikes between stages. The SKM in the Tricity is particularly bike-friendly.
  • Bike service — every larger town on the route has bike workshops.

Trip plan

  1. Świnoujście — Kołobrzeg
  2. Darłowo — Łeba
  3. Finale in Hel

Tip

Plan the route so that you spend one day in Słowiński National Park. The shifting dunes are an experience you will not forget — give yourself time to explore at leisure.
