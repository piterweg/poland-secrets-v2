LESSER POLAND

Podhale — Oscypek, Kwaśnica and Bryjka

2–3 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Podhale

Shepherd cheeses, sauerkraut, and highland soups. Cuisine straight from the shepherd's hut — thick and warming. Slow travel through Podhale cuisine.

Why Podhale cuisine, differently

Podhale cuisine is the cuisine of shepherds — thick, hearty, based on milk, cheese, cabbage, and potatoes. All these are dishes that were meant to warm and strengthen after a hard day in the mountains. Today they taste just as good in a shepherd's hut on the mountain meadow as in a Zakopane tavern. Podhale cuisine is the cuisine of mountain people — shepherds who for centuries grazed sheep on mountain meadows and lived on what the mountain gave them.

Slow travel through Podhale cuisine is a celebration of taste and tradition. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a shepherd's hut, in a highland tavern, in an agritourism farm. Each dish has its own story, its own shepherd, its own meadow. Ask where the cheese comes from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Podhale cuisine splits into several categories: sheep cheeses (oscypek, bryndza, bundz), soups (kwaśnica, broth), flour dishes (bryjka, moskole), meat dishes (duck with apples, lamb), baked goods (kołocz). Exploring the culinary region of Podhale takes 2–3 days.

Sheep cheeses

  • Oscypek — the icon of the region. Smoked sheep cheese formed into characteristic spindles. Served grilled, with cranberry, or in the classic version with mulled wine. Authentic oscypek is sold at shepherd huts on mountain meadows or in certified shops.
  • Podhale bryndza — sheep cheese made from sheep's milk, inscribed on the EU protected products list. Served with bread, honey, or as a filling for pierogi.
  • Bundz — fresh sheep cheese, not smoked. Delicate, slightly sour, perfect for sandwiches with horseradish or honey.
  • Redykołka — a smaller version of oscypek, often in the form of animal figures. Served with cranberry or as a souvenir.
  • Żętyca — whey from sheep's milk, the traditional drink of shepherds. Served hot or cold.

Highland soups

  • Kwaśnica — highland soup based on sauerkraut with meat. Warms from the first sip. Perfect after a day in the mountains.
  • Highland broth — chicken broth with noodles or dumplings. Served for breakfast or as the first course of dinner.
  • Mushroom soup — soup from dried mushrooms with groats or noodles. Warms you on cold days.
  • Kwaśnica with mushrooms — a variant of kwaśnica with dried mushrooms. A winter version, more filling.

Flour and potato dishes

  • Bryjka — highland dish made of corn flour, served with cranberry, butter, or cracklings. In the traditional version — savory, with bryndza. In the modern version — sweet, with fruit.
  • Moskole — potato pancakes baked on a sheet, often with flour and eggs. Served for breakfast or as a side for soups.
  • Kwaśnica with potatoes — a variant of kwaśnica with potatoes. A more filling version.
  • Silesian dumplings — round potato dumplings, served with sauce and meat. In the highland version — with bryndza or cracklings.

Meat dishes and baked goods

  • Duck with apples — roast duck with apples and marjoram, a Podhale classic. Available in elegant restaurants.
  • Lamb — roast or stewed lamb, a classic of shepherd cuisine. Served with potatoes and cabbage.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms, available at highland taverns.
  • Highland kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Baked for holidays and family celebrations.
  • Karpielówka and śliwowica — local liqueurs, available at highland taverns. Karpielówka (from horseradish root) and śliwowica łącka (from Łącko) are Podhale classics.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal produce, long days. Worth a walk around the shepherd huts.
  • July–September — pastoral season. Sheep graze on the meadows, you can see shepherds at work. Worth it for the experience.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people. September is a good time for mushroom picking in Podhale forests.
  • November–March — season for kwaśnica, grilled oscypek, mulled wine. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Podhale cuisine is from the southern region of Poland — best to travel by car or train, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Zakopane, Nowy Targ have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~2 hours from Kraków (S7 via Rabka), ~5–6 hours from Warsaw, ~4 hours from Wrocław.
  • By bus: PKS and Szwagropol from Kraków and other cities to Zakopane.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Zakopane (highland taverns, oscypek, kwaśnica) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Zakopane, Kościelisko, Bukowina Tatrzańska, Witów.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of Podhale — all the most important dishes and places.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose shepherd huts and highland taverns. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Grilled oscypek — the icon of the region, available at shepherd huts on the meadows.
  • Kwaśnica — highland sauerkraut soup, available at every highland tavern.
  • Bryjka — highland corn flour dish, available at shepherd huts.
  • Moskole — potato pancakes baked on a sheet, available at highland taverns.
  • Duck with apples — a Podhale classic, available in elegant restaurants.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Zakopane (largest choice, but expensive and busy), Bukowina Tatrzańska (quieter, near the thermal baths), Kościelisko and Witów (quiet, close to Kościeliska Valley), Murzasichle and Małe Ciche (small villages, guesthouses), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Shepherd huts — usually open during the pastoral season (May–September). Check hours before your trip.
  • Highland taverns — usually open from 12 p.m. to 10 p.m. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants on Krupówki.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (making oscypek, baking moskole, making kwaśnica). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Podhale cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Markets — the best place to buy local products (cheeses, honey, cold cuts, liqueurs).
  • Weather — mountain weather changes fast. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in Zakopane, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Grilled oscypek on Krupówki
  2. Kwaśnica in a mountain hut
  3. Bryjka with cranberry in a shepherd's hut

Tip

Order grilled oscypek in a shepherd's hut on Krupówki or Gubałówka — straight from the shepherd it tastes completely different than from a supermarket.
