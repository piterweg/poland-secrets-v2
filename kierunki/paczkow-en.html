OPOLE REGION

Paczków — The Polish Carcassonne

1 day
Home›Opole Region›Paczków

One of the best-preserved fortified towns in Poland. Walls, towers, and a gate that remembers the Middle Ages. Slow travel through Paczków — a town within walls.

Why Paczków, differently

Paczków is one of the few towns in Poland that has preserved an almost complete ring of defensive walls with towers and gates. No wonder it is called the Polish Carcassonne — a walk along the walls is a journey through time that lets you feel what a medieval fortified town looked like. Paczków's walls survived despite wars, fires and political changes, and today they are one of the most valuable monuments of this type in Poland.

Slow travel through Paczków is above all about slowing down. This is a town with no crowds, no haste, no pressure to sightsee. There is a market square, there are walls, there are cafés — and there is time to really see them. It is worth stopping here for a few hours, walking the walls, entering the church, having lunch in a local restaurant, and simply listening to how the town lives. It is an experience that shows that the most beautiful journeys are those where you don't have to do anything.

What to see

Paczków is compact — the main sights are within walking distance. Half a day or a full day is enough to see it.

Defensive walls and towers

  • Defensive walls — an almost complete ring of walls from the 14th century, ~1.2 km long. Walk them all the way around (~1.5 hours) — different views of the town and surroundings from each side.
  • Towers — Paczków's walls have 19 towers and 3 gates. The most distinctive are the Wrocław Tower, Ząbkowice Tower and Kłodzko Tower. Worth climbing one of them — a view over the town and the Sudetes.
  • Wrocław Gate — the best-preserved town gate in Paczków, from the 14th century. Worth seeing from the outside and climbing to the top.
  • Moat and greenery — the former moat around the walls, now a green belt. Worth walking along the moat, especially in spring when trees bloom.

Market square and town hall

  • Main Market Square — the heart of Paczków, surrounded by townhouses from different eras. In the centre, the town hall with a distinctive tower. Look at the townhouses — each has a different story.
  • Town Hall — a Classicist building from the 19th century, in the centre of the market square. Worth climbing the town hall tower — a view over the town and walls.
  • House Under the Eye of Providence — one of the most interesting townhouses in Paczków, with a distinctive detail.
  • Town well — a historic well on the market square, one of the town's symbols.

Churches and sacred monuments

  • Church of St. John the Evangelist — a Gothic temple from the 14th century, one of the most important in the town. The interior delights with vaults and altars. Worth stepping inside.
  • Church of St. Nicholas — former cemetery church, today an auxiliary temple. Less known but worth attention.
  • St. Anne's Chapel — a chapel on the outskirts, with Gothic origins. Worth seeing from the outside.
  • Water tower — a former 19th-century water tower, today a viewpoint. Worth climbing for views.

Surroundings (day trips)

  • Ząbkowice Śląskie — a small town with a leaning tower, ~15 km from Paczków. Worth it for architecture and atmosphere.
  • Kamieniec Ząbkowicki Castle — a 19th-century palace, ~25 km from Paczków. Worth visiting.
  • Kłodzko — a town with a fortress, ~30 km from Paczków. Worth a day trip.
  • Bystrzyca Kłodzka — a small town with a Gothic layout, ~40 km from Paczków.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time. The town blooms, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk along the walls.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. Market square and walls crowded, but the town has summer energy. Come on a weekday.
  • November–March — the town empties out, but is wintry. Worth it for those who want to see Paczków without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1.5 hours from Wrocław (A4 and DK46), ~1 hour from Opole (DK46), ~40 minutes from Kłodzko (DK46).
  • By train: station in Paczków, with connections from Wrocław (~2 hours), Opole (~1.5 hours), Kłodzko (~30 minutes).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Wrocław, Opole, Kłodzko.

Within the town, the centre is compact and most sights are within walking distance. But to see the surroundings (Ząbkowice, Kamieniec Ząbkowicki), a car is useful.

How much time you need

Half a day to a full day — enough to see the town and walk the walls.

A weekend (2 days) is realistic for a first visit. One day for Paczków, one for a trip (Ząbkowice Śląskie, Kłodzko or Kamieniec Ząbkowicki).

A week lets you explore the whole region — Paczków, Ząbkowice, Kłodzko, Bystrzyca Kłodzka and surroundings.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Duck with apples — a Lower Silesian classic, available in restaurants in the centre.
  • Silesian dumplings — round potato dumplings with a hole, served with sauce and meat.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms in the Sudetes.
  • Kwaśnica — cabbage soup with meat, a regional classic.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the Sudetes.
  • Coffee and cake — the market square has several good cafés, ideal for an afternoon break.

Accommodation: market square and surroundings (most convenient), streets adjacent to the centre (cheaper), private rooms and guesthouses (mid-price), Ząbkowice Śląskie and Kłodzko (~15–30 km, more options). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — tickets for museums and the town hall tower are worth booking in advance, especially in season.
  • Guided tours — Paczków's history is complex, and without context many places look similar. Worth going with a guide or audio guide.
  • Defensive walls — walking the walls is easy, but slippery after rain. Wear comfortable shoes.
  • Crowds — the market square and walls are less crowded than in other towns, but in season (May–September) it is worth coming on a weekday.
  • Parking — the centre has a paid parking zone, spaces are hard to find. Large car parks outside the walls, then a 5–10 min walk.
  • Signal — good in the centre, weaker on the outskirts.

Trip plan

  1. Walk along the defensive walls
  2. Town hall tower and market square
  3. St. John the Evangelist Church

Tip

Take a walk along the walls early in the morning or late in the afternoon. That is when the light is most beautiful, and the walls look most picturesque.
