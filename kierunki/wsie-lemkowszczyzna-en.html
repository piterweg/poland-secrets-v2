Lemko Region — Lemko Villages

3 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Lemko Region

Wooden churches, shingle-roofed cottages, and traces of a culture that survived despite deportations. Beskid villages with extraordinary history. Slow travel through Lemko villages.

Why Lemko villages, differently

Lemko villages represent one of the most extraordinary ethnographic groups in Poland. The Lemkos — a Ruthenian population inhabiting the Low and Sądecki Beskids — created a distinct culture over the centuries that survived despite the tragic events of the 20th century. Villages like Hańczowa, Kwiatoń, Owczary, Bartne are today a living testimony to this culture. This is one of the few places in Poland where you can still see wooden churches in their original setting, hear the Lemko language, feel the atmosphere of an old village.

Slow travel through Lemko villages is a journey through history and culture. Instead of rushing from village to village, stop in one longer — talk to residents, enter the church, see a shingle-roofed cottage, listen to how the village lives. Each village has its own history, its own churches, its own inhabitants. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly understand about Lemko culture.

What to see

Lemko villages split into several groups: villages with churches (Kwiatoń, Owczary, Bartne), villages with wooden architecture (Hańczowa, Zdynia), abandoned villages (Radocyna, Grab), villages with Lemko culture (Zyndranowa, Olchowiec). Exploring the region takes 2–3 days.

Villages with Lemko churches

  • Kwiatoń — a village with the Church of St. Paraskeva from the 17th century, inscribed on the UNESCO list. The church is three-part, covered with shingles, with a distinctive tower. One of the most beautiful Lemko churches in Poland.
  • Owczary — a village with the Church of the Protection of the Mother of God from the 17th century, inscribed on the UNESCO list. The church is three-part, covered with shingles, with a unique iconostasis.
  • Bartne — a village with the Church of St. Cosmas and Damian from the 19th century. Less known than Kwiatoń and Owczary, but equally beautiful.
  • Zdynia — a village with the Church of St. Michael the Archangel from the 18th century. There is also a Lemko cultural centre here.

Villages with wooden architecture

  • Hańczowa — a village with wooden architecture, shingle-roofed cottages, and a church. Worth it for the atmosphere and contact with Lemko culture.
  • Lemko cottages — wooden houses covered with shingles, often with a gable roof. A characteristic element of the Low Beskids landscape.
  • Villages with cottages — several villages in the Low Beskids have preserved wooden architecture. Worth cycling through several villages.
  • Open-air museum in Zyndranowa — an ethnographic museum presenting Lemko culture. Worth it for context.

Abandoned villages and memory

  • Radocyna — a village abandoned after 1947. Today you can only see cottage foundations, old orchards, and lonely churches.
  • Grab — a village abandoned after 1947. Worth it for history and memory.
  • Lemko cemeteries — in many cemeteries in the Low Beskids you can still see tombstones with Cyrillic inscriptions. Worth it for memory.
  • Shrines and roadside crosses — in many Lemko villages, shrines and roadside crosses with inscriptions in the Lemko language have survived.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk around the villages.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people. September is a good time for mushroom picking in the Low Beskids.
  • July and August — peak season. Villages crowded, but all attractions open. Come on a weekday.
  • November–March — villages in winter are less accessible (some churches closed). Worth it for those who want to see the villages without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Kraków (DK75), ~1 hour from Nowy Sącz (DK75), ~2 hours from Rzeszów (DK28).
  • By train: Nowy Sącz, Gorlice have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most villages.

Lemko villages are best explored by car or bike — they are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 villages in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Kwiatoń, Owczary, Bartne, Zdynia.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Villages with churches, villages with wooden architecture, abandoned villages, Zyndranowa.

A week lets you explore all Lemko villages — from Krynica to the Bieszczady, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Proziaki — soda bread pancakes, available at village inns.
  • Fuczki — cabbage fritters, a Lemko dish, available at local bars.
  • Lemko kwaśnica — sauerkraut soup, available at every inn.
  • Pierogi with bryndza — delicate pierogi with sheep cheese, available at agritourism farms.
  • Herbal liqueurs — local liqueurs from mountain herbs, available at inns.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Nowy Sącz (town, more options), Gorlice (town, more options), Krynica-Zdrój (spa, more options), Hańczowa (village, agritourism farms), Zyndranowa (village, open-air museum), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the history of Lemko villages is complex, and without context many churches look similar. Take a guide at selected places.
  • Services — in churches during services, keep quiet and do not photograph. Come to a service to hear the liturgical chants.
  • Clothing — modest dress is required in the church. Have a scarf or shawl for shoulders.
  • Opening hours — many churches are open only in season (May–October) and only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Bookings — visiting some churches requires an advance appointment. Call or email.
  • Weather — weather in the Low Beskids can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Churches in Kwiatoń, Owczary, and Bartne
  2. Lemko cottages in Hańczowa
  3. Traces of displacement in the Low Beskids

Tip

Take a hike through the Lemko villages in autumn — that is when the landscapes are most picturesque, and the old orchards look most beautiful.
