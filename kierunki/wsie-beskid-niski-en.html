LESSER POLAND

Low Beskids — Abandoned Lemko Villages

3 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Low Beskids

The Lower Beskid is the wildest and least populated region of Poland. Lemko villages, wooden Orthodox churches, deserted valleys. Slow travel through the Lower Beskid.

Why it's worth it

The Lower Beskid is a region that looks like Poland a hundred years ago. Villages are sparse, valleys are long, and forests are dense. Most residents are descendants of the Lemkos — a Ruthenian population deported after World War II as part of Operation Vistula (1947). What the Lemkos left behind are Orthodox churches — wooden and brick — and cemeteries, often overgrown. Some villages were completely abandoned and exist today only on maps. This is not a region for a tourist looking for attractions — it is a region for someone who wants to see a landscape where history is still present, even if no one talks about it loudly.

Slow travel through the Lower Beskid is a journey through a landscape that demands your attention. Villages are small — often 50–200 residents — and have no tourist infrastructure. There are no guesthouses in every village, no cafés, no souvenir shops. What there is, is silence that's hard to find elsewhere in Poland, and Orthodox churches that are among the most valuable wooden architecture monuments in Europe.

What to see

Lower Beskid villages are best explored in three clusters: around Sękowa and Gorlice (west), around Zdynia and Bartne (centre), and around Komańcza and Cisna (east). Exploring the region takes 3–4 days.

Villages around Sękowa and Gorlice

  • Sękowa — a village with the wooden church of St Philip and St James (1520), a UNESCO site. One of the most valuable wooden monuments in Poland.
  • Owczary — a village with a Lemko Orthodox church from 1653, a UNESCO site. Worth it for architecture.
  • Gorlice — a town with a market square and WWI monuments. Worth it for context.

Villages around Zdynia and Bartne

  • Zdynia — a village with a Greek Catholic church and the annual Lemko festival (Zdarzenia). Worth it for culture.
  • Bartne — a village with two Orthodox churches and preserved Lemko architecture. Worth it for atmosphere.
  • Kwiatoń — a village with an 1870 Orthodox church, a UNESCO site. Worth it for architecture.

Villages around Komańcza and Cisna

  • Komańcza — a village with a convent of the Sisters of the Holy Family of Nazareth, where Cardinal Stefan Wyszyński was interned in 1955. Worth it for history.
  • Cisna — a village in the Solinka valley, with numerous trails into the Bieszczady. Worth it for hiking.
  • Wysowa-Zdrój — a spa with mineral waters, one of the oldest in the region. Worth it for health.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Green, warm, few tourists.
  • July–August — season for Lemko festivals (Zdarzenia in Zdynia, Lemko Watra). Worth it for events.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, mushroom picking.
  • November–March — villages are hard to reach in winter, many roads unploughed. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Kraków (DK28), ~2 hours from Rzeszów (DK28), ~5 hours from Warsaw (S7 + DK28).
  • By train: Gorlice, Nowy Sącz, Sanok have railway stations. Then rent a car.
  • By bus: PKS serves most larger villages, but connections are sparse.

The Lower Beskid is best explored by car — villages are scattered over a large area, and public transport is very weak. Plan the route to connect 2–3 villages in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Sękowa, Owczary, Gorlice.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Sękowa, Owczary, Zdynia, Bartne, Kwiatoń.

A week lets you explore all of the Lower Beskid — from Gorlice to Komańcza, with numerous stops and hikes.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pierogi with bryndza — regional pierogi with sheep cheese, available at inns.
  • Kwaśnica — sour cabbage soup, a Lemko classic.
  • Proziaki — flatbreads of flour and sour milk, served with butter or lard.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Young wine from Wysowa — from local vineyards, available in season.

Accommodation: Gorlice (town, more options), Wysowa-Zdrój (spa, guesthouses), Komańcza (village, agritourism), Cisna (village, guesthouses), numerous agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — Lemko history is complex, take a guide in Sękowa or Zyndranowa.
  • Orthodox churches — many are closed on weekdays. Keys are often with the village head or at the parish. Ask around.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Lemko festivals — Zdarzenia in Zdynia (July) and Lemko Watra in Zdynia (August). Book accommodation well in advance.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable. Even in July bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — weak in many villages. Have an offline map.

What to avoid

  • Don't plan to explore the Lower Beskid without a car — public transport is very weak.
  • Don't expect restaurants in every village — in many places you need to order food in advance at the agritourism farm.
  • Don't go on an August weekend without a reservation — Lemko festivals draw crowds.

Trip plan

  1. Villages with churches in Radocyna and Grab
  2. Cemeteries and cottage foundations
  3. Trails through abandoned villages

Tip

Stay overnight at an agritourism farm in Bartne — one of the few villages where compact Lemko architecture survives.
