MASOVIA

Kampinos National Park — Wilderness by Warsaw

1 day
Home›National parks›Kampinos National Park

Kampinos National Park is the only national park in Poland so close to a major metropolis. Forest, marshes, moose. Slow travel near Warsaw.

Why it's worth it

Kampinos National Park was established in 1959 to protect the Kampinos Forest — one of the most valuable forest complexes in Poland. It lies 30 km from the centre of Warsaw, making it the only national park in Poland so close to a major metropolis. The Kampinos Forest is not just woodland — it is a mosaic of ecosystems: inland dunes, marshes, meadows, alder carrs, pine forests, and oak-hornbeam forests. About 200 moose live in the park — one of the largest populations in Poland. Add to that beavers, otters, wild boar, roe deer, and among birds — cranes, black storks, white-tailed eagles.

Slow travel through Kampinos National Park is a journey through a landscape that surprises with its diversity. The park is divided into two main belts: the dune belt (north) and the marsh belt (south), separated by the Łasica canal. It is worth spending 2–3 days here to see the different types of landscape. The best way to explore the park is on foot or by bike — there are about 360 km of hiking trails and 200 km of cycling trails, and connections with Warsaw are excellent (buses and trains).

What to see

Kampinos National Park is divided into three main parts: the dune belt (north), the marsh belt (south), and the centre (around Palmyra). Exploring the park takes 2–3 days.

Dune belt

  • Inland dunes — a unique landscape, dunes up to 30 m high. Worth it for views.
  • Palmyra — a hamlet in the park, with a WWII cemetery-mausoleum. Worth it for history.
  • Truskaw — a village by the park, starting point for hiking trails. Worth it as a base.

Marsh belt

  • Łasica marshes — the most valuable ecosystem in the park, a refuge for moose and beaver. Worth it for nature.
  • Kampinos — a village with a monastery and a former palace. Worth it for architecture.
  • Wólka Węglowa — a hamlet in the park, with a forester's lodge and a viewpoint. Worth it for views.

Centre

  • Izabelin — a village by the park, with the park headquarters and a museum. Worth it for information.
  • Granica — a village by the park, starting point for trails. Worth it as a base.
  • Stare Babice — a village by the park, with an old church. Worth it for architecture.

When to go

  • April–May — the best time for birds and blooming meadows. Worth it for nature.
  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — warm, but crowded (the park is popular with Varsovians).
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, mushroom picking.
  • November–March — the park is accessible in winter, but many trails are muddy. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~30 minutes from Warsaw (DK7 or S8), ~1.5 hours from Łódź (A2).
  • By train: Koleje Mazowieckie trains from Warsaw to Truskaw, Kampinos, Palmyra. Worth it for car-free travel.
  • By bus: ZTM and PKS lines from Warsaw to most villages by the park.

Kampinos National Park is best explored on foot or by bike — the park is excellently connected with Warsaw, and the trails are well marked. Leave the car at the car park at the park entrance.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Palmyra, Łasica marshes, dunes.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Palmyra, Łasica marshes, dunes, Kampinos, Izabelin.

A week lets you explore the whole park — all hiking and cycling trails, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Trout from the Łasica — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Forest mushrooms — from local harvests, available in season.
  • Goat cheeses — from local farms, available in village shops.
  • Mead — from Kampinos apiaries.

Accommodation: Warsaw (city, more options), Izabelin (village, guesthouses), Kampinos (village, guesthouses), Truskaw (village, agritourism).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the park has rich fauna and flora, take a guide.
  • Moose — about 200 moose live in the park. Keep quiet and do not approach too closely.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Opening hours — some trails are closed during the bird breeding season. Check before your trip.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — good throughout the park (close to Warsaw).

Trip plan

  1. Trail to Granica
  2. Kampinos Forest Museum
  3. Sunset over the dunes

Tip

Go to the Łasica marshes early in the morning — the best time to see moose and cranes in their natural habitat.
