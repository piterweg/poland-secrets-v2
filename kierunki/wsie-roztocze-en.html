LUBLIN REGION

Roztocze — Roztocze Villages with Mills

2–3 days
Home›Lublin Region›Roztocze

Roztocze is a region where villages look like they come from another era. Wooden cottages, limestone rocks, loess ravines. Slow travel through Roztocze villages.

Why Roztocze villages, differently

Roztocze is a belt of hills stretching from Kraśnik to Lviv — in Poland it covers mainly the Lublin and Subcarpathian voivodeships. Roztocze villages have a distinctive look: wooden cottages with tall, four-sloped roofs covered with thatch or shingles, whitewashed walls, often with an external chimney. This type of architecture has almost disappeared elsewhere in Poland. Roztocze is also a region where limestone — a local building material — appears in the architecture of churches, shrines, and fences. As a result, the villages look as if they were carved from a single material.

Slow travel through Roztocze is a journey through a hilly landscape cut by loess ravines. Villages here are small (100–400 residents), scattered across hills. Many have preserved their 19th-century spatial layout — with buildings along a single road, a well in the middle, and a church or Orthodox church at the end. This is a region best explored on foot or by bike, because only then can you see how the villages fit into the landscape — something you won't notice from a car.

What to see

Roztocze villages fall into three groups: villages in Central Roztocze (around Zwierzyniec), villages in Southern Roztocze (around Narol and Lubaczów), and villages in Western Roztocze (around Szczebrzeszyn). Exploring the region takes 3–4 days.

Villages in Central Roztocze

  • Zwierzyniec — a small town with a Baroque church and a former Zamoyski brewery. Worth it for architecture.
  • Guciów village — a village with the Guciów farmstead, one of the most interesting ethnographic museums in the region. Worth it for culture.
  • Sochy village — a village with a wooden church and typical Roztocze architecture. Worth it for the atmosphere.

Villages in Southern Roztocze

  • Narol — a small town with a wooden church and an Orthodox church. Worth it for architecture.
  • Horyniec-Zdrój village — a spa with mineral waters, one of the oldest in the region. Worth it for health.
  • Radruż village — a village with a wooden Orthodox church from 1583, a UNESCO site. One of the most valuable wooden monuments in Poland.

Villages in Western Roztocze

  • Szczebrzeszyn — a small town with a Renaissance church and a synagogue. Worth it for architecture.
  • Klemensów village — a village with the Zamoyski palace and park. Worth it for architecture.
  • Topólcza village — a village with a wooden church and a 19th-century cemetery. Worth it for the atmosphere.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists. Worth it for walks around the villages.
  • July–August — warm, but crowded around Zwierzyniec and Horyniec-Zdrój.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, mushroom picking in the forests.
  • November–March — villages are sleepy in winter, many attractions closed. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Lublin (DK17), ~1.5 hours from Rzeszów (DK19), ~3.5 hours from Warsaw (S17).
  • By train: Zamość, Zwierzyniec, Horyniec-Zdrój have railway stations. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS serves most larger villages.

Roztocze is best explored by car or bike — villages are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Zwierzyniec, Guciów, Sochy.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Zwierzyniec, Guciów, Sochy, Narol, Horyniec-Zdrój.

A week lets you explore all of Roztocze — from Kraśnik to Horyniec-Zdrój, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pierogi with buckwheat — a regional version of pierogi, available at inns.
  • Roztocze żurek — rye sour soup with white sausage.
  • Trout from Horyniec — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Korycin cheeses — from the Korycin dairy, available in village shops.
  • Young wine from Roztocze vineyards — from the region's numerous vineyards.

Accommodation: Zamość (town, more options), Zwierzyniec (small town, guesthouses), Horyniec-Zdrój (spa, guesthouses), villages (agritourism, guesthouses).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — Roztocze's history is complex, take a guide in Zamość or Zwierzyniec.
  • Loess ravines — among the most interesting in Poland, best explored on foot. Watch out for slippery walls after rain.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Opening hours — many attractions (museums, Orthodox churches) open only in season.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Water mill in Zwierzyniec
  2. Villages with wooden architecture near Krasnobród
  3. Roztocze farmsteads

Tip

Visit Radruż — the 1583 wooden Orthodox church is one of the most valuable wooden monuments in Poland and a pearl of the UNESCO route.
