WEST POMERANIA

Drawa National Park — Wild Drawa and Beavers

2 days
Home›West Pomerania›Drawa National Park

Drawa National Park is one of the wildest parks in Poland. The Drawa River, pine forests, lakes. Slow travel through the Drawa.

Why it's worth it

Drawa National Park was established in 1990 — relatively late, but that is precisely why it preserved its wildness. It lies on the border of the West Pomeranian, Lubusz, and Greater Poland voivodeships, within the Drawa Forest complex. Pine forests dominate here — dry, sandy, with sparse undergrowth. But the forest is not the most important thing here — the most important is the Drawa River, one of the most beautiful rivers in Poland, with a swift current, sandy bottom, and numerous meanders. The Drawa is a kayaking route of European class, and its valley is a refuge for rare birds and fish.

Slow travel through Drawa National Park is a journey through a landscape that requires patience. There are no spectacular peaks or waterfalls here — there are forests, lakes, the river, and silence. It is worth spending 2–3 days here to truly feel this landscape. The best way to explore the park is on foot or by kayak — from the water, the Drawa looks completely different than from the shore, and most of its course is accessible only to kayaks.

What to see

Drawa National Park is divided into three main parts: the Drawa valley (centre), lakes in the northern part, and forests in the southern part. Exploring the park takes 2–3 days.

Drawa valley

  • Drawa — the river, its valley is the most valuable element of the park. Worth it for a kayak trip (1–3 days).
  • Drawno — a small town by the park, starting point for kayak trips. Worth it for accommodation.
  • Barnimie — a hamlet in the park, with a former mill and water power plant. Worth it for architecture.

Northern lakes

  • Lake Ostrowiec — the largest lake in the park, with crystal-clear water. Worth it for swimming.
  • Lake Grażyna — a lake in the middle of the forest, accessible only on foot. Worth it for silence.
  • Lake Żerdno — a lake with islands, in the northern part of the park. Worth it for kayaking.

Southern forests

  • Pine forests — the largest forest complex in the park. Worth it for mushroom picking.
  • Płociczna valley — the valley of the Płociczna river, a tributary of the Drawa. Worth it for wildness.
  • Sitnica — a hamlet in the park, with a forester's lodge and a viewpoint. Worth it for views.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — kayaking season. Worth it for kayaks, but crowded on the Drawa.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, mushroom picking.
  • November–March — the park is hard to reach in winter, many roads unploughed. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Poznań (DK11), ~2 hours from Szczecin (DK10), ~4.5 hours from Warsaw (S10 + DK11).
  • By train: Krzyż, Choszczno, Drawno have railway stations. Then rent a car.
  • By bus: PKS serves Drawno and larger villages in the area.

Drawa National Park is best explored by car or kayak — the park is vast, and public transport between towns is weak.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — a Drawa kayak trip, Drawno, Lake Ostrowiec.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. A Drawa kayak trip, Drawno, northern lakes, southern forests.

A week lets you explore the whole park and surrounding forests — from Drawno to Czaplinek, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Trout from the Drawa — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Smoked fish — from smokehouses in Drawno and surroundings.
  • Forest mushrooms — from local harvests, available in season.
  • Beer from Greater Poland breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Drawno (small town, guesthouses), Choszczno (town, more options), Czaplinek (small town, guesthouses), villages (agritourism, guesthouses).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the park has rich fauna and flora, take a guide.
  • Kayaking — the Drawa is a route of European class. Rentals in Drawno, Czaplinek, Krzyż.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Opening hours — some trails are closed during the bird breeding season. Check before your trip.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — weak in many parts of the park. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Kayaking down the Drawa
  2. Drawa nature trail
  3. Overnight at a campsite

Tip

Book a Drawa kayak trip from Drawno to Barnimie — the most beautiful stretch of the river, with a swift current and sandy beaches.
