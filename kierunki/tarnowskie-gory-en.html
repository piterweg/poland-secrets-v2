Tarnowskie Góry — Underground of Silver and Lead

1 day
Home›Silesia›Tarnowskie Góry

A historic mine with an underground boat route. The only such trail in Europe. Slow travel through Tarnowskie Góry — a town inscribed on the UNESCO list.

Why Tarnowskie Góry, differently

Tarnowskie Góry is a town whose history is inseparably linked to mining. The Historic Silver Mine is one of the region's most important attractions — an underground labyrinth of galleries where silver and lead were extracted for centuries, and today you can trace the history of the mining craft. In 2017 the mine was inscribed on the UNESCO list as part of the post-mining trail.

Slow travel through Tarnowskie Góry is a journey through the history of industry. The tourist route leads through the most interesting parts of the mine, showing both old extraction techniques and the tools miners worked with. It is a journey through time that lets you understand what life underground looked like in past centuries. After touring, explore the town — market square, townhouses, former mining infrastructure. Tarnowskie Góry is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: traces of old shafts, mining memorabilia, the atmosphere of a town where industry has always been present.

What to see

Tarnowskie Góry is not just the mine itself — it is a whole complex of attractions: the underground route, the steam engine museum, the market square, the Museum in Tarnowskie Góry. One day is enough to see it, but it is worth devoting a full day.

Historic Silver Mine

  • Historic Silver Mine — a former silver and lead mine, today open to tourists. The tourist route leads through the most interesting fragments of the mine (~1.5 hours). Worth it for history and unusual experiences.
  • Underground boat route — a unique attraction on a European scale. The mine in Tarnowskie Góry is the only one in Europe where you can sail underground by boat (~15 minutes). Worth it for an unusual experience.
  • Galleries and chambers — galleries and chambers carved in rock, where silver and lead were extracted. Worth it for underground architecture.
  • Exhibitions underground — exhibitions about the history of the mine and mining tools. Worth it for historical context.
  • Steam Engine Museum — a collection of historic steam machines that once powered the mine. Worth it for technology enthusiasts.

Town and market square

  • Market square in Tarnowskie Góry — the heart of the town, surrounded by townhouses from different eras. In the centre, the town hall with a distinctive tower. Worth a walk and shopping.
  • Town Hall — a Neo-Classical building from the 19th century, in the centre of the market square. Worth seeing from the outside.
  • Church of the Apostles Peter and Paul — a Baroque temple from the 18th century, one of the most important monuments of the town. Worth seeing.
  • Museum in Tarnowskie Góry — an exhibition about the history of the town and region. Worth it for context.
  • City Park — a park in the town centre, with alleys and old trees. Worth a walk.

Industrial Monuments Trail

  • Industrial Monuments Trail — a tourist trail leading through the most interesting industrial sites in the region. Worth it for lovers of industrial heritage.
  • Czarny Pstrąg Adit — an underground boat route in a former adit, ~600 m. Worth it for unusual experiences.
  • Repty Park — a park in the Repty district, with alleys and ponds. Worth a walk.
  • Stare Tarnowice Castle — ruins of a castle in the Stare Tarnowice district. Worth it for history.
  • Upper Silesian Ethnographic Park — an open-air museum in Chorzów, ~30 km from Tarnowskie Góry. Worth it for understanding the region's culture.

Surroundings

  • Katowice — the regional capital, ~30 km from Tarnowskie Góry. Worth it for accommodation base and culture.
  • Chorzów — a town with the Upper Silesian Ethnographic Park, ~30 km from Tarnowskie Góry.
  • Bytom — a town with a historic old town, ~15 km from Tarnowskie Góry.
  • Gliwice — a town with a market square and Gliwice Radio Station, ~30 km from Tarnowskie Góry. Worth it for architecture and history.
  • Zabrze — a town with the Guido Mine, ~30 km from Tarnowskie Góry. Worth it for industrial enthusiasts.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk around the market square.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. The mine is crowded, ticket queues long. If you must — pick a weekday and come in the morning.
  • November–March — the mine is less crowded in winter, but it is cold underground. Worth it for those who want to see the mine without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~40 minutes from Katowice (A1 and DK11), ~1.5 hours from Kraków (A4 and DK11), ~2 hours from Wrocław (A4 and DK11). Car park at the mine (paid, in season full from 10 a.m.).
  • By train: station in Tarnowskie Góry, with connections from Katowice (~40 minutes), Kraków (~2 hours), Wrocław (~2.5 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Katowice, Kraków, Wrocław.

Within the town you can walk or use local minibuses. The mine is in the Repty district, ~3 km from the centre.

How much time you need

Half a day (3–4 hours) is the minimum for a visit to the mine and a walk around the market square.

A full day lets you add the Czarny Pstrąg Adit and a walk around the town.

A weekend (2 days) lets you combine Tarnowskie Góry with Katowice, Gliwice and Zabrze.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Silesian cuisine — roulade, Silesian dumplings, modra kapusta. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Duck with apples — a Silesian classic, available in elegant restaurants.
  • Silesian kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Worth it for dessert.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — the centre of Tarnowskie Góry has several good cafés.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Tarnowskie Góry (closest to the mine, smaller choice), Katowice (~30 km, more options), Gliwice (~30 km, more options), Bytom (~15 km, smaller choice), Zabrze (~30 km, more options). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — tickets for the mine are worth booking in advance, especially in season. The underground route has a limited number of places.
  • Temperature — underground the temperature is constant (~10°C). Dress warmly, even in summer.
  • Shoes — sturdy shoes are essential on the underground route (uneven surfaces, wet stones).
  • Boat route — the boat route is available on selected days. Check the schedule before your trip.
  • Crowds — the mine is crowded in season (May–September). Come in the morning or on weekdays.
  • Parking — the car park at the mine is paid. In season it fills from 10 a.m.
  • Signal — good throughout the town and the mine (outside the underground).

Trip plan

  1. Underground route with boat crossing
  2. Steam Engine Open-Air Museum
  3. Walk around the market square and town park

Tip

Visit the mine on a weekday. On weekends the route can be crowded, but on weekdays you can listen to the guide's stories more calmly and truly feel the atmosphere of the underground.
