Łowicz Masovia — Łowicz Villages with Striped Fabrics

2 days
Home›Villages & rural regions›Łowicz Masovia

Masovia is a region where the countryside looks different from anywhere else in Poland. Noble manors, fruit orchards, flat fields. Slow travel through Masovian villages.

Why Masovian villages, differently

Masovia is a region where the countryside was shaped for centuries by the minor nobility. Instead of great magnate estates — thousands of small noble manors, many of which survive to this day. Masovian villages have no single dominant type of architecture, but they share a common rhythm: a manor at the edge of the village, a park, a fruit orchard, a tree-lined driveway. Add to that churches — often Baroque, brick, from the 18th century — and wayside shrines. Masovia is also a region of orchards — apple, plum, cherry. In spring, the villages drown in blossoms; in autumn, in fruit.

Slow travel through Masovia is a journey through a flat, agricultural landscape dominated by fields. Villages are larger here than in eastern Poland (often 300–600 residents) and better connected. Many have preserved their 18th- and 19th-century spatial layout — with a manor, park, church, and inn in the centre. This is a region where it is worth exploring not just individual villages but whole clusters of them — because only together do they show what noble Masovia looked like.

What to see

Masovian villages are best explored in three clusters: villages around Warsaw (west and south), villages around Płock (northwest), and villages around Siedlce and Łosice (east). Exploring the region takes 3–4 days.

Villages around Warsaw

  • Żelazowa Wola — a village with the manor where Fryderyk Chopin was born. Worth it for history and the park.
  • Nieborów village — a village with the Baroque Radziwiłł palace and park. Worth it for architecture.
  • Arkadia village — a village with the romantic park of Helena Radziwiłł, one of the most beautiful in Poland. Worth it for the landscape.

Villages around Płock

  • Płock — a town with a cathedral and a former castle. Worth it for architecture.
  • Suserz village — a village with a wooden church and typical Masovian architecture. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Czerwińsk nad Wisłą village — a village with a Romanesque church from the 12th century, one of the most valuable in Poland. Worth it for architecture.

Villages around Siedlce and Łosice

  • Siedlce — a town with a Baroque church and a former town hall. Worth it for architecture.
  • Mordy village — a village with a wooden church and a manor. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Łosice village — a small town with a synagogue and a church, a former trading centre. Worth it for history.

When to go

  • April–May — the best time for orchards in bloom. Worth it for the landscape.
  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — fruit season. Worth it for orchards, but crowded around Warsaw.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people.
  • November–March — villages are sleepy in winter. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Warsaw to most villages, ~1.5 hours from Łódź (A2), ~3 hours from Gdańsk (S7).
  • By train: Warsaw, Płock, Siedlce have railway stations. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS serves most larger villages.

Masovia is best explored by car — villages are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 villages in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Żelazowa Wola, Nieborów, Arkadia.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Żelazowa Wola, Nieborów, Arkadia, Czerwińsk, Suserz.

A week lets you explore all of Masovia — from Płock to Siedlce, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a Masovian classic, available at inns.
  • Masovian żurek — rye sour soup with egg and white sausage.
  • Curd cheeses — from local dairies, available in village shops.
  • Apples from orchards — from local orchards, available in season.
  • Mead — from Masovian apiaries.

Accommodation: Warsaw (city, more options), Płock (town, more options), Siedlce (town, more options), villages (agritourism, manors, guesthouses).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — Masovia's history is complex, take a guide in Nieborów and Czerwińsk.
  • Noble manors — many manors are privately owned and not open to visitors. Check in advance.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and fruit directly from farmers.
  • Opening hours — many attractions (palaces, museums) open only in season.
  • Weather — weather can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — good throughout the region, including villages.

Trip plan

  1. Open-air museums in Sierpc and Łowicz
  2. Villages with Łowicz striped fabrics
  3. Łowicz cutouts and embroidery

Tip

Go to Arkadia in May — Helena Radziwiłł's romantic park in full bloom is one of the most beautiful places in Masovia.
