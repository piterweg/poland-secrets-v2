EASTERN POLAND

Green Velo — Poland's Longest Cycling Route

10–14 days
Home›Cycling routes›Green Velo

Over 2,000 km through five voivodeships. Warmia, Masuria, Podlasie, the Lublin region, and Subcarpathia. Slow travel along Green Velo — Poland's longest cycling route.

Why Green Velo, differently

Green Velo is the longest and best-marked cycling route in Poland — over 2,000 km leading through five voivodeships: Warmian-Masurian, Podlaskie, Lubelskie, Podkarpackie, and Świętokrzyskie. It is not just a road but a complete tourism product: signage, infrastructure, accommodation, and bike workshops along the entire route. The route was built between 2011 and 2015 and is one of the largest cycling projects in Central Europe.

Slow travel on Green Velo is above all about adjusting your pace to your own abilities. 50–60 km per day is the optimal distance that lets you notice landscapes and meet people while not exhausting yourself on the route. Instead of riding the entire route in one season, divide it into stages and complete them over several years. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are on the route, the more details you notice: villages, churches, landscapes, people who make this place.

What to see

Green Velo splits into several sections, corresponding to the regions it passes through: Warmia and Masuria (north), Podlasie (centre), Lublin region (east), Subcarpathia (south). Riding the whole route takes 10–14 days, but you can also ride selected stages.

Warmia and Masuria

  • Frombork — the town of Copernicus, with a cathedral on a hill. Worth it for history and views.
  • Lidzbark Warmiński — a small town with the Warmian bishops' castle. Worth it for architecture.
  • Kętrzyn and Wolf's Lair — a town and Hitler's former headquarters. Worth it for history.
  • Giżycko and Mikołajki — towns on the Masurian lakes. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Węgorzewo and the Masurian Canal — a town and canal in northern Masuria. Worth it for sailors.

Podlasie

  • Białystok — the regional capital, with the Branicki Palace and garden. Worth it for architecture.
  • Supraśl and Knyszyn Forest — an Orthodox monastery and wild forest. Worth it for the sacred and nature.
  • Białowieża National Park — the last primeval forest in Europe, with bison. Worth it for nature.
  • Biebrza National Park — the largest wetlands in Europe, with waterfowl. Worth it for birdwatchers.
  • Tykocin and Kruszyniany — a small town with a synagogue and a Tatar village with a mosque. Worth it for multicultural history.

Lublin region

  • Lublin — a city with a historic Old Town and Castle. Worth it for culture.
  • Zamość — the ideal Renaissance town with a market square (UNESCO). Worth it for architecture.
  • Roztocze — a region with loess ravines, cascades and wooden villages. Worth it for nature.
  • Kazimierz Dolny — the artists' town on the Vistula, with the Korzeniowy Dół ravine. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Chełm — a town with chalk hills and underground passages. Worth it for history.

Subcarpathia

  • Sandomierz — a town on seven hills, with the Queen Jadwiga Ravine. Worth it for architecture.
  • Rzeszów — the regional capital, with a market square and underground route. Worth it for culture.
  • Przemyśl — a town with a fortress and three cathedrals. Worth it for history.
  • Bieszczady — wild mountains in the south, with alpine meadows and abandoned villages. Worth it for nature.
  • Sanok — a town with the Folk Architecture Museum (open-air) and Icon Museum. Worth it for culture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth cycling.
  • September — second best. Warm days, fewer people, autumn colours. September is a good time for longer stages.
  • July and August — peak season. Route crowded, prices high, but all attractions open. Worth it for those who like the company of other cyclists.
  • October — end of the season. Still beautiful autumn colours, but the weather can be capricious. Worth it for those with a flexible plan.
  • November–April — the route in winter is less accessible (snow, ice). Worth it for experienced cyclists.

Getting there

  • By train: Green Velo crosses several main railway lines (Warsaw–Białystok, Warsaw–Lublin, Kraków–Rzeszów, Gdańsk–Olsztyn). You can reach any point on the route by train.
  • By car: the route crosses main national roads. You can arrive by car and park at one of the car parks along the route.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

Green Velo is well connected to the rest of Poland — you can start anywhere, arrive by train, ride a chosen stage and return by train. Plan the route so that the start and end of the stage are near railway stations.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one stage of 50–60 km per day, around a chosen town (e.g. Białystok, Lublin, Rzeszów).

A week is realistic for a first visit. You can ride 300–400 km, completing one region (e.g. Warmia and Masuria, Podlasie or the Lublin region).

Two weeks is time for the whole route — from Elbląg to Końskie (through five voivodeships). This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

Three to four weeks — if you want to ride truly slow, with numerous stops, sightseeing and rest.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Regional cuisine — along the route you can try Podlasie cuisine (kibiny, kartacze), Lublin cuisine (cebularz, pierogi with bryndza), Subcarpathian (proziaki, fuczki), Warmian (vendace, marzipan).
  • Pierogi — at every regional inn, with various fillings.
  • Fish — in Masuria and Podlasie, smoked, fried, grilled.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in each region.
  • Coffee and cake — each larger town on the route has several good cafés.
  • Local products — cheeses, honey, cold cuts, available at agritourism farms and regional shops.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), campsites (for those who want to sleep in a tent), PTTK mountain huts (on mountain sections). Book accommodation in advance, especially in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bike — a trekking or gravel bike is best for Green Velo. The route runs mainly on asphalt and gravel roads. A mountain bike will also work but will be slower on asphalt.
  • Bookings — book accommodation on the route in advance, especially in summer season (July–August). In May and September it is easier.
  • Signage — the route is very well marked (Green Velo logo, information boards). GPS navigation is not necessary, but have an app with the route.
  • Infrastructure — there are numerous cyclist service points (MORs) along the route, with shelters, benches, bike racks, often with drinking water.
  • Bike service — every larger town on the route has bike workshops. Carry a basic repair kit.
  • Weather — Polish weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Transport — Polish trains have bike carriages. Use them if you want to transport bikes between stages.

Trip plan

  1. Warmian-Masurian stage around the lakes
  2. Podlasie — Białowieża and Biebrza
  3. Finale in the Bieszczady and above the San

Tip

Don't plan to ride the entire route in one season. Divide it into 3–4 stages of 500–700 km each and complete them over successive years — that way you can truly enjoy the route.
