WEST POMERANIA

Szczecin Lagoon — Waters on the Border

2 days
Home›West Pomerania›Szczecin Lagoon

Islands, fishing harbor towns, and wide open horizons in western Poland. Slow travel through the Szczecin Lagoon — where the river meets the sea.

Why the Szczecin Lagoon, differently

The Szczecin Lagoon is a special place — an area where the fresh water of rivers mixes with the salinity of the Baltic, and land dissolves into thousands of islands and peninsulas. It is one of the few places in Poland where you can feel what a lagoon landscape looks like — wide horizons, water to the horizon, fishing cutters and waterfowl. This is a land for those who seek space and peace.

Slow travel through the Szczecin Lagoon is a celebration of the horizon. Instead of rushing from attraction to attraction, sit on the promenade in Świnoujście, watch the ships passing and simply breathe. This is a region that rewards the patient — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: fishermen at work, birds on the water, changing light. The Szczecin Lagoon is a land for those who want to truly rest.

What to see

The Szczecin Lagoon splits into several parts: Wolin Island (with Wolin NP), Uznam Island (with Świnoujście), the city of Szczecin (with its port), and the surroundings of Stepnica and Kamień Pomorski (south of the lagoon). Three to four days are best to visit.

Wolin Island and Wolin National Park

  • Wolin National Park — a park on Wolin Island, protecting cliffs, coastal pine forests and unique geological formations. The park's symbol is the bison — the largest wild land animal in Europe. The park has a Bison Show Enclosure where you can see these animals up close.
  • Gosań Cliff — the highest cliff in Wolin NP (93 m), with views over the Baltic and surroundings. See it from a safe viewpoint — walking on the cliff is prohibited.
  • Kawcza Góra — the highest hill in the park, with a panorama of the entire island. Worth climbing at sunset.
  • Międzyzdroje — a town on Wolin Island, a popular resort with a promenade, pier and numerous guesthouses. Worth a promenade walk and a swim in the sea.
  • Wisełka — a small village in the middle of the island, with wooden cottages and the atmosphere of an old fishing settlement. Worth a walk.

Świnoujście and Uznam Island

  • Świnoujście — a city on 44 islands, with the tallest lighthouse in Poland (68 m). Worth climbing the lighthouse — a panorama of the whole city and surroundings.
  • Świnoujście Lighthouse — the tallest lighthouse on the Polish coast, with a viewpoint. Worth it for views and history.
  • City ferries — Świnoujście lies on islands, so ferries are part of public transport. Worth taking a ferry — it is one of the city's attractions.
  • Promenade in Świnoujście — a wide seaside promenade with numerous cafés and restaurants. Worth a walk.

Szczecin and surroundings

  • Szczecin — a port city on the Oder, with the Chrobry Embankment (a riverside boulevard), the Pomeranian Dukes' Castle and the Old Town. Worth a walk and sightseeing.
  • Chrobry Embankment — a ceremonial boulevard in Szczecin, with a view over the Oder and port. Worth a walk and photos.
  • Pomeranian Dukes' Castle — a Renaissance castle in the centre of Szczecin, today a cultural centre. Worth visiting.
  • Stepnica and Kamień Pomorski — smaller towns in the south of the lagoon, with fishing harbours and the atmosphere of old settlements. Worth a walk.

Lesser-known corners

  • Chrząszczewska Island — a small island in the southern part of the lagoon, accessible by ferry. Worth it for silence and nature.
  • Ptasi Raj Reserve — a waterfowl reserve east of Świnoujście. Worth it for birdwatching.
  • Karsiborska Kępa Reserve — a water and marsh bird reserve, one of the most important in the region. Worth it for birdwatchers.
  • Coastal Landscape Park — a park protecting coastal landscapes around the Szczecin Lagoon. Worth a walk.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk on the promenade and in the park.
  • July and August — peak season. Beaches and promenades crowded, but all attractions open. Come on a weekday.
  • September and October — second best. Warm sea, fewer crowds, autumn colours in the park. September is bird migration time.
  • November–March — winter on the lagoon can be harsh, but empty and quiet. Worth it for those who seek solitude.

Getting there

  • By car: from Szczecin to Świnoujście ~1.5 hours (S3), ~2.5 hours from Berlin, ~3 hours from Poznań.
  • By train: station in Świnoujście, with connections from Szczecin (~2 hours), Poznań (~4 hours), Warsaw (~6 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Szczecin and other cities.

Within Świnoujście, public transport is by ferry — part of the attraction. On Wolin Island, a car or bike is useful to explore the park and surrounding towns.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Świnoujście, the lighthouse and a promenade walk, plus one day in Wolin NP.

Three to four days is realistic for a first visit. One day for Świnoujście, one for Wolin NP, one for Międzyzdroje and Wisełka, one for Szczecin or Stepnica.

A week lets you explore the entire region — Świnoujście, Wolin Island, Uznam Island, Szczecin, the Kamień Lagoon and surroundings.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Fish from the Lagoon — herring, flounder, cod, eel. Smoked or fried, available in local fish shops.
  • Smoked eel — a regional delicacy, available in smokehouses in Świnoujście and on Wolin Island.
  • Duck with apples — a West Pomeranian classic, available in restaurants in Świnoujście.
  • Fish pierogi — a variant popular in coastal regions, available in restaurants.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in Szczecin and surroundings (Browar Podgórski, Browar Stara Komenda).
  • Coffee and cake — Świnoujście and Międzyzdroje have several good cafés, ideal for a break.

Accommodation: Świnoujście (largest choice, resort), Międzyzdroje (resort, near Wolin NP), Wisełka (small village, quiet), Szczecin (larger city, more options), Stepnica and Kamień Pomorski (smaller towns, quiet). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — in summer season (July–August) hotels and guesthouses in Świnoujście and Międzyzdroje must be booked 2–3 weeks in advance.
  • Ferries — in Świnoujście ferries are part of public transport (standard ticket). Check the schedule — they run every few minutes.
  • Lighthouse — lighthouse entry is paid. Go in the morning or evening to avoid crowds.
  • Cliffs — walking on cliffs is prohibited (risk of collapse). Use viewpoints.
  • Birds — waterfowl reserves are breeding habitats. Keep quiet and do not approach nests.
  • Weather — by the sea, it changes quickly. Even in July, bring a windproof jacket and hat.
  • Parking — Świnoujście and Międzyzdroje have paid parking zones. Large car parks at promenades and the lighthouse. Leave the car and walk or cycle.
  • Signal — good in cities, weaker on Wolin Island away from main trails.

Trip plan

  1. Ferry to Wolin Island
  2. Świnoujście Lighthouse
  3. Stroll along Międzyzdroje promenade

Tip

Take a walk along the wild beaches around Wolin Island. There you will find true peace and a sense of solitude that slow travel seekers look for.
