Biebrza National Park — The Largest Wetlands in Europe

2–3 days
Home›Podlasie›Biebrza National Park

A land of peat bogs, moose, and birds. The largest national park in Poland — no roads, no crowds, with wooden boardwalks over the marshes. Slow travel through Biebrza National Park.

Why Biebrza National Park, differently

Biebrza National Park is the largest national park in Poland — covering almost 600 km² of marshes, peat bogs, and floodplains of the Biebrza river. It is the most valuable wetland area in Central Europe, called Polish Amazonia because of the unique water labyrinth the river creates here. There are no asphalt roads or large settlements in the park — this is a land where nature rules by its own laws.

Slow travel through Biebrza is a journey through time that flows differently. Instead of rushing from viewpoint to viewpoint, it is worth stopping on a boardwalk over the marsh, sitting on an observation tower and simply watching. This is a park that demands patience — but rewards those who can slow down. In spring, the marshes fill with flocks of geese, cranes and wading birds, and their call carries over the horizon like a symphony.

What to see

The park splits into three basins: Lower (around Goniądz), Middle (the wildest) and Upper (around Sztabin). Two to three days are best to visit — the park route is long and attractions are scattered.

Boardwalks and observation towers

  • Długa Luka Boardwalk — the most famous route in the park, an over-a-kilometre boardwalk leading over peat bogs near Goniądz. Free entry, open all year. Come at dawn or dusk.
  • Śliwno-Waniewo Boardwalk — a boardwalk in the Narew Valley, slightly outside the park, but worth seeing. It leads over the Narew floodplains.
  • Observation tower in Goniądz — the tower offers a view over the whole marsh and Biebrza valley. A meeting place for birdwatchers from all over Europe.
  • Towers in Buda and Okrasin — smaller towers in the central part of the park. Worth it for quiet and less crowded places.
  • Viewpoint in Osowiec — a viewpoint at the park headquarters (Osowiec-Twierdza), with an exhibition about the park's nature.

Birds, moose and other animals

  • Birds — the park is a paradise for birds: over 270 species live here, including cranes, greylag geese, ruffs, aquatic warblers, greater spotted eagles. In spring and autumn the park becomes a resting place for thousands of migrating birds.
  • Moose — the park's symbol, the largest member of the deer family. Moose can be seen at dawn or dusk, when they emerge from the marshes to feed. Watch from a distance — do not approach.
  • Beavers and otters — inhabit the Biebrza river and its tributaries. Beavers build dams and lodges, creating habitats for other animals.
  • Observation with a guide — several local guides specialize in bird and moose observation. Worth hiring a guide — without one it is hard to spot the most interesting species.

Hiking and cycling trails

  • Hiking trails — the park has several hiking trails of varying difficulty. The most popular lead to boardwalks and observation towers.
  • Cycling routes — several cycling routes run through the park, including a section of Green Velo. Worth cycling, as distances are large, and a bike lets you see more than a car.
  • Kayak trip on the Biebrza — one of the most beautiful kayak routes in Poland. The river is calm, wild, surrounded by marshes and forests. The trip lasts 2–5 days, depending on the section.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time for birdwatching. The marshes fill with migrating birds, and spring greenery is intense. Book accommodation in advance — this is the peak birdwatching season.
  • July and August — fewer birds, but the park is in full bloom. Mosquitoes troublesome — bring repellent.
  • September and October — second bird peak (autumn migration), autumn colours on the marshes, fewer tourists. Worth a walk.
  • November–March — the park is quiet and peaceful. Most attractions (boardwalks, towers) are accessible, but tourist infrastructure (cafés, rentals) may be closed.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1 hour from Białystok (route 65), ~3 hours from Warsaw (S8 and route 65), ~4 hours from Gdańsk.
  • By train: station in Osowiec-Twierdza (on the Białystok–Ełk line) or in Grajewo. Then bus or taxi.
  • By bus: PKS from Białystok to Goniądz, Sztabin and other towns in the park.

Public transport in the park is sparse — a car or bike makes moving between attractions much easier. Plan your route in advance.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Długa Luka boardwalk, tower in Goniądz, birdwatching. Come with a guide.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for boardwalks and towers, one for birdwatching, one for kayaking or cycling.

A week lets you cycle the entire park, do a longer kayak trip and explore the surroundings (Białystok, Narew NP, Knyszyn Forest).

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Podlasie cuisine — kibiny, kartacze, potato babka. Worth trying in local inns and agritourism farms.
  • Fish from the Biebrza — pike, bream, tench. In restaurants in Goniądz and Osowiec.
  • Mushrooms — in season (August–October) local dishes with mushrooms.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee — Goniądz has several cafés, ideal for a break.

Accommodation: Goniądz (largest choice, close to boardwalks), Osowiec-Twierdza (park headquarters, guesthouses), villages in the park (agritourism, quiet), Białystok (~1 hour, more options). In the birdwatching season (April–May, September–October) book ahead.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — guides and places at agritourism farms should be booked in advance, especially in birdwatching season. Guided tours are not mandatory but very helpful.
  • Tickets — park entry is free, but some attractions (towers, viewpoint in Osowiec) may have small fees. Check before your visit.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — the park is marshland, mosquitoes and ticks are active from May to October. Repellent, long trousers and socks — essential. Consider a face mosquito net.
  • Clothing — humidity is high, even summer can be cool. Rain jacket and sturdy shoes (rubber boots okay) — essential.
  • Binoculars — without binoculars you will not see most birds. Rent or buy before your trip.
  • Patience — Biebrza demands time. Come for several days and slow down to truly feel this place.
  • Signal — good in Goniądz and Osowiec, weaker deep in the park.
  • Fuel and shopping — petrol stations in Goniądz, Grajewo and Białystok. Larger shopping in Białystok.

Trip plan

  1. Długa Luka boardwalk
  2. Observation towers in Goniądz
  3. Kayaking on the Biebrza

Tip

Come to Biebrza in May — it is the best month for birdwatching. The marshes are full of life then, and the trails are not yet crowded.
