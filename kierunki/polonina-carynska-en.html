Połonina Caryńska — A Trail for Rest and Broad Vistas

2 days
Home›Subcarpathia›Połonina Caryńska

The best mountain route for an unhurried, restorative climb with 360-degree Carpathian vistas. Slow travel to Połonina Caryńska.

Why Połonina Caryńska, differently

Połonina Caryńska is a place that teaches us that the road to the destination can be as satisfying as the destination itself. It is a trail that does not overwhelm with difficulty but invites you to mindfully discover the beauty of every step. It lies in the High Bieszczady, at 1,100–1,297 m, and is the second-largest alpine meadow in the Polish Bieszczady.

Slow travel to Połonina Caryńska is a celebration of the moment. A ridge walk across vast meadows is an experience that resets the mind. When you look at the 360-degree panorama of the Carpathians, you can feel how perspective changes and the problems that seemed enormous become mere details in the great picture of the world. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: rare plants, interesting rock formations, animal tracks.

What to see

Połonina Caryńska splits into several parts: the eastern ridge (with approach from Brzegi Górne), the western ridge (with a view over Połonina Wetlińska), the Prowcza valley (approach from Ustrzyki Górne) and Wyżniańska Pass. One day is enough to see it, but it is worth devoting a full day.

Ridge and panoramas

  • Połonina Caryńska ridge — the main route across the meadow, with views over the whole Bieszczady, the Tatras and Slovak mountains. Worth a walk at sunrise.
  • Kruhły Wierch (1,297 m) — the highest point of Połonina Caryńska, with a 360-degree panorama of the Carpathians. Worth it for the best views.
  • View of Połonina Wetlińska — the most recognisable view from Połonina Caryńska. Photograph at dawn or sunset.
  • Orłowicz Pass — a pass between Połonina Caryńska and Połonina Wetlińska (~1.5 hours). Worth it for ambitious hikers.
  • 360-degree panorama — from the meadow ridge you can see in all directions. Worth it for the most spectacular views in the Bieszczady.

Prowcza Valley and approaches

  • Prowcza Valley — the valley through which one of the approaches to Połonina Caryńska leads. Worth a walk with views.
  • Approach from Brzegi Górne — the most popular approach to Połonina Caryńska (~2 hours). Moderately difficult, requires fitness.
  • Approach from Ustrzyki Górne — alternative approach (~2.5 hours), less known than the Brzegi Górne route.
  • Approach from Wyżniańska Pass — the shortest approach (~1.5 hours), with a view over Połonina Wetlińska.
  • Trail to Wielka Rawka — a peak (1,307 m), accessible from Połonina Caryńska (~2 hours). Worth it for ambitious hikers.

Lesser-known corners

  • Rzeczyca Valley — a valley on the western side of the meadow, with wild nature. Worth it for ambitious hikers.
  • Trail to Mała Rawka — a peak (1,266 m), accessible from Połonina Caryńska (~1.5 hours). Worth it for lesser-known views.
  • View of Solina Lake — from some points on the meadow you can see Lake Solina. Worth it for panoramas.
  • Chapel on the meadow — a small chapel in the western part of the meadow. Worth it for history.
  • Nature reserve in the valley — a fragment of the valley under reserve protection. Entry only on marked trails.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. The meadow blooms, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people. September is red deer rutting season.
  • July and August — peak season. Meadow crowded, car parks full. If you must — pick a weekday and start at 6:00 AM.
  • Winter — Połonina Caryńska in winter is accessible but requires proper equipment (crampons, poles). The route may be icy.

Getting there

  • By car: ~4–5 hours from Kraków (via Nowy Sącz and Krosno), ~2 hours from Rzeszów, ~6–7 hours from Warsaw. Car park in Brzegi Górne or Ustrzyki Górne (paid, in season full from 8 a.m.).
  • By train: station in Zagórz or Sanok, with connections from Kraków (~4 hours), Rzeszów (~1.5 hours). Then PKS bus to Brzegi Górne or Ustrzyki Górne.
  • By bus: PKS from Rzeszów and Sanok to Brzegi Górne and Ustrzyki Górne. Services sparse — check timetables.

Public transport in the Bieszczady is sparse — a car makes moving between trailheads much easier.

How much time you need

A full day (5–7 hours) is the minimum for a leisurely visit to Połonina Caryńska.

A weekend (2 days) lets you add Połonina Wetlińska or Tarnica.

A week lets you explore the whole High Bieszczady — Połonina Caryńska, Połonina Wetlińska, Tarnica, Wielka Rawka.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Proziaki — soda bread pancakes, served with garlic butter or lard. A Bieszczady classic.
  • Fuczki — cabbage fritters, a Lemko dish. Worth trying in local taverns.
  • Kwaśnica — sour cabbage soup with meat. Warms you after a day on the trail.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms, available at taverns along the way.
  • Coffee and cake — Ustrzyki Górne and Brzegi Górne have several good cafés.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Ustrzyki Górne (largest choice, close to Tarnica), Brzegi Górne (close to the meadow), Wetlina (close to Połonina Wetlińska), Cisna (Bieszczady capital, atmosphere), PTTK shelters (cheap but book ahead).

Practical tips

  • Bookings — car parks in Brzegi Górne and Ustrzyki Górne fill by 8 a.m. in season. Arrive before eight.
  • Weather — Bieszczady weather changes fast. Even in July, it can rain. Pack a rain jacket, hat and gloves.
  • Trekking boots are essential on the trail across the meadow. The route is long but does not require climbing.
  • Crowds — Połonina Caryńska in July and August is crowded. Start at 6:00 AM to avoid the crowd.
  • Water and food — there is no shelter on the route, so bring your own food and water.
  • Mobile signal — poor in the Bieszczady, non-existent in some valleys. Download offline maps.
  • BdPN tickets — required on trails in Bieszczady National Park. Buy online or at entry kiosks (Brzegi Górne, Ustrzyki Górne).

Trip plan

  1. Start from Wyżniańska Pass
  2. Ridge walk across the meadow summits
  3. Overnight stay overlooking the Bieszczady mountains

Tip

Try starting your hike from Wyżniańska Pass very early in the morning. This way you will avoid the largest groups of tourists and experience the true silence of the mountain meadows.
