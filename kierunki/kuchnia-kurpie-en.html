Kurpie — Rejbak, Fafernuchy and Pierogi

2 days
Home›Regional cuisine›Kurpie

Kurpie cuisine is dishes made of potatoes, flour, and mushrooms. Simple, rural, and still alive in the homes of the Kurpie people. Slow travel through Kurpie cuisine.

Why Kurpie cuisine, differently

Kurpie cuisine is the cuisine of forest people — dishes made of potatoes, flour, mushrooms, and game. Simple, hearty, and still alive in homes in Kurpie, where traditional recipes are passed down from generation to generation. Kurpie is an ethnographic region in Masovia, in the Green Forest and White Forest, where for centuries lived people who made their living from the forest — gathering, hunting, beekeeping, and farming.

Slow travel through Kurpie cuisine is a celebration of taste and tradition. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a Kurpie farmstead, in a village inn, in an agritourism farm. Each dish has its own story, its own cook, its own village. Ask where the ingredients come from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Kurpie cuisine splits into several categories: potato dishes (rejbak, pancakes, pierogi), flour dishes (fafernuchy, dumplings), mushroom dishes (soups, sauces), game dishes (boar, roe deer, red deer), liqueurs (krupnik, herbal liqueurs). Exploring the culinary region of Kurpie takes 1–2 days.

Potato dishes

  • Rejbak — a regional classic. Potato pancakes with flour and cracklings, baked on a sheet. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce. Best at Kurpie farmsteads around Kadzidło and Myszyniec.
  • Potato pancakes — in the Kurpie version with marjoram and bacon. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a dish that appears on almost every table in Kurpie. Dried, pickled, fried mushrooms — all gathered in the Kurpie Forest and used in local cuisine throughout the year.
  • Pierogi with potatoes and curd cheese — popular in Kurpie, served with cracklings or sour cream.

Flour and sweet dishes

  • Fafernuchy — sweet cookies made of flour, honey, and spices, baked in Kurpie for centuries. Traditionally served at Easter and Christmas.
  • Kurpie dumplings — dumplings made of flour and water, served with mushroom or meat sauce. A simple, filling dish.
  • Kurpie kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Baked for weddings and family celebrations.
  • Kurpie gingerbread — gingerbread with honey and spices, baked for holidays.

Mushrooms, game and liqueurs

  • Kurpie mushroom soup — soup from dried mushrooms with groats or noodles. Warms you on cold days.
  • Mushroom sauce — sauce from dried mushrooms, served with dumplings, pierogi, or meats.
  • Boar in mushroom sauce — roast or stewed boar with mushroom sauce. Served with dumplings or potatoes.
  • Roe deer with juniper — roast roe deer with juniper and herbs. Served with modra kapusta and dumplings.
  • Kurpie liqueurs — krupnik, herbal liqueurs, honey liqueurs. Available at Kurpie farmsteads.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal produce, long days. Worth a walk through the forest.
  • July–September — season for fresh vegetables, fruit, mushrooms. Worth it for markets and culinary festivals.
  • September–October — season for mushrooms, pumpkins, plums. September and October are good times for mushroom picking in the Kurpie Forest.
  • November–March — season for bigos, żur, krupnik. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Kurpie cuisine is from the eastern region of Masovia — best to travel by car, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Ostrołęka, Myszyniec (station nearby), Chorzele have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~2 hours from Warsaw (S8 and DK61), ~2.5 hours from Olsztyn, ~2.5 hours from Białystok.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (1–2 days) is the minimum — Kadzidło or Myszyniec (Kurpie farmsteads, rejbak, fafernuchy) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Kadzidło, Myszyniec, Ostrołęka, Green Forest.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of Kurpie — all the most important dishes and places.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose Kurpie farmsteads and village inns. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Rejbak — a Kurpie classic, available at Kurpie farmsteads around Kadzidło.
  • Fafernuchy — sweet cookies, available at shops with local products.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a Kurpie classic, available at every farmstead.
  • Mushroom soup — soup from dried mushrooms, available at farmsteads and inns.
  • Mushroom sauce — sauce from dried mushrooms, available at local restaurants.
  • Kurpie liqueurs — krupnik, herbal liqueurs, available at Kurpie farmsteads.

Accommodation: Kadzidło (small town, smaller choice), Myszyniec (small town, smaller choice), Ostrołęka (town, more options), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Kurpie farmsteads — usually open from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., longer in season. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants in city centres.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (baking fafernuchy, making rejbak, baking bread). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Kurpie cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Markets — the best place to buy local products (cheeses, honey, cold cuts, mushrooms).
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Rejbak in a Kurpie homestead
  2. Fafernuchy in Kadzidło
  3. Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms

Tip

Order rejbak at one of the Kurpie farmsteads around Kadzidło or Myszyniec — the most authentic place to try this dish in its traditional version.
