Suwałki Region — The Coolest Summer

3 days
Home›Podlasie›Suwałki Region

Glacial valleys, rolling hills, and open northern landscapes full of fresh air. Slow travel through the Suwałki Region — the most Scandinavian part of Poland.

Why the Suwałki Region, differently

The Suwałki Region is a land that somewhat resembles Scandinavia — it is raw, spacious and full of peace that soothes the nerves and clears the mind. It is the northernmost region of Poland, with the lowest average temperature in the country (hence the name the coolest summer), glacial hills and lakes that have retained a wild, pristine form.

Slow travel through the Suwałki Region is a journey through a landscape that in few places in Poland looks so untouched. Instead of rushing from attraction to attraction, it is worth stopping by a lake, walking into the forest, finding a country road that leads nowhere. The Suwałki Region has no great monuments or crowds of tourists — but it has something that is missing in other regions of Poland: genuine space and silence. This is a region for those who want to feel the raw beauty of nature.

What to see

The Suwałki Region splits into several parts: the surroundings of Suwałki and Wigry (with Wigry NP), the Augustów Primeval Forest (south), Lake Hańcza (the deepest in Poland), Stańczyki (railway bridges) and the surroundings of Sejny (east). Three to four days are best to visit.

Lake Hańcza and surroundings

  • Lake Hańcza — the deepest lake in Poland (108.5 m). The water is exceptionally clear, and the surroundings are wild. Worth sitting by it, especially at dawn when mists rise over the water.
  • Lake Hańcza Reserve — a reserve protecting the lake and surrounding forests. Entry only on marked trails.
  • Bachanowo — a village on Lake Hańcza, with accommodation base and trailhead. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Szeszupa Gorge — a gorge of the Szeszupa river through a moraine, with picturesque meanders and ravines. Worth a walk.

Wigry, Sejny and Stańczyki

  • Wigry National Park — a park around Lake Wigry, with a Camaldolese monastery on a peninsula, glacial lakes and wild trails. Worth it for contact with wild nature.
  • Camaldolese Monastery in Wigry — a Baroque monastery from the 17th century, one of the most valuable sacred monuments in Poland. Worth visiting with a guide.
  • Sejny — a small town in the east of the Suwałki Region, with a Baroque Dominican monastery, cathedral and atmospheric market square. Worth a walk.
  • Railway bridges in Stańczyki — two massive railway bridges from the early 20th century, called aqueducts of the Romincka Forest. Worth seeing — they impress.

Augustów Primeval Forest and Augustów Canal

  • Augustów Primeval Forest — one of the largest forest complexes in Poland, with lakes and wild trails. Worth a walk and bike ride.
  • Augustów Canal — a 19th-century canal connecting the Vistula with the Neman, with historic locks. Worth a walk and kayak trip.
  • Augustów — a town on the Augustów Canal, a popular resort. Worth it for accommodation base and promenade walk.
  • Lake Wigry — a glacial lake, around which Wigry NP extends. You can kayak, sail and fish.

Culture and tradition

  • District Museum in Suwałki — an exhibition about the history and culture of the region, including Maria Konopnicka and other famous residents.
  • Open-air museum in Sejny — an ethnographic museum presenting the traditional culture of the Suwałki village.
  • Shrines and roadside crosses — there are many in the Suwałki Region, often in picturesque spots. Pay attention on your hikes.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Greenery in full bloom, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk by the lake.
  • July and August — peak season. Lakes in full glory, but still fewer crowds than by the sea or in the Tatras. Worth it for swimming and water sports.
  • September — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people. September is mushroom season.
  • November–March — winter in the Suwałki Region is harsh (the coldest region in Poland), but beautiful. Lakes freeze, the landscape looks fairy-tale. For experienced travellers.

Getting there

  • By car: ~4 hours from Warsaw (S8 and route 61), ~1.5 hours from Białystok, ~5 hours from Gdańsk.
  • By train: stations in Suwałki and Augustów, with connections from Warsaw (~5 hours), Białystok (~2 hours), Gdańsk (~6 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Warsaw, Białystok and other cities.

Public transport in the region is sparse — a car or bike makes moving between attractions much easier.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Lake Hańcza and Stańczyki, or Wigry and Augustów.

Three to four days is realistic for a first visit. One day for Hańcza, one for Wigry and Sejny, one for Stańczyki and Augustów Primeval Forest, one for a quiet walk by a lake.

A week lets you explore the entire Suwałki Region — all lakes, forests, Augustów Canal, and even the Lithuanian side (if you have a visa or passport).

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Kartacze — potato dumplings with meat filling, a northeastern Polish classic.
  • Potato sausage (kiszka ziemniaczana) — a traditional rural dish.
  • Whitefish (sielawa and sieja) — fish from Suwałki lakes, smoked or fried.
  • Berry pierogi — seasonally (July–August), with berries picked in the forests.
  • Kvass (kwas chlebowy) — a traditional regional drink, available in local shops.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Suwałki (largest choice, but a city), Augustów (resort on the canal), Bachanowo (close to Hańcza), Wigry (close to the monastery), Sejny (in the east), agritourism farms in surrounding villages. Book ahead in season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — in summer season (July–August) book accommodation in advance, especially in Augustów and by Hańcza.
  • Weather — the Suwałki Region is the coldest region in Poland. Even in July, temperatures can drop to 15°C. Bring warm clothes and a rain jacket.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — forests and lakes are paradise for mosquitoes and ticks. Repellent and mosquito net — essential.
  • Water — you can swim in lakes and rivers, but the water is cold. Be prepared for lower temperatures than in other regions of Poland.
  • Bike — the Suwałki Region is an ideal region for cycling. Routes are well-marked, and distances between attractions are short.
  • Kayak — the Augustów Canal and the Czarna Hańcza river are among the most beautiful kayak routes in Poland.
  • Border — the Suwałki Region borders Lithuania. Carry an identity document if you plan to cross the border.
  • Car parks — paid car parks in Suwałki and Augustów. In smaller towns you can park for free.

Trip plan

  1. Lake Hańcza — Poland's deepest lake
  2. Historic railway bridges in Stańczyki
  3. Suwałki and regional culinary treats

Tip

Take a walk around the lakes early in the morning, when the sun is just emerging from behind the hills. That is when the Suwałki Region looks most magical.
