LESSER POLAND

Bochnia — Poland's Oldest Salt Mine

1 day
Home›Lesser Poland›Bochnia

The quieter sister of Wieliczka. Underground train, boat crossing, and a unique geological route. Slow travel through the Bochnia Salt Mine.

Why Bochnia, differently

The Bochnia Salt Mine is the oldest salt mine in Poland, founded in 1248. Though less famous than Wieliczka, it offers an experience that many find even more authentic — quieter, calmer, and with more space for contact with the history of mining. Inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2013.

Slow travel through Bochnia is a journey through the history of mining. The underground tourist route leads through galleries and chambers carved in salt over eight centuries. St. Kinga's Chapel, stables, extraction chambers — every element of this route tells the story of mining life that pulsed underground for centuries. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: tool marks on the walls, interesting salt formations, the history of people who worked here for generations. Bochnia is a place for those who seek authenticity without crowds.

What to see

The Bochnia mine is not just the tourist route itself — it is a whole complex of attractions: underground train, boat crossing, St. Kinga's Chapel, geological route, museum. Half a day is enough to see it, but it is worth devoting a full day.

Tourist route

  • Tourist route — a route through the most interesting fragments of the mine (~3 hours). Worth it for history and unusual experiences.
  • St. Kinga's Chapel — an underground chapel carved in salt, one of the most beautiful places in the mine. Worth it for architecture and the sacred.
  • Underground train — a ride through a narrow tunnel, in darkness, at a speed that lets you feel the distance from the surface. Worth it for an unusual experience.
  • Boat crossing — a short cruise on an underground lake, where water reflects light and salt. Worth it for an unusual experience.
  • Extraction chambers — chambers where salt was extracted, with an exhibition about mining techniques. Worth it for context.
  • Stables and forges — the mine's former stables and forges, where miners and horses worked. Worth it for history.

Geological route and museum

  • Geological route — a route through the most interesting geological fragments of the mine, with an exhibition about the history of the Earth. Worth it for geology enthusiasts.
  • Mine Museum — an exhibition about the history of the mine and region. Worth it for historical context.
  • Salt exhibition — a collection of salts from various eras, including pink and crystal salt. Worth it for details.
  • Reception hall — the mine's former reception hall, today a concert and banquet hall. Worth it for architecture.

Surroundings

  • Bochnia — a small town with a market square and church, ~1 km from the mine. Worth a walk and shopping.
  • Nowy Wiśnicz — the Kmita and Lubomirski Castle, ~10 km from Bochnia. Worth it for architecture.
  • Wieliczka Salt Mine — the second salt mine (UNESCO), ~30 km from Bochnia. Worth it for comparison.
  • Kraków — the regional capital, ~40 km from Bochnia. Worth it for accommodation base and culture.
  • Wiśnicz — a small town with a Carmelite monastery, ~12 km from Bochnia. Worth it for architecture.
  • Tarnów — a town with a historic market square, ~50 km from Bochnia. Worth a day trip.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth a walk around the town.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours in the region, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. The mine is crowded, ticket queues long, car parks full. If you must — pick a weekday and come in the morning.
  • November–March — the mine is less crowded in winter, but it is cold underground. Worth it for those who want to see the mine without crowds.

Getting there

  • By car: ~45 minutes from Kraków (A4), ~1 hour from Tarnów (A4), ~2 hours from Rzeszów (A4). Car park at the mine (paid, in season full from 10 a.m.).
  • By train: station in Bochnia, with connections from Kraków (~40 minutes), Rzeszów (~2 hours), Tarnów (~30 minutes).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Kraków, Tarnów, Rzeszów.

Within Bochnia the centre is compact and most sights are within walking distance. The mine is in the town centre.

How much time you need

Half a day (4–5 hours) is the minimum for a visit to the mine and a walk around the town.

A full day lets you add a walk around Bochnia, a museum visit and lunch in a local restaurant.

A weekend (2 days) lets you combine Bochnia with Nowy Wiśnicz or Wieliczka.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Lesser Poland cuisine — pierogi, żurek, duck with apples. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Kraków obwarzanek — Kraków's symbol, also available in Bochnia.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms in the region.
  • Grilled oscypek — smoked sheep cheese with cranberry, available at shepherd huts.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Bochnia has several good cafés, especially around the market square.

Accommodation: Bochnia (closest to the mine, smaller choice), Nowy Wiśnicz (~10 km, smaller choice), Kraków (~40 km, more options), Tarnów (~50 km, more options). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — tickets for the mine are worth booking in advance, especially in season. The tourist route has a limited number of places.
  • Temperature — the mine has a constant temperature (~14°C). Dress in layers, even in summer.
  • Shoes — sturdy shoes are essential on the underground route (uneven surfaces, stairs).
  • Underground train — the train is available on selected days. Check the schedule before your trip.
  • Boat crossing — the cruise on the underground lake is an extra attraction not available in Wieliczka. Worth taking.
  • Crowds — the mine is less crowded than Wieliczka, but in season (July–August) come in the morning or on weekdays.
  • Parking — the car park at the mine is paid. In season it fills from 10 a.m.
  • Signal — good throughout the town and the mine (outside the underground).

Trip plan

  1. Descent by the Campi shaft
  2. Underground boat crossing
  3. Geological route with salt exhibition

Tip

Choose the route with the boat crossing. This is an extra attraction not available in Wieliczka, and truly worth it — especially for children and those who enjoy unusual experiences.
