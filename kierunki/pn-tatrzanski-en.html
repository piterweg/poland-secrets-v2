Tatra National Park — The Highest Mountains in Poland

2–3 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Tatra National Park

Tatra National Park is Poland's only alpine mountain range. Granite peaks, crags, dwarf pine. Slow travel in the Tatras.

Why it's worth it

Tatra National Park is the oldest national park in Poland (established 1954) and the only mountain range in Poland with an alpine character. It lies on the border with Slovakia, covering the entire Polish Tatras — about 22% of the whole range. The landscape is unique: granite peaks, gullies, glacial cirques, dwarf pine, alpine meadows, and tarns. The highest peak is Rysy (2,499 m) — the highest point in Poland. Add to that valleys: Five Polish Lakes Valley, Kościeliska Valley, Chochołowska Valley — among the most beautiful in Europe.

Slow travel through Tatra National Park is a journey through a landscape that demands effort. The park is not easy — trails are steep, rocky, and the weather changes rapidly. But that is precisely why the views are so rewarding. It is worth spending 3–5 days here to truly feel this landscape. The best way to explore the park is on foot — there are about 275 km of hiking trails of varying difficulty. Public transport from Zakopane is excellent (minibuses, trains).

What to see

Tatra National Park is divided into three main parts: the High Tatras (south), the Western Tatras (west), and the Chochołowska Valley (north). Exploring the park takes 3–5 days.

High Tatras

  • Rysy — the highest peak in Poland (2,499 m). Worth it for views, but requires fitness.
  • Morskie Oko — the largest Tatra lake, with a classic view of Mnich. Worth it for views.
  • Five Polish Lakes Valley — a valley with five lakes, one of the most beautiful places in the Tatras. Worth it for views.

Western Tatras

  • Giewont — a distinctive peak with a cross, the symbol of Zakopane. Worth it for views.
  • Kościeliska Valley — the most beautiful valley in the Western Tatras, with caves. Worth it for views.
  • Mroźna Cave — a cave in the Kościeliska Valley, open to tourists. Worth it for nature.

Chochołowska Valley

  • Chochołowska Valley — the longest valley in the Tatras, with shepherd huts and sheep. Worth it for the landscape.
  • Chochołowska Clearing — a clearing in the middle of the valley, with shepherd huts. Worth it for culture.
  • Witów village — a village at the entrance to the valley, with a wooden church. Worth it for architecture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, fewer tourists. Beware of snow in higher sections.
  • July–August — season for high-mountain trails. Worth it for peaks, but crowded in Zakopane and at Morskie Oko.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people.
  • November–April — the Tatras in winter are accessible only to experienced tourists. Worth it for skiers and mountaineers.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2 hours from Kraków (DK47), ~3 hours from Katowice (DK47), ~5.5 hours from Warsaw (S7 + DK47).
  • By train: Zakopane has a railway station, with connections from Kraków and Warsaw.
  • By bus: PKS and private carriers from Kraków and Katowice.

Tatra National Park is best explored on foot — the park is well marked, and public transport from Zakopane is excellent. Leave the car at the car park in Zakopane.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Morskie Oko, Kościeliska Valley.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Morskie Oko, Kościeliska Valley, Chochołowska Valley, Giewont.

A week lets you explore the whole park — from Rysy to Giewont, with numerous stops.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Kwaśnica — sour cabbage soup, a highlander classic.
  • Oscypek — smoked sheep cheese, available at shepherd huts.
  • Bryndza — sheep cheese, available at shepherd huts.
  • Trout from the Dunajec — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Beer from Podhale breweries — several craft breweries in the region.

Accommodation: Zakopane (town, more options), Bukowina Tatrzańska (village, guesthouses), Kościelisko (village, agritourism), Witów (village, agritourism).

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the Tatras are demanding, take a guide on harder trails.
  • Weather — Tatra weather changes rapidly. Check the forecast before setting out and bring a rain jacket.
  • Shopping — buy oscypek, bryndza, and honey directly from highlanders.
  • Tickets — park entry tickets are mandatory. Buy online in advance.
  • Safety — do not attempt high-mountain trails without experience. Consider travel insurance.
  • Signal — weak in higher sections. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Morskie Oko
  2. Five Lakes Valley
  3. Kasprowy Wierch and Świnica

Tip

Head to the Five Polish Lakes Valley early in the morning — the shelter opens at 7:00, and the trail is empty and quiet then.
