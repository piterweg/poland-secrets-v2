Augustów Canal — By Bike Along a 19th-Century Canal

2–3 days
Home›Podlasie›Augustów Canal

Locks, the Augustów Primeval Forest, and lakes. A cycling route with the soul of old engineering. Slow travel along the Augustów Canal.

Why the Augustów Canal, differently

The Augustów Canal is one of the most interesting hydrotechnical structures in Poland — a 19th-century canal that connected the Vistula with the Neman, and today is a route for both kayakers and cyclists. Historic locks, wooden bridges, old ports — all this creates a unique trail that combines history with nature. The canal is ~100 km long (in Poland ~80 km), with 18 locks.

Slow travel along the Augustów Canal is a celebration of 19th-century engineering details. Each lock has its own history, each bridge — its own significance, each section of the canal — its own story. Instead of rushing the route in one day, divide it into stages of 20–30 km per day. Accommodation in Augustów, agritourism farms along the canal, campsites — all this makes the route comfortable, and you can really ride it slow. This is a land that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: traces of old builders, interesting technical solutions, the unique nature of the Augustów Primeval Forest.

What to see

The Augustów Canal splits into several parts: northern (from Augustów to Przewięź Lock), central (from Gorczyca Lock to Mikaszówka Lock) and southern (from Perkuć Lock to Kurzyniec Lock). Two to three days are best to visit.

Locks and infrastructure

  • Przewięź Lock — one of the most interesting locks of the canal, with an original 19th-century mechanism. Worth seeing up close.
  • Gorczyca Lock — a lock with wooden gates, one of the most picturesque. Worth it for details.
  • Mikaszówka Lock — the largest lock of the canal, with interesting architecture. Worth it for engineering.
  • Perkuć Lock — a lock deep in the Augustów Primeval Forest, surrounded by forest. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Kurzyniec Lock — a lock in the southern part of the canal. Worth it for history.
  • Augustów Canal Museum — an exhibition about the history of the canal and its builders. Worth it for context.

Augustów and surroundings

  • Augustów — a town on the Augustów Canal, a popular resort. Worth it for accommodation base and a walk along the promenade.
  • Promenade in Augustów — a promenade along the canal, with numerous cafés and restaurants. Worth a walk.
  • Lake Necko — a lake in Augustów, with a beach and water equipment rental. Worth it for swimming and water sports.
  • Augustów Primeval Forest — one of the largest forest complexes in Poland, with lakes and wild trails. Worth it for walks and cycling.
  • Studzieniczna — a Marian sanctuary on an island in Lake Studzieniczne, ~5 km from Augustów. Worth it for architecture and the sacred.

Lesser-known corners

  • Swoboda Lock — a smaller lock, less known than the main ones. Worth it for less crowded places.
  • Mikaszówka village — a village around the lock, with wooden architecture. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Kuriańskie Bagno Reserve — a peat bog reserve in the Augustów Primeval Forest. Worth a walk.
  • Frącki village — a village on the canal, with old infrastructure. Worth it for history.
  • Lake Studzieniczne — a lake on the canal route, with a sanctuary on an island. Worth it for views.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, long days, stable weather. Worth cycling.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours in the Augustów Primeval Forest, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. The canal is crowded, kayaks need booking, prices higher. Worth it for swimming in the lakes.
  • November–March — the canal in winter is quiet and harsh. Worth it for those who seek solitude.

Getting there

  • By car: ~3 hours from Warsaw (S8 and DK16), ~1.5 hours from Białystok (DK8 and DK16), ~4 hours from Gdańsk (S7 and DK16).
  • By train: station in Augustów, with connections from Warsaw (~4 hours), Białystok (~2 hours), Gdańsk (~5 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Warsaw, Białystok and other cities.

Public transport in the region is sparse — a car or bike makes moving much easier. A well-marked cycling route runs along the canal.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one day cycling along the canal (~40 km), one for a walk in Augustów and a lake cruise.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Two days cycling (stages of 30–40 km), one day for Augustów and surroundings.

A week lets you ride the whole canal — from Augustów to the Belarusian border, with numerous stops and overnight stays at agritourism farms.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Podlasie cuisine — kibiny, kartacze, potato babka. Worth trying in local inns.
  • Lake fish — whitefish, bream, pike. Smoked or fried.
  • Buckwheat pierogi — a regional variant popular in Podlasie.
  • Beer from a local brewery — several craft breweries in the region.
  • Coffee and cake — Augustów has several good cafés.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Augustów (largest choice, resort), villages along the canal (agritourism, quiet, cheaper), campsites on the canal (for those who want to sleep in a tent), Studzieniczna (sanctuary, quiet).

Practical tips

  • Bike — the cycling route along the canal is well-marked. Rent a bike in Augustów.
  • Bookings — book campsites and agritourism farms along the canal in advance in summer season.
  • Kayak — kayak rentals operate in Augustów and along the canal. Book a kayak in advance for longer trips.
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Mosquitoes and ticks — the Augustów Primeval Forest is paradise for mosquitoes and ticks. Repellent and mosquito net — essential.
  • Signal — good in Augustów, weaker deep in the Augustów Primeval Forest.
  • Border — the canal ends at the border with Belarus. Carry an identity document.

Trip plan

  1. Augustów — Przewięź Lock
  2. Gorczyca Lock and Studziany Las
  3. Finale in Augustów on the Netta

Tip

Take the route on a weekday, outside the season. That is when the canal is quiet, the locks are less crowded, and you can truly feel its 19th-century character.
