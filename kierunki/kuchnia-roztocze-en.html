Roztocze — Pierogi with Bryndza and Cebularze

2–3 days
Home›Lublin Region›Roztocze

The cuisine of Roztocze is a mix of Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish traditions. Simple, earthy, and deeply filling. Slow travel through Roztocze cuisine.

Why Roztocze cuisine, differently

The cuisine of Roztocze is borderland cuisine — where Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish influences meet. This is visible in the dishes: pierogi with bryndza (sheep cheese), cebularze with onion, krupnik, groats, potatoes. All simple, earthy, and deeply filling dishes. Roztocze is a region in southeastern Poland, on the border of the Lublin region and Subcarpathia, where for centuries Poles, Ukrainians, Jews, and Armenians lived side by side.

Slow travel through Roztocze cuisine is a celebration of taste and tradition. Instead of rushing from restaurant to restaurant, stop in one longer — in a village inn, in an agritourism farm, in a small restaurant in Zamość or Zwierzyniec. Each dish has its own story, its own cook, its own village. Ask where the ingredients come from, how the dish is prepared, who passed down the recipe. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more flavors you can truly get to know.

What to see

Roztocze cuisine splits into several categories: flour dishes (cebularz, pierogi with bryndza, pierogi with cabbage), potato dishes (pancakes, dumplings, babka), meat dishes (duck, trout), mushroom dishes (soups, sauces), baked goods (kołocz, cheesecake). Exploring the culinary region of Roztocze takes 1–2 days.

Flour dishes

  • Lublin cebularz — one of the most recognizable dishes of the region. Flat yeast dough with onion and poppy seeds, sold at markets and bakeries throughout the Lublin region. Worth trying for breakfast, with fresh butter.
  • Pierogi with bryndza — delicate pierogi with sheep cheese, a specialty of the Polish-Ukrainian borderland. Served with cracklings or sour cream.
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms — a traditional Lenten dish, served at Christmas Eve. In the Roztocze version — with marjoram and pepper.
  • Pierogi with buckwheat groats — a regional variant, popular in Roztocze. Served with cracklings or onion.

Potato, meat, and fish dishes

  • Potato pancakes — in the Roztocze version with marjoram and bacon. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Roztocze dumplings — dumplings made of flour and potatoes, served with mushroom or meat sauce.
  • Potato babka — a baked dish of grated potatoes, bacon, onion, and eggs. Served with sour cream or mushroom sauce.
  • Roztocze trout — fresh, grilled or from the grate, with horseradish and lemon. One of the best dishes you can eat in the region.
  • Duck with apples — roast duck with apples and marjoram, a Roztocze classic. Available in elegant restaurants.

Baked goods and other specialties

  • Roztocze kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions. Baked for weddings and family celebrations.
  • Roztocze cheesecake — cheesecake with curd cheese, raisins, and orange peel. Available at pastry shops in Zamość.
  • Roztocze krupnik — barley groat soup with vegetables and meat. In the Lublin version — with dried mushrooms.
  • Beer from a local brewery — a craft brewery operates in Zwierzyniec (Browar Zwierzyniec).
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops. Inscribed on the EU protected products list.

When to go

  • April–June — the best time for slow travel. Fewer tourists, fresh seasonal produce, long days. Worth a walk through the villages.
  • July–September — season for fresh vegetables, fruit, mushrooms. Worth it for markets and culinary festivals.
  • September–October — season for mushrooms, pumpkins, plums. September and October are good times for mushroom picking in Roztocze forests.
  • November–March — season for bigos, krupnik, duck. Worth it for winter cuisine, especially during the holiday season.

Getting there

Roztocze cuisine is from the southeastern region of Poland — best to travel by car, connecting several towns in one trip.

  • By train: Zamość, Zwierzyniec, Hrubieszów have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities.
  • By car: ~1 hour from Lublin (DK17), ~2 hours from Rzeszów (DK19), ~4 hours from Warsaw (S17 and DK17).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

How much time you need

A weekend (1–2 days) is the minimum — Zamość or Zwierzyniec (Roztocze restaurants, cebularz, pierogi with bryndza) and surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Zamość, Zwierzyniec, Krasnobród, Susiec.

A week lets you explore the whole culinary region of Roztocze — all the most important dishes and places, and the neighbouring Lublin region.

What to eat and where to sleep

Instead of restaurants, choose village inns and agritourism farms. There, cooks prepare what they have known since childhood, and they do it best.

  • Lublin cebularz — a Roztocze classic, available at bakeries in Zamość and Lublin.
  • Pierogi with bryndza — a borderland classic, available at inns in Zamość.
  • Roztocze trout — fresh, grilled, available at local smokehouses in Zwierzyniec.
  • Duck with apples — a Roztocze classic, available in elegant restaurants.
  • Roztocze kołocz — sweet yeast cake, available at pastry shops in Zamość.
  • Korycin cheeses — smoked and aged, available in local shops.

Accommodation: Zamość (town, more options), Zwierzyniec (small town, smaller choice), Krasnobród (spa, more options), Susiec (village, quiet), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, home-cooked food).

Practical tips

  • Village inns — usually open from 12 p.m. to 9 p.m., longer on weekends. Check hours before your trip.
  • Regional restaurants — look for places where cooks prepare traditional recipes. Avoid tourist restaurants in city centres.
  • Culinary workshops — many farms and agritourism businesses offer workshops (baking cebularze, making pierogi, baking bread). Book ahead.
  • Diet — Roztocze cuisine can be heavy and filling. Eat smaller portions to try more dishes.
  • Markets — the best place to buy local products (cheeses, honey, cold cuts, mushrooms).
  • Weather — weather in the region can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Lublin cebularz for breakfast
  2. Pierogi with bryndza in Zamość
  3. Roztocze trout in a local smokehouse

Tip

Order trout at one of the local smokehouses in Zwierzyniec or Krasnobród — fresh, straight from a local farm, served with horseradish and lemon.
