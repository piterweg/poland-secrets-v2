LESSER POLAND

Magura National Park — Low Beskids Without Crowds

1–2 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Magura National Park

Magura National Park is one of the youngest parks in Poland. Beech forests, wolves, bears. Slow travel through the Lower Beskid.

Why it's worth it

Magura National Park was established in 1995 — it is one of the youngest national parks in Poland. It lies in the Lower Beskid, on the border of the Małopolska and Subcarpathian voivodeships. It protects the most valuable fragments of the Lower Beskid — extensive Carpathian beech forests, which are among the most important forest types in Poland. Wolves, bears, lynxes, and wildcats live in the park — it is one of the largest refuges for large predators in Poland. Add to that numerous birds of prey: lesser spotted eagle, goshawk, sparrowhawk.

Slow travel through Magura National Park is a journey through a landscape that surprises with its wildness. The park is not as well known as the Tatras or Bieszczady, so crowds do not reach here. It is worth spending 2–3 days here to truly feel this landscape. The best way to explore the park is on foot — there are about 60 km of hiking trails, including nature paths. Public transport is weak, so rent a car or bike.

What to see

Magura National Park is divided into three main parts: the Magura Małastowska massif (west), the Wisłoka valley (centre), and the Magura Wątkowska massif (east). Exploring the park takes 2–3 days.

Magura Małastowska massif

  • Magura Małastowska — the highest peak in the park (813 m), with a viewpoint. Worth it for views.
  • Folusz village — a village by the park, seat of the park management. Worth it for information.
  • Krempna village — a village with a wooden church and an Orthodox church. Worth it for architecture.

Wisłoka valley

  • Wisłoka — the river, its valley is the most valuable element of the park. Worth it for kayaking.
  • Żydowskie village — a village with an Orthodox church and preserved Lemko architecture. Worth it for culture.
  • Ożenna village — a village with an Orthodox church and a former border crossing to Slovakia. Worth it for history.

Magura Wątkowska massif

  • Magura Wątkowska — the second-highest peak in the park (846 m), with a viewpoint. Worth it for views.
  • Huta Polańska village — a village with an Orthodox church and a former glassworks. Worth it for history.
  • Bartne village — a village with two Orthodox churches and preserved Lemko architecture. Worth it for culture.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Warm, green, few tourists.
  • July–August — warm, but crowded around Krempna.
  • September–October — second best. Autumn colours, fewer people, mushroom picking in the forests.
  • November–March — the park is hard to reach in winter. Worth it for those seeking silence.

Getting there

  • By car: ~2.5 hours from Kraków (DK28), ~2 hours from Rzeszów (DK28), ~5 hours from Warsaw (S7 + DK28).
  • By train: Gorlice, Jasło, Nowy Sącz have railway stations. Then rent a car.
  • By bus: PKS serves most larger villages, but connections are sparse.

Magura National Park is best explored by car or on foot — the park is vast, and public transport is weak.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — Folusz, Krempna, Magura Małastowska.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Folusz, Krempna, Magura Małastowska, Magura Wątkowska, Bartne.

A week lets you explore the whole park and surrounding Beskids — from Gorlice to Komańcza.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Pierogi with bryndza — regional pierogi with sheep cheese, available at inns.
  • Kwaśnica — sour cabbage soup, a Lemko classic.
  • Trout from the Wisłoka — from local farms, available at inns.
  • Proziaki — flatbreads of flour and sour milk.

Accommodation: Gorlice (town, more options), Krempna (village, agritourism), Folusz (village, guesthouses), numerous agritourism farms in villages.

Practical tips

  • Guided tours — the park has rich fauna, take a guide.
  • Wolves and bears — large predators live in the park. Keep quiet and do not leave food.
  • Shopping — buy honey, cheese, and cold cuts directly from farmers.
  • Opening hours — some trails are closed during the bird breeding season. Check before your trip.
  • Weather — weather in the Lower Beskid can be changeable, even in July bring a jacket.
  • Signal — weak in many parts of the park. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Nature trail in Krempna
  2. Orthodox church in Bartne
  3. Views from Magura Wątkowska

Tip

Climb Magura Małastowska early in the morning — on a clear day you can see the Tatras, and the trail is rarely used.
