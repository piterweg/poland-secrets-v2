Babia Góra National Park — Queen of the Beskids, Protected

1–2 days
Home›Lesser Poland›Babia Góra National Park

The highest peak of the Beskids and the oldest nature reserve in Poland. Diablak, Markowe Szczawiny, and a forest that remembers the Ice Age. Slow travel through Babia Góra National Park.

Why Babia Góra National Park, differently

Babia Góra National Park protects the highest peak of the Beskids — Babia Góra (1,725 m), called Diablak. This is a place where the weather changes in the blink of an eye, and the wind can surprise even experienced hikers. The height and exposure give Babia Góra an almost alpine climate — in winter it is covered with a thick layer of snow, and in summer it can be cooler than the Tatras.

Slow travel on Babia Góra means giving up the marathon in favour of a hike. Instead of running up to the summit and back down, plan the day so that you stop on the ridge, listen to the wind, eat a sandwich with a view of the Tatras. This is a mountain that rewards the patient — for the effort of the ascent it gives one of the most beautiful views in Poland. Babia Góra teaches humility — and that is its greatest value.

What to see

The park splits into two main zones: the strict zone (reserve, entry only on marked trails) and the active protection zone (with Zawoja, the open-air museum and educational paths). One to two days are best to visit.

Diablak summit and trails

  • Diablak (1,725 m) — the highest peak of the Beskids and the entire Outer Carpathians. The summit is rocky, with a distinctive outcrop. Views of the Tatras, Silesian Beskids, Żywiec Beskids, and in good weather Slovak mountains. Plan the ascent at dawn — then a chance for a sea of clouds and a beautiful panorama.
  • Trails to Diablak — two main approaches: from Zawoja (Krowiarki) and from Zawoja (Markowe Szczawiny). The route from Krowiarki (~3 hours) is shorter but steeper. The route from Markowe Szczawiny (~2.5 hours) is longer but gentler. Choose according to your fitness.
  • Perć Akademików — the most difficult trail to Babia Góra, leading through rock steps and chains. Requires experience and proper equipment. In winter and in wet weather — very dangerous.
  • PTTK Shelter at Markowe Szczawiny — a shelter at 1,180 m, with traditional atmosphere and home cooking. A good base for the summit and a place to rest.

Strict reserve and forests

  • Strict reserve — a fragment of old forest that remembers the Ice Age. Firs, beeches and spruces grow here, some over 300 years old. Entry only on marked trails.
  • Lower montane forest — forest covering the lower parts of Babia Góra. Rich in tree and plant species, including rare ones. Worth a walk.
  • Clearings and meadows — clearings and mountain meadows on the slopes of Babia Góra, with rich vegetation. In spring and summer, rare plant species bloom here, including orchids and crocuses.

Zawoja and educational paths

  • Zawoja — the largest village in Poland, situated at the foot of Babia Góra. There is an open-air museum, a park museum and an information point. Worth a walk and learning about the region's history.
  • Open-air museum in Zawoja — an ethnographic museum presenting the traditional culture of the Babia Góra highlanders. Wooden cottages, tools, folk costumes. Worth it for understanding the region.
  • Educational path in Zawoja — a path around Zawoja, leading through various habitats. Good for families with children.
  • Carmelite Monastery in Zawoja — a 19th-century monastery, today a retreat house. Worth seeing from the outside and visiting the chapel.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for the summit. Trails are open, days are long, weather is stable. Note: snow may still lie in the higher parts.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer crowds. First snow usually in the second half of October.
  • July and August — peak season. Diablak trail crowded, car parks full. If you must — pick a weekday and start at 6:00 AM.
  • Winter — Babia Góra in winter is harsh and demanding. Strong wind, blizzards, temperatures below -15°C. Requires experience and proper equipment (crampons, ice axe, winter jacket). For skiers — slopes in Zawoja.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1.5 hours from Kraków (via Rabka and Skawa), ~1 hour from Zakopane, ~1.5 hours from Bielsko-Biała.
  • By train: nearest station is Sucha Beskidzka (~30 km from Zawoja). Then bus or taxi.
  • By bus: PKS from Kraków, Zakopane and Sucha Beskidzka to Zawoja and Krowiarki.

Public transport in the region is sparse — a car makes moving between trailheads (Krowiarki, Markowe Szczawiny, Zawoja) much easier.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one day for the ascent to Diablak, one for Zawoja and the surroundings.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. Diablak, Markowe Szczawiny, Perć Akademików (for experienced hikers), the open-air museum in Zawoja and educational paths.

A week lets you explore the entire region — Babia Góra, Żywiec Beskids, Silesian Beskids and Orava. Worth it for those who want to combine mountains with culture.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Kwaśnica — highland sour cabbage soup with meat. A Beskid cuisine classic, available at the shelter on Markowe Szczawiny.
  • Grilled oscypek — smoked sheep cheese with cranberry. Worth trying at local shepherd huts.
  • Podhale bryndza — sheep cheese, served with bread and honey.
  • Karpielówka and śliwowica — local liqueurs, available in regional taverns.
  • Grilled trout — from local farms, available in restaurants in Zawoja.
  • Coffee and cake — Zawoja has several cafés, ideal for an afternoon break.

Accommodation: Zawoja (largest choice, guesthouses, agritourism farms), Markowe Szczawiny (PTTK shelter), Krowiarki (small settlement with guesthouses), around Sucha Beskidzka (cheaper, but further). Book ahead in season, especially on weekends.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — book accommodation at the Markowe Szczawiny shelter in advance, especially in season.
  • Park tickets — entry to Babia Góra NP is free on trails, but car parks are paid (Krowiarki, Markowe Szczawiny).
  • Weather — Babia Góra is treacherous. Even in July, snow can fall above 1,500 m, and wind can exceed 100 km/h. Check the forecast before setting out.
  • Shoes — sturdy trekking boots are essential on the Diablak trail. In higher parts it can be slippery (stones, roots, snow).
  • Water and food — no water points on the trail. Bring a supply of water and food. The shelter at Markowe Szczawiny is the only place with a hot meal.
  • Safety — do not go alone into higher parts. In winter and in bad weather, the Diablak trail is very dangerous. Go with a guide or in a group.
  • Signal — good in Zawoja, weaker on trails. Have an offline map.
  • Car parks — Krowiarki and Markowe Szczawiny have paid car parks. In season they fill by 8 a.m.

Trip plan

  1. Summit Diablak at dawn
  2. Markowe Szczawiny mountain hut
  3. Educational trails in Zawoja

Tip

Check the weather forecast before setting out. Babia Góra can be treacherous — even in summer it can surprise you with fog, wind, and rain. Take a rain jacket even on a hot day.
