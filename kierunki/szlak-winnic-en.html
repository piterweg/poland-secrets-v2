LUBUSZ AND LESSER POLAND

Vineyard Trail — Polish Wine from the Source

4–6 days
Home›Lubusz›Vineyard Trail

A route through Polish vineyards — from Zielona Góra to the Lesser Poland Vistula Gorge. Tastings, workshops, evenings among the vines. Slow travel along the Wine Trail.

Why the Wine Trail, differently

Polish wine is experiencing a renaissance. Though winemaking in Poland has a long tradition — dating back to the Middle Ages — it was forgotten for centuries. Today it is returning, and Polish vineyards produce wines that win awards at international competitions. The Wine Trail is a proposal for those who want to get to know Polish wine directly at the source — in vineyards, in cellars, among the vines.

Slow travel along the Wine Trail is a celebration of taste and landscape. Instead of rushing from vineyard to vineyard, stop in one longer — tour with the owner, understand the production process, try different vintages. Each vineyard has its own history, its own grape varieties, its own secrets. Ask, learn, try. This is a journey that rewards those who slow down — the longer you are in one place, the more you can truly understand about Polish wine.

What to see

The Wine Trail splits into several sections: Zielona Góra and surroundings (oldest wine region in Poland), Lesser Poland Vistula Gorge (Sandomierz, Kazimierz Dolny), Lower Silesia (vineyards in the Sudetes), Subcarpathia (vineyards in the Foothills). Riding the whole route takes 4–6 days.

Zielona Góra and Lubusz region

  • Zielona Góra — the capital of Polish winemaking, with numerous vineyards around the city. Worth it for the atmosphere and tastings.
  • Grape Harvest in Zielona Góra — the largest wine festival in Poland, held in September. Worth it for the event.
  • Lubusz Vineyard — one of the oldest vineyards in the region, with traditions dating back to the 19th century. Worth it for history and tastings.
  • Vineyard Under the Forest — a family vineyard near Zielona Góra, offering tastings and workshops. Worth it for the experience.
  • Wine Museum in Zielona Góra — an exhibition about the history of winemaking in Poland. Worth it for context.
  • Palm House in Zielona Góra — one of the largest in Poland, with a collection of exotic plants. Worth it for contrast.

Lesser Poland Vistula Gorge

  • Sandomierz — a town on seven hills, with numerous vineyards nearby. Worth it for architecture and tastings.
  • Kazimierz Dolny — the artists' town on the Vistula, with vineyards nearby. Worth it for the atmosphere and tastings.
  • Sandomierz Vineyard — one of the oldest vineyards in the region, with a view over the Vistula. Worth it for tastings.
  • Vineyard by the Ravine — a family vineyard near Sandomierz, offering tastings and workshops. Worth it for the experience.
  • Kresy Vineyard — a vineyard near Kazimierz Dolny, with a view over the Vistula gorge. Worth it for tastings.
  • Janowiec Castle — a Renaissance castle on the Vistula, near vineyards. Worth it for architecture.

Lower Silesia and Subcarpathia

  • Jaworek Vineyard — a vineyard in the Sudetes, with a view over the Karkonosze. Worth it for tastings and views.
  • Miękinia Vineyard — a vineyard near Wrocław, with traditions dating back to the 19th century. Worth it for history and tastings.
  • Srebrna Góra Vineyard — a vineyard in the Sudetes, near the Srebrna Góra fortress. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Jasło Vineyard — a vineyard in Subcarpathia, with a view over the Low Beskids. Worth it for tastings.
  • Sanok Vineyard — a vineyard in the Bieszczady, near Sanok. Worth it for the atmosphere.
  • Dwór Sanna Vineyard — a vineyard in Kazimierz Dolny, with a historic manor. Worth it for architecture and tastings.

When to go

  • May and June — the best time for slow travel. Vines bloom, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk through the vineyards.
  • September and October — grape harvest time. Work is underway in the vineyards, wines are freshest, many vineyards organize open days. Worth it for events.
  • July and August — peak season. Vineyards crowded, but all attractions open. Worth it for outdoor tastings.
  • November–March — vineyards in winter are less accessible (some closed). Worth it for those who want to see vineyards without crowds, but check opening hours.

Getting there

The Wine Trail runs through the whole country — you can start anywhere. Best to travel by car, connecting several vineyards in one trip.

  • By car: the trail runs through main national roads. Plan the route to connect several vineyards.
  • By train: Zielona Góra, Sandomierz, Kazimierz Dolny (station in Puławy), Wrocław have railway stations, with connections from most Polish cities. Then rent a car or bike.
  • By bus: PKS and private operators serve most towns on the route.

The Wine Trail is best explored by car — vineyards are scattered over a large area, and public transport between them can be sparse. Plan the route to connect 3–4 vineyards in one day.

How much time you need

A weekend (2 days) is the minimum — one wine region (e.g. Zielona Góra, Lesser Poland Vistula Gorge or Lower Silesia).

Three to four days is realistic for a first visit. You can see 3–4 wine regions, trying local wines and taking part in workshops.

A week lets you explore the whole trail — all the most important vineyards in Poland. This is a big challenge, but satisfying.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Regional cuisine — in each region you can try local dishes: duck with apples (Lower Silesia), pierogi (Lesser Poland), cebularz (Lublin region).
  • Local wines — Polish wines, worth trying at vineyards and restaurants.
  • Beer from local breweries — several craft breweries in each region.
  • Coffee and cake — every larger town on the route has several good cafés.
  • Local products — cheeses, honey, cold cuts, available at agritourism farms and regional shops.

Accommodation: guesthouses and hotels in towns on the route, vineyards with accommodation (some offer guest rooms), agritourism farms in villages (quiet, cheaper), palaces and manor houses (for those seeking a unique place).

Practical tips

  • Tastings — in most vineyards, tastings require booking in advance. Call or email.
  • Workshops — many vineyards offer workshops (grape harvest, wine production, tasting). Book in advance.
  • Shopping — buy directly at vineyards. It is not only a souvenir but also support for local producers.
  • Transport — after wine tastings, do not drive. Have a plan B (driver, taxi, overnight stay on site).
  • Opening hours — many vineyards are open only in season (May–October) and only on selected days. Check hours before your trip.
  • Weather — Polish weather can be changeable. Even in July, bring a rain jacket.
  • Signal — good in towns, weaker in villages. Have an offline map.

Trip plan

  1. Vineyards around Zielona Góra
  2. Lesser Poland Vistula Gorge — Sandomierz and Kazimierz
  3. Vineyards in the Sudetes and Lower Silesia

Tip

Plan the route for September — it is grape harvest time, when work is underway in the vineyards and the wines are freshest. Many vineyards organize open days then.
