Cieszyn — A Town on the Border

1 day
Home›Silesia›Cieszyn

Cieszyn Venice, the Three Brothers Well, and the Friendship Bridge. Coffee on one side of the Olza, dessert on the other. Slow travel through Cieszyn — a town divided by a river.

Why Cieszyn, differently

Cieszyn is one of the most extraordinary towns in Poland — it lies on the border with the Czech Republic, divided by the Olza river into two parts: Polish Cieszyn and Czech Český Těšín. A walk through the town is a journey through two cultures at once, which lived side by side for centuries. This is a town where the border is just a formality — you can cross from one country to another in a few minutes.

Slow travel through Cieszyn is a celebration of small things. Instead of racing from monument to monument, sit in a café on the market square, drink coffee on one side of the Olza, then cross the bridge to the other side and have dessert in a Czech pastry shop. This is a town that rewards the patient — the longer you are here, the more details you notice: traces of two cultures, influences of Austrian architecture, the history of the division. Cieszyn is a land where history is still present in everyday life.

What to see

Cieszyn is compact — the main sights are within walking distance. One day is enough to see it, but it is worth staying longer to feel the atmosphere.

Market square, town hall and Three Brothers Well

  • Main Market Square — the heart of Cieszyn, surrounded by townhouses from different eras. In the centre, the town hall with a Classicist facade. Look at the townhouses — each has a different story.
  • Three Brothers Well — one of the most recognisable attractions of Cieszyn, with a sculpture of three brothers who, according to legend, founded the town. Worth sitting by it and looking at the details.
  • Town Hall — a Classicist building from the 19th century, in the centre of the market square. Worth seeing from the outside and stepping inside.
  • National House — a former hotel, today the seat of cultural institutions. Worth seeing from the outside.

Castle Hill and Rotunda

  • Castle Hill — a hill above the town, with castle ruins and a Romanesque rotunda. Worth climbing for views and history.
  • St. Nicholas Rotunda — a Romanesque rotunda from the 11th century, one of the oldest temples in Poland. One of the most valuable monuments of Romanesque architecture in Poland.
  • Piast Tower — the only remnant of the castle of the Dukes of Cieszyn. Worth climbing the tower — a view over the town and surroundings.
  • Cieszyn Castle — a former Habsburg castle, today the seat of cultural institutions. Worth seeing from the outside.

Cieszyn Venice and Friendship Bridge

  • Cieszyn Venice — a picturesque part of the town, where houses stand right by the water, and small bridges connect the banks of the Młynówka. A walk here is like a journey to Italy — with the difference that instead of Venetian canals we have a small river and Silesian architecture.
  • Friendship Bridge — a bridge over the Olza, a symbol of reconciliation and shared history. You can cross to the other side of the river, have coffee in the Czech Republic, and return to Poland — all within a few minutes.
  • Český Těšín — the Czech part of the town, with a market square, church and small-town atmosphere. Worth walking through it to feel the difference.

Museums and lesser-known corners

  • Museum of Cieszyn Silesia — one of the oldest museum institutions in Poland, with an exhibition about the region's history.
  • Museum of Protestantism — an exhibition about the history of Protestantism in Cieszyn Silesia.
  • Castle Park — a park around the castle hill, with alleys and old trees. Worth a walk.
  • Cieszyn Library — a historic library with valuable collections. Worth it for those interested.
  • Castle Chapel — a chapel in Cieszyn Castle, one of the region's sacred monuments.

Surroundings (day trips)

  • Ustroń — a spa in the Silesian Beskids, ~20 km from Cieszyn. Worth it for walks and mineral water pump rooms.
  • Wisła — a town at the source of the Vistula, ~30 km from Cieszyn. Worth it for walks and the ski jump.
  • Bielsko-Biała — a town with a historic old town, ~30 km from Cieszyn.
  • Ostrava (Czech Republic) — an industrial city with an interesting cultural scene, ~60 km from Cieszyn.

When to go

  • April and May — the best time. The town blooms, fewer tourists, long days. Worth a walk through Cieszyn Venice.
  • September and October — second best. Autumn colours, clear air, fewer people.
  • July and August — peak season. Market square crowded, but the town has summer energy. Come on a weekday.
  • December — Christmas market on the market square, with Polish and Czech accents. Beautiful but cold.

Getting there

  • By car: ~1.5 hours from Katowice (A1 and DK52), ~2 hours from Kraków (A4 and DK52), ~2.5 hours from Wrocław.
  • By train: station in Cieszyn, with direct connections from Katowice (~2 hours), Kraków (~3 hours), Wrocław (~3 hours).
  • By bus: PKS and private operators from Katowice, Kraków and other cities.

Within the town, the centre is compact and most sights are within walking distance. You can also cross to the Czech side by bridge — the border is open.

How much time you need

A weekend (1 day) is the minimum — market square, castle hill, Cieszyn Venice and a walk on the Czech side.

Three days is realistic for a first visit. One day for the town itself, one for the walls and castle hill, one for a trip (Ustroń, Wisła or Bielsko-Biała).

A week lets you explore the whole region — Cieszyn, Ustroń, Wisła, Bielsko-Biała and the Czech side.

What to eat and where to sleep

  • Silesian and Czech cuisine — in Cieszyn and Český Těšín you can try both cuisines. Worth comparing.
  • Silesian roulade — beef rolled with bacon, onion and cucumber, with Silesian dumplings.
  • Pierogi — in restaurants on the market square and side streets. Traditional and regional variants.
  • Silesian kołocz — sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions.
  • Beer from a local brewery — a craft brewery operates in Cieszyn (Browar Zamkowy Cieszyn).
  • Coffee and dessert in the Czech Republic — on the Czech side, try coffee and dessert in a local pastry shop.

Accommodation: market square and surroundings (most convenient, but expensive), streets adjacent to the centre (cheaper, close to attractions), private rooms and guesthouses (mid-price), Ustroń and Wisła (~20–30 km, more options, close to mountains). Book ahead in summer season.

Practical tips

  • Bookings — tickets for museums and the Piast Tower are worth booking in advance, especially in season.
  • Guided tours — Cieszyn's history is complex, and without context many places look similar. Worth going with a guide.
  • Border — the Polish-Czech border is open (Schengen), so you can cross freely. Carry an identity document.
  • Crowds — the market square and main attractions are crowded in season (May–September). Rise early and start sightseeing before the crowds.
  • Parking — the centre has a paid parking zone, spaces are hard to find. Large car parks at the market square, then a 5–10 min walk.
  • Signal — good throughout the centre (Polish and Czech).
  • Currency — in Poland złoty, in the Czech Republic koruna. Carry both currencies or use a card.

Trip plan

  1. Market square and the Three Brothers Well
  2. Castle Hill and the Rotunda
  3. Walk along the Friendship Bridge

Tip

Cross the Friendship Bridge to the Czech side. Coffee and dessert in Český Těšín is an experience that shows how close the two countries are to each other.
