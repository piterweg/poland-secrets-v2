Lower Silesian Cuisine — Silesian Heaven, Duck, and Dumplings

Lower Silesian cuisine is a mix of Polish, German, and Czech traditions. Discover what is worth eating in Wrocław and Lower Silesia — from Silesian Heaven to krupnioki, with specific places and prices.

Lower Silesian cuisine is the cuisine of a borderland region — Polish, German, and Czech influences meet here. This is visible in the dishes: dumplings, duck, Silesian Heaven, krupnik. All hearty dishes, deeply rooted in tradition and adapted to the region's cold climate. Lower Silesia belonged for centuries to Bohemia, Austria, and Prussia — and it shows on the plate. Instead of the lighter dishes of Lesser Poland cuisine, we have heavy, meaty dishes here, with plenty of dumplings, cabbage, and dried fruit. This is a cuisine that does not try to be fashionable. It is simply familiar.

This guide shows 8 dishes you must try in Lower Silesia, and specific places in Wrocław, Jelenia Góra, Wałbrzych, and Kłodzko where you can eat them in an authentic — not touristy — version.

Meat Dishes and Soups

1. Wrocław Duck

Roast duck with apples, marjoram, and caraway. It is served with modra kapusta (red cabbage) and Silesian dumplings. This is a classic of Lower Silesian cuisine that has graced Wrocław restaurant tables for generations. The duck is roasted slowly here — at least 2 hours — and served with its own sauce, made from the roasting juices. The tradition of roasting duck with apples came to us from German cuisine, but in Lower Silesia it gained its own character — the addition of marjoram and caraway gives it a distinctive, slightly bitter aroma.

Price: 60–90 PLN in regional restaurants, 40–55 PLN in village inns. Where: Karczma Lwowska (Wrocław, Rynek 4), Restauracja Jaś i Małgosia (Wrocław, ul. Świdnicka 8), Karczma Zagroda (Wrocław, ul. Ks. Skorupki 2).

2. Silesian Krupnioki

Sausage made of buckwheat groats and blood, with bacon and onion. Characteristic of Silesia and Lower Silesia. It is served hot, with mustard or onion — usually at markets and fairs, where it is fried on large pans. A simple dish, but deeply rooted in the region's tradition. In the “grilled krupniok” version, it is a popular snack at Lower Silesian festivals.

Price: 10–18 PLN per portion. Where: Nowy Targ market (Wrocław), Christmas markets, local butcher shops (e.g., Sklep Mięsny Kowalczyk, Wrocław, ul. Kłodnicka 32).

3. Silesian Dumplings

Round potato dumplings with a characteristic indentation in the middle (hence the name: “dumplings with a hole”). They are served with meats, sauces, and goulash. The secret of good dumplings is the right proportions of boiled and raw potatoes and potato flour. Traditionally, they are made from grated raw potatoes mixed with boiled ones, formed into balls, and a hole is made with a finger — this is not only aesthetics but also technique, thanks to which the dumplings cook evenly.

Price: 12–20 PLN per portion as a side. Where: every regional restaurant in Lower Silesia — Wrocław, Wałbrzych, Jelenia Góra.

4. Wrocław Pierogi

A variation of classic pierogi — filled with meat, cabbage and mushrooms, or bryndza. In the Wrocław version, curd cheese and finely chopped onion are added to the filling, which gives the pierogi a more creamy consistency. They are served with crackling or sour cream. This is a dish that combines the Polish tradition of pierogi with the German preference for fattier, creamier additions.

Price: 20–35 PLN per portion (8–10 pieces). Where: Pierogarnia Stary Młyn (Wrocław, ul. Wita Stwosza 5), Pierogarnia Swojska (Wrocław, ul. Ruska 51), Karczma Młyńska (Wrocław, ul. Młyńska 16).

5. Lower Silesian Krupnik

Barley groat soup with vegetables and meat. In the Lower Silesian version — with dried mushrooms and sour cream. Hearty, warming, perfect for cold days. The buckwheat groat variant (“buckwheat krupnik”) is also popular in the region, especially in the Sudetes, where it is often served in mountain huts in winter. Krupnik is one of those dishes that taste better the next day — worth ordering a double portion and taking it home.

Price: 12–20 PLN per bowl. Where: Bierhalle (Wrocław, Rynek 32), Karczma Lwowska (Wrocław), Sudeten mountain huts (Jakuszyce, Szklarska Poręba).

Sweets, Bakes, and Holiday Dishes

6. Silesian Heaven

A traditional Lower Silesian dish — dried fruit (plums, apples, pears) cooked with spices, served with dumplings or potatoes. A sweet-sour flavor that perfectly complements meat. The dish is also known in Silesia, but in Lower Silesia it has its own specificity — more cinnamon and cloves are added here. “Silesian Heaven” accompanies Christmas Eve and holidays — it is served hot, as an addition to roast meat or as a standalone dish.

Price: 15–25 PLN per portion. Where: regional restaurants during the holiday season, Karczma Lwowska (Wrocław), Karczma Młyńska (Wrocław).

7. Makiełki

A traditional Lower Silesian dish — pasta with poppy seeds, honey, dried fruit, and nuts. It is served at Christmas Eve and holidays. Sweet, filling, deeply traditional. Unlike kutia (which is made with groats), makiełki are made with pasta — hence their specific, somewhat “dumpling-like” consistency. This tradition came to Lower Silesia from German cuisine (“Mohnnudeln”), but settled here for good.

Price: 15–25 PLN per portion. Where: regional restaurants during the holiday season.

8. Lower Silesian Village Bread

Sourdough bread made from rye and wheat flour, with caraway and flaxseed. Characteristic of Lower Silesian villages, still baked in home ovens. Lower Silesian bread has a dark, moist crumb and a crispy crust — thanks to the long fermentation of the sourdough (up to 24 hours). In many villages it is still baked in bread ovens, once a week — usually on Saturday.

Price: 8–15 PLN per loaf. Where: rural markets, directly from bakers in Kłodzko, Bystrzyca Kłodzka, Lądek-Zdrój.

Where to Eat — Specific Places

Wrocław (Ostrów Tumski, Market Square, Nadodrze):

  • Karczma Lwowska (Rynek 4) — a classic of Lower Silesian cuisine, operating since 1999. Duck, krupnik, Silesian Heaven.
  • Karczma Młyńska (ul. Młyńska 16) — home-style Lower Silesian cuisine in a family version.
  • Pierogarnia Stary Młyn (ul. Wita Stwosza 5) — the best Wrocław pierogi.
  • Bierhalle (Rynek 32) — krupnik, duck, beer from a local brewery.

Region (Jelenia Góra, Wałbrzych, Kłodzko):

  • Karczma Zagroda (Wrocław, ul. Ks. Skorupki 2) — rural Lower Silesian cuisine, authentic, without fireworks.
  • Gospoda Pod Jeleniem (Jelenia Góra, ul. 1 Maja 20) — regional Sudeten dishes, including duck and dumplings.
  • Restauracja Charlotta (Wałbrzych, ul. Rynek 12) — Lower Silesian cuisine in a modern version.

Sudetes (agritourism farms and mountain huts):

  • Schronisko Jakuszyce (Szklarska Poręba) — krupnik, makiełki, village bread in a mountain-hut version.
  • Agritourism farm in Lądek-Zdrój — home-style Lower Silesian cuisine, Wrocław duck with dumplings.

Practical Tips

  • Restaurants on the Market Square in Wrocław are more expensive and touristy. Go 2 streets further — to Nadodrze, Ostrów Tumski, Kazimierza Wielkiego — where the food is cheaper and better.
  • Buy krupnioki at markets — the best are at Nowy Targ in Wrocław (Tuesdays, Fridays, Saturday mornings).
  • Village bread can be found in shops with regional products, but the best is directly from bakers in smaller towns — especially in the Sudetes.
  • During the holiday season (Christmas Eve, Easter) restaurants serve Silesian Heaven and makiełki. This is a good time to visit Lower Silesia — the cuisine is at its most traditional then.
  • Wrocław duck takes time (2 hours of roasting). Book a table in advance, especially on weekends.
  • In the Sudetes, the cuisine is more rustic — more dumplings, cabbage, and meat. This is a good destination for those seeking authenticity, not modern finesse.

Summary

Lower Silesian cuisine is the cuisine of a borderland — hearty, deeply rooted in tradition, adapted to the cold climate. Instead of fashionable dishes, you will find classics here: duck, dumplings, krupnik, Silesian Heaven. Plan your trip to Lower Silesia to try at least three of the eight dishes — and preferably eat them in places where the cuisine is still home-style, not touristy.
