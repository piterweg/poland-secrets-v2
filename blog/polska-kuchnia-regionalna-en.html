Polish Regional Cuisine — 10 Dishes You Must Try

Kibiny, oscypek, kartacze, roulade — Polish cuisine is not just pierogi and pork chops. It is a dozen different regional cuisines, each with its own history.

Polish cuisine is unfairly reduced to pierogi, pork chops, and bigos. Meanwhile, it is a dozen different regional cuisines, each with its own traditions, ingredients, and flavors. Here are 10 dishes you must try at least once in your life.

1. Kibiny (Podlasie)

Tatar meat pastries, baked or fried. A classic of Podlasie cuisine that has survived for centuries. Best at milk bars in Białystok.

2. Oscypek (Podhale)

Smoked sheep's cheese, formed into characteristic spindles. Served grilled, with cranberry, straight from the shepherd. The taste of the mountains that cannot be faked.

3. Kartacze (Podlasie)

Large potato dumplings stuffed with meat. Hearty, substantial, rural. A dish that appears at every wedding in the region.

4. Silesian Roulade (Silesia)

Beef rolled with bacon, onion, and cucumber, served with Silesian dumplings and modra kapusta. A classic of working-class cuisine.

5. Pierogi with Bryndza (Roztocze)

Delicate pierogi with sheep cheese — a specialty of the Polish-Ukrainian borderland. Served with cracklings or sour cream.

6. Kwaśnica (Podhale)

Highland sauerkraut soup with meat. Warms from the first sip. Perfect after a day in the mountains.

7. Lublin Cebularz (Lublin Region)

Flat yeast dough with onion and poppy seeds. Sold at markets and bakeries throughout the region. Best for breakfast.

8. St. Martin's Croissant (Greater Poland)

Sweet croissant with white poppy seeds, dried fruit, and almonds. Baked only on November 11th. Protected by the EU as a regional product.

9. Smoked Vendace (Warmia-Masuria)

Delicate, fatty fish from Masurian lakes. Traditionally smoked in home smokehouses. Tastes completely different from the store version.

10. Proziaki (Lemko Region)

The simplest of Lemko flatbreads — made of flour, sour milk, and baking soda. Served with garlic butter or lard.

How to Eat Them?

Best — in the place they come from. In a small inn, milk bar, agritourism farm. Regional cuisine tastes best where it is part of everyday life, not a tourist attraction.
