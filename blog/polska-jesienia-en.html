Poland in Autumn — 7 Places That Look Like a Fairytale

Polish autumn is golden leaves, mists over lakes, and empty trails. Here are 7 places that particularly delight at this time of year.

Autumn is the best season for slow travel in Poland. Crowds disappear, prices drop, and the landscape turns into a spectacle of colors and light. Here are 7 places that particularly delight in autumn.

1. Bieszczady — Golden Mountain Meadows

The grasses on the mountain meadows turn copper and russet in autumn, and the views become even more spacious. It is the best time for hiking — without heat, without crowds, with crystal-clear air.

2. Roztocze — Cascades and Pine Forests

The pine forests of Roztocze smell of resin and damp moss in autumn. The Szumy cascades on the Tanew look particularly picturesque then — the water reflects golden leaves, and the trail is almost empty.

3. Karkonosze — Mists over Śnieżka

Autumn in the Karkonosze is mists, damp forests, and dramatic views. The trail to Śnieżka on a cold October day is an experience that stays in memory — especially when the clouds part over the ridge.

4. Masuria — Mists over Lakes

Masurian lakes are covered in autumn with mist that rises over the water at dawn. A kayak, silence, and only the splash of the paddle — what more could you need?

5. Białowieża Forest — Gold and Red

The primeval forest turns into a spectacle of colors in autumn. Bison, deer, boars — all animals come out of the thicket in search of food. It is the best time for observation.

6. Barycz Valley — Birds and Mists

Autumn is the time of bird migration. The Milicz Ponds fill with cranes, geese, and ducks. It is worth bringing binoculars and spending the whole day on an observation tower.

7. Kraków — Polish Golden Autumn

Kraków in autumn is warmer, calmer, and more reflective than in the middle of the season. The Planty park is covered with golden leaves, cafés fill with people reading books, and museums are less crowded.

How to Prepare?

An autumn trip requires a good jacket, shoes, and an umbrella. It is also worth planning the route flexibly — the weather in Poland in autumn can be capricious. But it is precisely this unpredictability that makes autumn trips so special.
