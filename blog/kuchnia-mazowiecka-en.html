Masovian Cuisine — Flaki, Krupnik, and Liver

Masovian cuisine is a mix of rural and Warsaw traditions. Find out what is worth eating in Warsaw and Masovia.

Masovian cuisine is the cuisine of a region where rural and urban traditions meet. On one hand, simple rural dishes — krupnik, groats, potatoes — on the other, refined Warsaw cuisine — flaki, liver, duck. All this makes one of the more interesting regional cuisines in Poland.

1. Warsaw Flaki

A classic of Warsaw cuisine — beef tripe soup with vegetables, marjoram, and paprika. It is served with a bread roll or bread. A labor-intensive dish, but deeply rooted in the capital's tradition.

2. Masovian Krupnik

Barley groat soup with vegetables and meat. In the Masovian version — with dried mushrooms and sour cream. Hearty, warming, perfect for cold days. A variant with buckwheat groats is also popular in Masovia.

3. Poultry Liver

Fried poultry liver with onion and apple. It is served with potatoes and salad. A classic of Warsaw cuisine that has graced Warsaw restaurant tables for generations.

4. Masovian Duck

Roast duck with apples, marjoram, and caraway. It is served with modra kapusta and dumplings. In the Masovian version — with dried plums and honey.

5. Pierogi with Cabbage and Mushrooms

Traditional pierogi, which in Masovia have their own specificity — the filling is sweeter, with dried plums and onion. They are served at Christmas Eve, weddings, and family celebrations.

6. Jewish-Style Carp

A traditional Warsaw dish, derived from Jewish cuisine. Carp in jelly with raisins, almonds, and spices. It is served at Christmas Eve and holidays.

7. Apple Racuchy

Yeast pancakes with apples, fried in a pan. Popular in Masovia as a dessert or afternoon tea. They are served with powdered sugar or sour cream.

8. Masovian Bread

Sourdough bread made from rye and wheat flour. Characteristic of Masovian villages, still baked in home ovens. It is served with all dishes, especially with butter and local honey.

Where to Eat?

In Warsaw, it is worth visiting restaurants in the Old Town, Powiśle, and Śródmieście — especially places serving Warsaw cuisine. In smaller Masovian towns — local inns and regional restaurants. In Płock, Ciechanów, and Pułtusk — restaurants in the old market squares.
