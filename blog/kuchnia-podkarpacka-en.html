Subcarpathian Cuisine — Proziaki, Fuczki, and Hreczanyki

Subcarpathian cuisine is a mix of Polish, Ukrainian, and Lemko traditions. Find out what is worth eating in the Bieszczady and Subcarpathia.

Subcarpathian cuisine is borderland cuisine — Polish, Ukrainian, Lemko, and Boyko influences meet here. This is visible in the dishes: proziaki, fuczki, hreczanyki, pierogi with bryndza. All simple, rural, and deeply filling dishes, based on flour, potatoes, cabbage, and cheese.

1. Proziaki

The simplest of Subcarpathian flatbreads — made of flour, sour milk, and baking soda, baked on a sheet. They are served with garlic butter, lard, or curd cheese. In the sweet version — with sugar and cream. A classic of Subcarpathian rural cuisine.

2. Fuczki

Pancakes with sauerkraut, fried in a pan. A Lemko dish, available in the Bieszczady and Low Beskids. They are served with sour cream or mushroom sauce. Simple, filling, deeply rural.

3. Hreczanyki

Buckwheat groat pancakes with curd cheese and eggs, fried in a pan. Characteristic of Subcarpathian cuisine — especially in the version with mushroom sauce. A vegetarian dish, deeply rooted in tradition.

4. Pierogi with Bryndza

Delicate pierogi with sheep cheese — a specialty of the Polish-Ukrainian borderland. They are served with cracklings or sour cream. Bryndza gives them a characteristic, slightly salty flavor.

5. Sauerkraut Soup

Sour soup based on sauerkraut with meat, dried mushrooms, and sour cream. It warms and fills — perfect for cold days. In the Subcarpathian version — with barley or millet groats.

6. San Trout

Trout farmed in local farms in the San valley. Fresh, grilled or from the grate, with horseradish and lemon. One of the best dishes you can eat in the region.

7. Subcarpathian Kwaśnica

Sour soup based on sauerkraut with meat — a variant similar to the Podhale version, but with its own specificity. In the Subcarpathian version — with dried mushrooms and juniper.

8. Pierogi with Cabbage and Mushrooms

Traditional pierogi, which in Subcarpathia have their own specificity — the filling is more spicy, with marjoram and pepper. They are served at Christmas Eve, weddings, and family celebrations.

Where to Eat?

In Rzeszów, it is worth visiting restaurants in the center and in Staromieście. In the Bieszczady — local inns, agritourism farms, and taverns, especially in Cisna, Wetlina, Ustrzyki Górne. In Przemyśl and Sanok — regional restaurants. In smaller villages — agritourism farmsteads, where the cuisine is most authentic.
