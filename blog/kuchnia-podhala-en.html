Podhale Cuisine — Oscypek, Kwaśnica, and Bryjka

Podhale cuisine is the cuisine of shepherds — thick, hearty, based on milk, cheese, cabbage, and potatoes. Find out what is worth trying in the mountains.

Podhale cuisine is the cuisine of mountain people — shepherds who for centuries grazed sheep on mountain meadows and lived on what the mountain gave them. That is why it is simple, thick, and hearty — based on milk, cheese, cabbage, potatoes, and meat.

1. Oscypek

Smoked sheep cheese — the icon of Podhale. Formed into characteristic spindles, with a characteristic pattern on the surface. It is served grilled, with cranberry, or in the classic version — with mulled wine. The best is straight from the shepherd, in a shepherd's hut on the mountain meadow.

2. Kwaśnica

A highland soup based on sauerkraut with meat — usually ribs or bacon. It warms from the first sip. It is served with bread and potatoes. Perfect after a day in the mountains — especially on cold days.

3. Bryjka

A dish of corn flour, cooked in water or milk. It is served with cranberry, butter, or cracklings. In the traditional version — savory, with bryndza. In the modern version — sweet, with fruit.

4. Moskole

Potato pancakes baked on a sheet, often with flour and eggs. They are served for breakfast or as a side for soups. In the highland version — with bryndza, lard, or sour cream.

5. Bundz

Fresh sheep cheese, not smoked — unlike oscypek. Delicate, slightly sour, perfect for sandwiches with horseradish or honey. It is served in shepherd's huts throughout the pastoral season.

6. Roast Lamb

A traditional Easter dish in Podhale. Lamb roasted with herbs, garlic, and marjoram, served with potatoes and cabbage. In the highland version — with smoked lard and juniper.

Where to Eat?

The best Podhale food can be found in shepherd's huts on mountain meadows, in highland taverns, and in guesthouses run by local hosts. In Zakopane, avoid restaurants in the center — instead look for small, family places on the outskirts of the city.
