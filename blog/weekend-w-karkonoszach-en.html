Weekend in the Karkonosze — A 3-Day Plan

The Karkonosze are the most dramatic mountains in Poland — raw, windy, and full of contrasts. Here is a 3-day plan to see the most beautiful corners of the region.

The Karkonosze are some of the most interesting mountains in Poland — raw, unpredictable, and full of drama. In three days, you can see the most important places: Śnieżka, the Snowy Cirques, Samotnia, and the waterfalls. Here is a weekend plan.

Day 1 — Śnieżka and Karpacz

Start with the ascent to Śnieżka (1,603 m) — the highest peak of the Karkonosze and the entire Sudetes. The route from Karpacz via Samotnia and Dom Śląski takes about 4–5 hours. At the summit there is a meteorological observatory, the Chapel of St. Lawrence, and a mountain hut — it is worth resting and drinking hot tea.

In the evening, return to Karpacz and walk through the center. It is worth seeing Wang Temple — a wooden church from the 12th century, moved from Norway in the 19th century. It is one of the most interesting places in the region.

Day 2 — Snowy Cirques and Szklarska Poręba

In the morning, drive to Szklarska Poręba and enter the Snowy Cirques — glacial depressions that look like mountain amphitheaters. The route leads through beautiful forests and offers views of the entire Karkonosze. On the way, it is worth stopping at Samotnia — the mountain hut over Mały Staw, one of the most beautifully situated in Poland.

In the afternoon, descend to Szklarska Poręba and walk through the center. It is worth seeing Kamieńczyk Waterfall — one of the highest waterfalls in the Karkonosze, and Szklarka Waterfall — the second highest.

Day 3 — Karpacz and Surroundings

The last day is a walk through Karpacz and its surroundings. It is worth seeing the Łomnica Dam, the Karkonosze Municipal Museum, and the Cable Car to Kopa — a chairlift that leads to the summit of Kopa (1,377 m).

In the afternoon, return home. If you have time, stop in Jelenia Góra — a picturesque town at the foot of the Karkonosze, with a beautiful market square and old town.

Where to Sleep?

The best bases are Karpacz and Szklarska Poręba — both towns have extensive accommodation, restaurants, and tourist infrastructure. It is worth choosing a guesthouse or hotel in the center to be close to the trails and attractions.

What to Eat?

Karkonosze cuisine is a mix of Polish, Czech, and German cuisines. It is worth trying kwaśnica (cabbage soup), Silesian dumplings, duck with apples, and local cheeses. In Karpacz and Szklarska Poręba there are many regional restaurants.

Practical Tips

The Karkonosze are one of the windiest places in Europe — the weather changes quickly here. Bring a rain jacket, warm layers, and sturdy shoes, even in summer. In winter, the trails require crampons and appropriate experience.
