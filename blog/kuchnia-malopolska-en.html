Lesser Poland Cuisine — Obwarzanek, Żurek, and Makowiec

Lesser Poland cuisine is a mix of Kraków, Podhale, and rural traditions. Find out what is worth eating in a region where East and West meet.

Lesser Poland cuisine is one of the richest regional cuisines in Poland. It combines the burgher tradition of Kraków, the pastoral tradition of Podhale, the rural tradition of the Low Beskids, and the influence of Jewish cuisine from Kazimierz. That is why it is so diverse — from refined dishes to simple rural fare.

1. Obwarzanek Krakowski

The symbol of Kraków, inscribed on the EU protected products list. Sold on every street corner, best freshly baked — sprinkled with salt, poppy seeds, or sesame. It is a mandatory stop on every visit to Kraków.

2. Kraków Żurek

Sour soup with rye leaven, served with white sausage, egg, and horseradish. In the Kraków version — in a hollowed-out bread loaf. It warms and fills, perfect for cold days.

3. Kraków Makowiec

Sweet yeast cake with poppy seeds, dried fruit, and icing. Served at holidays and family celebrations. A symbol of Lesser Poland home baking, available in every pastry shop in the region.

4. Dry Kraków Sausage

A ripening cold cut, made of pork and beef, with garlic and pepper. Inscribed on the EU protected products list. Served cold, as a snack or with bread.

5. Podhale Bryndza

Sheep cheese from Podhale, produced using traditional methods. Inscribed on the EU protected products list. Served with cranberry, bread, and honey. In the pastoral version — in shepherd's huts on mountain meadows.

6. Lemko Fuczki

Pancakes with sauerkraut, fried in a pan. A Lemko dish, available in small bars in Kazimierz and the Low Beskids. Simple, filling, deeply rural.

7. Duck with Apples

A traditional Kraków dish, served in elegant restaurants. Roast duck with apples, marjoram, and dumplings. In the modern version — with raspberry or cranberry sauce.

8. Pierogi with Bryndza and Cranberry

A variation of pierogi with sheep cheese filling — a specialty of Lesser Poland restaurants in Zakopane and Kraków. Delicate, with a distinct cheese flavor and sweetness of cranberry.

Where to Eat?

In Kraków, it is worth visiting milk bars in Kazimierz (such as Milkbar Tomasza) and small family restaurants in side streets. In Zakopane — highland taverns and shepherd's huts. In Tarnów and Nowy Sącz — local inns and regional restaurants.
