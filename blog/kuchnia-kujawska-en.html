Kuyavian Cuisine — Toruń Gingerbread, Żur, and Kołocz

Kuyavian-Pomeranian cuisine is a mix of agricultural and burgher traditions. Find out what is worth eating in Toruń and Kuyavia.

Kuyavian-Pomeranian cuisine is the cuisine of a region where the agricultural traditions of Kuyavia and the burgher traditions of Pomerania meet. This is visible in the dishes: Toruń gingerbread, Kuyavian żur, kołocz, duck. All dishes deeply rooted in tradition and adapted to the region's cold climate.

1. Toruń Gingerbread

The symbol of Toruń and one of the most recognizable dishes of the region. Gingerbread baked from honey, flour, and spices — cinnamon, ginger, cloves. The tradition of baking dates back to the 13th century. It is worth trying it at one of Toruń's gingerbread shops.

2. Kuyavian Żur

Sour soup with rye leaven, served with white sausage, egg, and horseradish. In the Kuyavian version — with potatoes and dried mushrooms. It warms and fills, perfect for cold days.

3. Kuyavian Kołocz

Sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions — poppy seeds, cheese, fruit. It is served at weddings, holidays, and family celebrations. The Kuyavian variant — with honey and nuts.

4. Kuyavian Duck

Roast duck with apples, marjoram, and caraway. It is served with modra kapusta and dumplings. In the Kuyavian version — with dried plums and honey.

5. Kuyavian Pyzy

Potato dumplings with meat filling, steamed or boiled in water. They are served with cracklings or mushroom sauce. A classic of Kuyavian cuisine, deeply rooted in rural tradition.

6. Pickle Soup

Sour soup with pickled cucumbers, potatoes, carrots, and sour cream. It is served with rice or groats. In the Kuyavian version — with dried mushrooms and dill.

7. Kuyavian Carp

A traditional Kuyavian dish, prepared for Christmas Eve and holidays. Carp in jelly or fried, with onion, raisins, and spices. It is served with horseradish or tartar sauce.

8. Kuyavian Bread

Sourdough bread made from rye and wheat flour, with flaxseed and caraway. Characteristic of Kuyavian villages, still baked in home ovens.

Where to Eat?

In Toruń, it is worth visiting gingerbread shops in the Old Town and restaurants serving Kuyavian cuisine. In Bydgoszcz — restaurants in the Old Market Square and on Mill Island. In smaller towns — local inns and taverns, where the cuisine is most authentic.
