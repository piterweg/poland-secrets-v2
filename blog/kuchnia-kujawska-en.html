Kuyavian Cuisine — Toruń Gingerbread, Żur, and Kołocz

Kuyavian-Pomeranian cuisine is a mix of agricultural and burgher traditions. Discover what is worth eating in Toruń and Kuyavia — from gingerbread to carp, with specific places and prices.

Kuyavian-Pomeranian cuisine is the cuisine of a region where two traditions meet: agricultural Kuyavia and burgher Pomerania. Kuyavia is fertile fields, wheat, sugar beets, and potatoes — hence the abundance of bakes, dumplings, and flour dishes in the cuisine. Pomerania and Toruń are Hanseatic cities, where the spice trade flourished since the Middle Ages — hence gingerbread, spices, and sweet pastries. The result is one of the more interesting regional cuisines in Poland: hearty, but with a distinct sweet accent, deeply rooted in both rural and burgher tradition.

This guide shows 8 dishes you must try in Kuyavia and Pomerania, and specific places in Toruń, Bydgoszcz, and smaller towns of the region where you can eat them in an authentic version.

Cities, Bakes, and Gingerbread

1. Toruń Gingerbread

The symbol of Toruń and one of the most recognizable dishes of the region. Gingerbread baked from honey, flour, and spices — cinnamon, ginger, cloves, cardamom, pepper. The tradition of baking dates back to the 13th century, when Toruń — a Hanseatic city — had access to spices brought from all over the world. In the 16th century, Toruń gingerbread was known throughout Europe, and the gingerbread bakers' guild was one of the richest in the city. Today, gingerbread is baked in several Toruń gingerbread shops, and their recipes are closely guarded. The dough matures for up to several months, which makes the gingerbread soft and aromatic.

Price: 25–45 PLN per 500 g in a gingerbread shop, 8–15 PLN for a small packet in a shop. Where: Piernikarnia Kopernik (Toruń, ul. Szeroka 40), Fabryka Cukiernicza Kopernik (Toruń, ul. Żółkiewskiego 34), Piernikarnia Starotoruńska (Toruń, ul. Podmurna 18).

2. Kuyavian Kołocz

Sweet yeast cake with streusel and various additions — poppy seeds, cheese, fruit, nuts. It is served at weddings, holidays, and family celebrations. The Kuyavian variant — with honey and nuts, often with curd cheese mixed with egg yolk. Kołocz is rectangular or round, and the top is sprinkled with streusel made of flour, butter, and sugar. In Kuyavia, kołocz is an almost mandatory element of a wedding — no celebration takes place without it.

Price: 30–50 PLN for a whole cake, 8–12 PLN per slice. Where: Cukiernia Sowa (Toruń, ul. Szeroka 30), Piekarnia Kujawska (Bydgoszcz, ul. Długa 24), local pastry shops in Inowrocław and Włocławek.

3. Kuyavian Żur

Sour soup with rye leaven, served with white sausage, egg, and horseradish. In the Kuyavian version — with potatoes and dried mushrooms, which give the soup a deeper, forest aroma. The leaven is made from rye flour and water, fermenting for several days — only then is the soup cooked on it. Żur warms and fills, perfect for cold days. In Kuyavia, żur is often served in bread — a hollowed-out loaf that soaks up the soup and becomes part of the dish.

Price: 15–25 PLN per bowl. Where: Karczma Kujawska (Toruń, ul. Podmurna 8), Restauracja Luizjana (Bydgoszcz, ul. Niedźwiedzia 8), Bar Mleczny Kujawiak (Toruń, ul. Różana 5).

Meat and Fish Dishes

4. Kuyavian Duck

Roast duck with apples, marjoram, and caraway. It is served with modra kapusta and dumplings. In the Kuyavian version — with dried plums and honey, which give the meat a sweet aftertaste and a more intense colour. The duck is roasted slowly, at least 2 hours, and during roasting it is basted with its own sauce and honey. A restaurant dish, requiring time — you will not find it in milk bars. Kuyavian duck is often served at holidays and family celebrations — it is a “festive” dish.

Price: 55–85 PLN in regional restaurants. Where: Restauracja Szeroka 40 (Toruń, ul. Szeroka 40), Karczma Kujawska (Toruń), Hotel Pod Orłem (Bydgoszcz, ul. Gdańska 14).

5. Kuyavian Carp

A traditional Kuyavian dish, prepared for Christmas Eve and holidays. Carp in jelly or fried, with onion, raisins, and spices. It is served with horseradish or tartar sauce. In the Kuyavian version — the carp is first marinated in vinegar with onion and spices, then fried or served in jelly. Carp has been farmed in the region since the Middle Ages — in Kuyavian ponds, especially around Inowrocław and Włocławek. On Christmas Eve, carp is as important in Kuyavia as in Silesia.

Price: 35–60 PLN per portion. Where: Restauracja Kujawska (Inowrocław, ul. Królowej Jadwigi 1), Karczma Kujawska (Toruń), Gospoda pod Strzechą (Włocławek, ul. Płocka 112).

6. Kuyavian Pyzy

Potato dumplings with meat filling, steamed or boiled in water. They are served with cracklings or mushroom sauce. A classic of Kuyavian cuisine, deeply rooted in rural tradition. Pyzy differ from pierogi in that the dough is made from grated potatoes (not flour), and the filling — from minced meat with onion. In Kuyavia, pyzy are eaten for lunch, especially on Sundays, and served with crackling or mushroom sauce. This is a cheap, filling, and very regional dish — you will not find it in restaurants outside the region.

Price: 20–30 PLN per portion. Where: Bar Mleczny Kujawiak (Toruń), Karczma Kujawska (Toruń), Restauracja Kujawska (Inowrocław).

Soups, Sides, and Bread

7. Pickle Soup

Sour soup with pickled cucumbers, potatoes, carrots, and sour cream. It is served with rice or groats. In the Kuyavian version — with dried mushrooms and dill, which give the soup freshness. Pickle soup is a classic of Polish cuisine, but in Kuyavia it has its own specific character — it is thicker and more sour than in other regions. In many homes it is served with boiled potatoes rather than rice. Pickled cucumbers in Kuyavia are often homemade, with horseradish and garlic.

Price: 12–18 PLN per bowl. Where: Bar Mleczny Kujawiak (Toruń), Karczma Kujawska (Toruń), regional restaurants in Bydgoszcz and Włocławek.

8. Kuyavian Bread

Sourdough bread made from rye and wheat flour, with flaxseed and caraway. Characteristic of Kuyavian villages, still baked in home ovens. Kuyavia is an agricultural region — wheat and rye have grown here for centuries, so bread has always been the basis of nutrition. Kuyavian bread has a dark, moist crumb and a crispy crust — thanks to the long fermentation of the sourdough (up to 24 hours) and baking in a bread oven. In many Kuyavian villages, bread is still baked once a week — usually on Saturday.

Price: 8–15 PLN per loaf. Where: Piekarnia Kujawska (Bydgoszcz, ul. Długa 24), Market in Toruń (Saturday morning), local bakeries in Inowrocław, Włocławek, Aleksandrów Kujawski.

Where to Eat — Specific Places

Toruń (Old Town and surroundings):

  • Karczma Kujawska (ul. Podmurna 8) — a classic of Kuyavian cuisine, żur, duck, pyzy.
  • Restauracja Szeroka 40 (ul. Szeroka 40) — elegant regional cuisine, Kuyavian duck.
  • Bar Mleczny Kujawiak (ul. Różana 5) — cheap and authentic Kuyavian dishes.
  • Piernikarnia Kopernik (ul. Szeroka 40) — Toruń gingerbread, the best in the city.

Bydgoszcz (Old Market and Mill Island):

  • Restauracja Luizjana (ul. Niedźwiedzia 8) — regional cuisine with Kuyavian accents.
  • Hotel Pod Orłem (ul. Gdańska 14) — a classic of Kuyavian cuisine in an elegant version.
  • Piekarnia Kujawska (ul. Długa 24) — Kuyavian bread, kołocz, bakes.

Region (Inowrocław, Włocławek, Aleksandrów Kujawski):

  • Restauracja Kujawska (Inowrocław, ul. Królowej Jadwigi 1) — Kuyavian carp, pyzy, żur.
  • Gospoda pod Strzechą (Włocławek, ul. Płocka 112) — home-style Kuyavian cuisine.
  • Zagroda Kujawska (Aleksandrów Kujawski, ul. Wojska Polskiego 8) — rural cuisine, bread from the oven.

Practical Tips

  • Buy gingerbread in a gingerbread shop, not in a supermarket — only there you will get real, fresh gingerbread from properly matured dough.
  • Kuyavian duck takes time (2 hours of roasting). Book a table in advance, especially on weekends.
  • Kołocz is best at weddings and holidays — on ordinary days it is hard to find in pastry shops. Ask in local bakeries.
  • Buy Kuyavian bread at the market on Saturday morning — then it is the freshest. On weekdays the selection is smaller.
  • Restaurants in the Old Town of Toruń are more expensive and touristy. Go 2 streets further — to milk bars and taverns — where the food is cheaper and better.
  • Kuyavian carp is mainly available during the holiday season (Christmas Eve, Easter). Out of season it is hard to find.

Summary

Kuyavian cuisine is the cuisine of a borderland — agricultural Kuyavia and burgher Pomerania. Hearty, sweet, deeply rooted in tradition. Instead of fashionable dishes, you will find classics here: gingerbread, żur, kołocz, duck, pyzy. Plan your trip to Toruń and Kuyavia to try at least four of the eight dishes — and preferably eat them in places where the cuisine is still home-style, not touristy.
