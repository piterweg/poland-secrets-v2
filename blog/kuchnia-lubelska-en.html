Lublin Cuisine — Cebularze, Pierogi with Bryndza, and Roztocze Trout

The cuisine of the Lublin region is a mix of Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish traditions. Discover what is worth eating in a region where East and West meet — with specific places and prices.

The cuisine of the Lublin region is borderland cuisine — Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish influences meet here. This is visible in the dishes: pierogi with bryndza, cebularze, krupnik, groats, potatoes. All simple, earthy, and deeply filling dishes. The Lublin region was multicultural for centuries — alongside Poles, Ukrainians, Jews, Armenians, and Germans lived here. Each of these groups contributed something to the local cuisine: Jews — bread and fish, Ukrainians — pierogi and groats, Armenians — spices and cold cuts. The result is one of the most diverse regional cuisines in Poland, still little known outside the region.

This guide shows 6 dishes you must try in the Lublin region, and specific places in Lublin, Zamość, and Roztocze where you can eat them in an authentic version.

Bread and Bakes

1. Lublin Cebularz

Flat yeast dough with onion and poppy seeds — one of the most recognizable dishes of the region. Sold at markets and bakeries throughout the Lublin region. The best freshly baked, with butter. Lublin cebularz was added to the EU protected products list in 2014 — it has a protected designation of origin. The baking tradition dates back to the 19th century, when Lublin bakers began baking flat cakes with onion, sprinkled with poppy seeds. The secret of a good cebularz is hand-kneaded yeast dough and onion browned to a golden colour, not raw — this makes the cake slightly sweet, not sharp.

Price: 4–7 PLN per piece. Where: Piekarnia Kuźmiuk (Lublin, ul. Głęboka 23), Cukiernia Sowa (Lublin, Krakowskie Przedmieście 66), Market under the Castle (Lublin, Saturday morning), Piekarnia Zamość (Zamość, ul. Grodzka 24).

2. Lublin Chałka

Sweet yeast bread in the form of a braid, sprinkled with streusel or poppy seeds. Popular for breakfast, with coffee or tea. A symbol of home baking in the Lublin region. Chałka differs from other Polish breads in that it is softer and more delicate — the dough contains more eggs and butter. In the Lublin region, chałka is also baked in a festive version — with dried fruit and raisins, served at Easter. Buy it in a bakery, not a supermarket — it is incomparably better there.

Price: 8–15 PLN per chałka. Where: Piekarnia Kuźmiuk (Lublin), Piekarnia Rarytas (Lublin, ul. Rusałka 12), local bakeries in Zamość and Chełm.

Main Dishes

3. Pierogi with Bryndza

Delicate pierogi with sheep cheese — a specialty of the Polish-Ukrainian borderland. They are served with cracklings or sour cream. Bryndza gives them a characteristic, slightly salty flavour. Bryndza is a sheep cheese produced in Podhale and the Eastern Carpathians — it came to the Lublin region with Ukrainian and Lemko settlers who arrived here after 1947 as part of Operation Vistula. Today, pierogi with bryndza is one of the most recognizable dishes of the Lublin region — served in most regional restaurants, as well as in milk bars. The dough is thin, the filling creamy, with a distinct note of sheep cheese.

Price: 20–30 PLN per portion (8–10 pieces). Where: Bar Mleczny Lubelski (Lublin, ul. Krakowskie Przedmieście 21), Restauracja Kardamon (Lublin, ul. Rynek 11), Karczma Łemkowska (Zamość, ul. Staszica 8).

4. Pierogi with Cabbage and Mushrooms

Traditional pierogi, which in the Lublin region have their own specificity — the filling is more spicy, with marjoram and pepper. They are served at Christmas Eve, weddings, and family celebrations. The filling is made from sauerkraut and dried mushrooms (usually porcini or bay bolete), seasoned with marjoram, pepper, and onion. In the Lublin version, buckwheat groats are sometimes added, which gives the pierogi a more “earthy” character. They are served with cracklings, onion, or sour cream. This is a dish that in the Lublin region is eaten not only at Christmas Eve — it is served in milk bars all year round.

Price: 18–28 PLN per portion. Where: Bar Mleczny Lubelski (Lublin), Restauracja Kardamon (Lublin), Karczma Łemkowska (Zamość).

5. Roztocze Trout

Trout farmed in local farms in Roztocze. Fresh, grilled or from the grate, with horseradish and lemon. One of the best dishes you can eat in the region. Roztocze is a region where trout farming has a long tradition — clean rivers and streams (Wieprz, Tanew, Gorajec) and a cool climate favour farming. In fish farms around Zwierzyniec, Krasnobród, and Józefów, trout are farmed in ponds and cages, then sold to local smokehouses and restaurants. Roztocze trout is served in several ways: grilled, smoked, fried in butter, even in jelly. The best, however, is fresh, straight from the grate, with horseradish and lemon.

Price: 35–55 PLN per portion (1 trout with sides). Where: Gospodarstwo Rybackie Jacnia (Jacnia, Roztocze), Smażalnia Pstrąga Roztocze (Zwierzyniec, ul. Browarna 5), Restauracja Roztocze (Zamość, ul. Partyzantów 6).

Soups and Sides

6. Lublin Krupnik

Barley groat soup with vegetables and meat. In the Lublin version — with dried mushrooms and sour cream. Hearty, warming, perfect for cold days. Krupnik is one of the oldest soups in Polish cuisine — its history dates back to the Middle Ages. In the Lublin region, krupnik has its own specificity: it is thicker, with more groats and meat, and the addition of dried mushrooms (often porcini) gives it a deeper, forest aroma. In the wedding version, krupnik is sometimes served with sour cream and egg yolk, which gives it a creamy consistency. In the Lublin region, krupnik is traditionally served at weddings — it is one of those soups that “must be there”.

Price: 12–20 PLN per bowl. Where: Bar Mleczny Lubelski (Lublin), Restauracja Kardamon (Lublin), Karczma Łemkowska (Zamość).

Where to Eat — Specific Places

Lublin (Old Town and Krakowskie Przedmieście):

  • Bar Mleczny Lubelski (ul. Krakowskie Przedmieście 21) — a classic, cheap and authentic regional dishes.
  • Restauracja Kardamon (Rynek 11) — elegant Lublin cuisine, pierogi with bryndza, krupnik.
  • Cukiernia Sowa (Krakowskie Przedmieście 66) — cebularze, chałka, bakes.
  • Piekarnia Kuźmiuk (ul. Głęboka 23) — the best cebularze in Lublin.

Zamość (Great Market Square and surroundings):

  • Karczma Łemkowska (ul. Staszica 8) — borderland cuisine, pierogi with bryndza and cabbage.
  • Restauracja Roztocze (ul. Partyzantów 6) — Roztocze trout, krupnik.
  • Piekarnia Zamość (ul. Grodzka 24) — cebularze, chałka.

Roztocze (Zwierzyniec, Krasnobród, Józefów):

  • Gospodarstwo Rybackie Jacnia (Jacnia) — fresh farmed trout, grilled.
  • Smażalnia Pstrąga Roztocze (Zwierzyniec, ul. Browarna 5) — trout fried, smoked, in jelly.
  • Agroturystyka Roztocze (Krasnobród) — home-style Lublin cuisine, krupnik, pierogi.

Practical Tips

  • Buy cebularze in the morning — the best are freshly baked. By afternoon, only the second-bake ones remain in bakeries.
  • Order Roztocze trout at fish farms, not at restaurants in the centre of Lublin — there the fish is more expensive and often frozen.
  • Pierogi with bryndza are best in milk bars and small restaurants — not in elegant places in the Old Town.
  • Lublin krupnik is served at weddings and celebrations — in restaurants it is sometimes available, but usually in a “restaurant” version, less thick than homemade.
  • Lublin chałka is best bought in small bakeries — in supermarkets it is usually dry and without character.
  • In the summer season (July–August) in Roztocze it is crowded — on weekends it is worth booking a table at trout smokehouses in advance.

Summary

Lublin cuisine is borderland cuisine — Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish traditions meet on one plate. Hearty, earthy, deeply rooted in the tradition of the multicultural region. Instead of fashionable dishes, you will find classics here: cebularze, pierogi with bryndza, Roztocze trout, krupnik. Plan your trip to the Lublin region to try at least four of the six dishes — and preferably eat them in places where the cuisine is still home-style, not touristy.
