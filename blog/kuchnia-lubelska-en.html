Lublin Cuisine — Cebularze, Pierogi with Bryndza, and Roztocze Trout

The cuisine of the Lublin region is a mix of Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish traditions. Find out what is worth eating in a region where East and West meet.

The cuisine of the Lublin region is borderland cuisine — Polish, Ukrainian, and Jewish influences meet here. This is visible in the dishes: pierogi with bryndza, cebularze, krupnik, groats, potatoes. All simple, earthy, and deeply filling dishes.

1. Lublin Cebularz

Flat yeast dough with onion and poppy seeds — one of the most recognizable dishes of the region. Sold at markets and bakeries throughout the Lublin region. The best freshly baked, with butter. A symbol of Lublin and the whole region.

2. Pierogi with Bryndza

Delicate pierogi with sheep cheese — a specialty of the Polish-Ukrainian borderland. They are served with cracklings or sour cream. Bryndza gives them a characteristic, slightly salty flavor.

3. Roztocze Trout

Trout farmed in local farms in Roztocze. Fresh, grilled or from the grate, with horseradish and lemon. One of the best dishes you can eat in the region.

4. Krupnik

Barley groat soup with vegetables and meat. In the Lublin version — with dried mushrooms and sour cream. Hearty, warming, perfect for cold days.

5. Pierogi with Cabbage and Mushrooms

Traditional pierogi, which in the Lublin region have their own specificity — the filling is more spicy, with marjoram and pepper. They are served at Christmas Eve, weddings, and family celebrations.

6. Lublin Chałka

Sweet yeast bread in the form of a braid, sprinkled with streusel or poppy seeds. Popular for breakfast, with coffee or tea. A symbol of home baking in the Lublin region.

Where to Eat?

In Lublin, it is worth visiting Old Town restaurants and cafés, especially on Krakowskie Przedmieście and around the Castle. In Roztocze — local trout smokehouses and agritourism farms. In Zamość — restaurants around the Great Market Square.
