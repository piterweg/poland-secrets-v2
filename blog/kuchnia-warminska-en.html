Warmia-Masuria Cuisine — Vendace, Marzipan, and Kindziuk

The cuisine of Warmia and Masuria is fish from the lakes, game, mushrooms, and Olsztyn marzipan. Discover what is worth eating in the land of a thousand lakes — with specific places and prices.

The cuisine of Warmia and Masuria is the cuisine of the land of a thousand lakes — based on fish, game, and the gifts of the forest. Vendace, bream, pike, eel — all fish that have been finding their way to the tables of residents of this region for centuries. Warmia and Masuria are two different historical regions: Warmia was Catholic (ruled by the Warmian chapter), Masuria — Protestant (from 1525 it was part of Ducal Prussia). Until 1945, the region belonged to Germany, hence the strong German and Prussian influences in Warmian-Masurian cuisine: marzipan, kindziuk, potato sausage, smoked fish. After World War II, the region was settled by settlers from various parts of Poland, which further enriched the local cuisine. Today Warmia and Masuria is one of the most interesting culinary regions in Poland — on one hand fish-based, on the other — with a clear influence of German cuisine, especially in bakes and cold cuts.

This guide shows 6 dishes you must try in Warmia and Masuria, and specific places in Olsztyn, Giżycko, Mikołajki, and Mrągowo where you can eat them in an authentic, not touristy, version.

Fish from Masurian Lakes

1. Smoked Vendace

A delicate, fatty fish from Masurian lakes. Traditionally smoked in home smokehouses, it tastes completely different from the store version. Vendace is a fish from the salmon family — it has delicate, white meat, rich in fat, and a unique, slightly sweet flavour. It has lived in Masurian lakes for thousands of years — its smoking is a craft passed from father to son in a few fishing families on Lakes Nidzkie, Śniardwy, Mamry, and Bełdany. Vendace is smoked whole, in alder or beech smoke, for several hours. It is served cold, with bread, butter, and lemon, optionally with horseradish. Try it in small, family smokehouses in Ruciane-Nida, Pisz, and Giżycko — the best is fresh, straight from the smokehouse, still warm.

Price: 25–45 PLN per kg in a smokehouse, 15–25 PLN per portion (1–2 pieces) in a fish shop. Where: Wędzarnia Ryb in Ruciane-Nida (ul. Guzianka 4), Wędzarnia Ryb in Pisz (ul. Rybacka 12), Smażalnia Ryb in Giżycko (al. Wojska Polskiego 8).

2. Fish Soup

A soup based on broth from lake fish, with vegetables, potatoes, and spices. It is served with croutons or bread. In the Masurian version — with sour cream and dill, which gives the soup a creamy consistency and freshness. The key to a good fish soup is the broth — it is cooked from several species of fish (usually roach, perch, bream, and pike), and only at the end is the main fish added, e.g. vendace or eel. Fish soup in Masuria is sometimes thickened with egg yolk and sour cream. In the Warmian version, mushrooms (bay bolete or chanterelle) are added; in the Masurian version — dill and bay leaf. Perfect after a day by the lake, especially on cool, autumn evenings.

Price: 15–25 PLN per bowl. Where: Karczma Mazurska (Giżycko, ul. Wodociągowa 8), Restauracja Kormoran (Mikołajki, ul. Portowa 4), Bar Rybny Mazury (Olsztyn, ul. Rynek 12).

3. Pierogi with Fish

A lesser-known version of pierogi, characteristic of lake regions. Fish filling with onion and spices — usually from bream, pike, or pike-perch, finely minced or diced. They are served with butter or sour cream, sometimes with cracklings. Pierogi with fish is one of those dishes that in Warmia and Masuria is eaten primarily at home — less often in restaurants. This is thanks to the fishing tradition: fish were always cheaper than meat here, so they formed the basis of the diet of poor families. In the Warmian version, pierogi with fish is served with mushroom sauce; in the Masurian version — with lard and onion topping. Try them in small, family restaurants and agritourism farms, where they are made by hand.

Price: 20–35 PLN per portion (8–10 pieces). Where: Karczma Mazurska (Giżycko), Restauracja Kormoran (Mikołajki), Agritourism farm on Lake Nidzkie (Ruciane-Nida).

Game and Cold Cuts

4. Kindziuk

A cold cut characteristic of the region — a ripening sausage made from boar or pork meat, seasoned with garlic and marjoram. It is served cold, as a snack or with bread. In the rural version — smoked in a home smokehouse. Kindziuk is one of those dishes that in Warmia and Masuria have German roots — similar sausages (“Kinderwurst”) were made in East Prussia since the 18th century. The name probably comes from the German word “Kind” (child) or from the Old Prussian “kindis” (boar) — which is more likely, because kindziuk was traditionally prepared from boar meat. In the modern version, kindziuk is sometimes also made from pork or beef, but it is always a ripening cold cut, firm, with an intense, smoky flavour. It is served in thin slices, with bread, butter, and pickled cucumber. Buy kindziuk in small butcher shops or at markets in Olsztyn and Giżycko — supermarkets sell fakes.

Price: 60–90 PLN per kg. Where: Sklep Mięsny Warmiński (Olsztyn, ul. Dąbrowszczaków 12), Market in Olsztyn (Saturday morning), local butcher shops in Masuria (Giżycko, Mrągowo, Pisz).

5. Game

Masuria is famous for game — boar, roe deer, red deer. Roasted or stewed, with mushrooms and juniper. It is served in elegant restaurants and village taverns. In the regional version — with mushroom sauce and dumplings. Game is one of those dishes that in Warmia and Masuria have a long tradition — the region is forested (Piska Forest, Napiwodzko-Ramuckie Forests), so there was no shortage of wild game. In the Warmian version, game is stewed with juniper; in the Masurian version — roasted in an oven with mushrooms. It is served with Silesian dumplings or kopytka, with modra kapusta or mushroom sauce. Try it in autumn — it is game season (from October to December), when the meat is most flavourful. In the restaurant version, game is sometimes served with cranberry or blueberry sauce, which gives it a sweet, expressive flavour.

Price: 60–120 PLN per portion in a restaurant. Where: Restauracja Mazurska (Giżycko, ul. Nowowiejska 3), Karczma Warmińska (Olsztyn, ul. Rynek 8), Restauracja Kormoran (Mikołajki, ul. Portowa 4).

Sweets

6. Olsztyn Marzipan

The sweet icon of the region — handmade from almonds and sugar, formed into various shapes. The tradition of making it dates back to the 18th century. You can buy it at pastry shops in Olsztyn and at fairs throughout the region. Olsztyn marzipan is one of the most recognizable sweets in Poland — alongside Toruń marzipan (which has a similar tradition) and Gdańsk marzipan. Olsztyn was for centuries a German city (until 1945 — Allenstein), and marzipan was present here from the 18th century as a sweet associated with Prussian traditions. Contemporary Olsztyn marzipan is produced by several companies (including Sowa, Warmińska) — by hand, from almonds and sugar, without preservatives. It is formed into various shapes: hearts, flowers, animal figurines, even a copy of the Castle of the Warmian Chapter in Olsztyn. Buy marzipan in a pastry shop — in a supermarket you will get fakes that have little to do with real marzipan.

Price: 40–80 PLN per kg in a pastry shop, 15–30 PLN for decorative figurines. Where: Cukiernia Sowa (Olsztyn, ul. Staromiejska 8), Cukiernia Warmińska (Olsztyn, ul. Piłsudskiego 12), Christmas market in Olsztyn (December).

Where to Eat — Specific Places

Olsztyn (Old Town and around the castle):

  • Karczma Warmińska (ul. Rynek 8) — a classic of Warmian cuisine, game, fish soup, kindziuk.
  • Bar Rybny Mazury (ul. Rynek 12) — a fish bar with regional classics, vendace, pierogi with fish.
  • Restauracja Przystań (ul. Nad Jarem 8) — Warmian-Masurian cuisine in an elegant version.
  • Cukiernia Sowa (ul. Staromiejska 8) — Olsztyn marzipan, sękacz, regional bakes.

Masuria (Giżycko, Mikołajki, Mrągowo):

  • Karczma Mazurska (Giżycko, ul. Wodociągowa 8) — a classic of Masurian cuisine, vendace, fish soup, pierogi.
  • Restauracja Kormoran (Mikołajki, ul. Portowa 4) — Masurian cuisine in a modern version, game, fish.
  • Gospoda Mazurska (Mrągowo, ul. Rynek 6) — home-style Masurian cuisine, kindziuk, fish soup.

Smokehouses by the lakes:

  • Wędzarnia Ryb in Ruciane-Nida (ul. Guzianka 4) — smoked vendace, bream, eel.
  • Wędzarnia Ryb in Pisz (ul. Rybacka 12) — fish straight from Lake Pisz, traditionally smoked.
  • Smażalnia Ryb in Giżycko (al. Wojska Polskiego 8) — vendace, bream, pike-perch.

Practical Tips

  • Buy vendace in smokehouses, not in tourist shops — in smokehouses it is cheaper and real; tourist shops often sell frozen fish.
  • Olsztyn marzipan buy in regional pastry shops (Sowa, Warmińska) — not in supermarkets. Real marzipan is soft, has an intense almond flavour, and contains no preservatives.
  • Kindziuk is best from small butcher shops and markets — supermarkets sell fakes.
  • Order game in autumn (October–December) — this is the season when the meat is most flavourful.
  • Fish soup in the home version is more expressive than in restaurants — ask whether the soup is made on site or from concentrate.
  • In the summer season (July–August) in Mikołajki and Giżycko it is crowded — on weekends it is worth booking a table in restaurants in advance.

Summary

Warmian-Masurian cuisine is the cuisine of the land of a thousand lakes — fish-based, game-based, with a clear influence of German and Prussian cuisine. Instead of fashionable dishes, you will find classics here: smoked vendace, fish soup, pierogi with fish, kindziuk, game, and Olsztyn marzipan. Plan your trip to Warmia and Masuria to try at least four of the six dishes — and preferably eat them in Masurian taverns and smokehouses by the lakes, where the cuisine is still home-style, not touristy.
