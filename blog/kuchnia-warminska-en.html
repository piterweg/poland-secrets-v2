Warmia-Masuria Cuisine — Vendace, Marzipan, and Kindziuk

The cuisine of Warmia and Masuria is fish from the lakes, game, mushrooms, and Olsztyn marzipan. Find out what is worth eating in the land of a thousand lakes.

The cuisine of Warmia and Masuria is the cuisine of the land of a thousand lakes — based on fish, game, and the gifts of the forest. Vendace, bream, pike, eel — all fish that have been finding their way to the tables of residents of this region for centuries.

1. Smoked Vendace

A delicate, fatty fish from Masurian lakes. Traditionally smoked in home smokehouses, it tastes completely different from the store version. It is worth trying it by one of the Masurian lakes — especially in small, family smokehouses.

2. Olsztyn Marzipan

The sweet icon of the region — handmade from almonds and sugar, formed into various shapes. The tradition of making it dates back to the 18th century. You can buy it at pastry shops in Olsztyn and at fairs throughout the region.

3. Kindziuk

A cold cut characteristic of the region — a ripening sausage made from boar or pork meat, seasoned with garlic and marjoram. It is served cold, as a snack or with bread. In the rural version — smoked in a home smokehouse.

4. Fish Soup

A soup based on broth from lake fish, with vegetables, potatoes, and spices. It is served with croutons or bread. In the Masurian version — with sour cream and dill. Perfect after a day by the lake.

5. Game

Masuria is famous for game — boar, roe deer, red deer. Roasted or stewed, with mushrooms and juniper. It is served in elegant restaurants and village taverns. In the regional version — with mushroom sauce and dumplings.

6. Pierogi with Fish

A lesser-known version of pierogi, characteristic of lake regions. Fish filling with onion and spices. They are served with butter or sour cream. It is worth trying them in small, family restaurants.

Where to Eat?

In Olsztyn, it is worth visiting restaurants in the Old Town and around the castle. In Giżycko, Mikołajki, and Mrągowo — Masurian taverns and marinas. In smaller towns — local fish smokehouses and agritourism farms.
