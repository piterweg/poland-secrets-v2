What to Eat in Kraków — 10 Dishes You Must Try

Kraków is not just obwarzanek and pierogi. Discover 10 dishes you must try in Lesser Poland's capital — from the Okrąglak zapiekanka to the highlander potato pancake, with specific places and prices.

Kraków is one of the most interesting culinary cities in Poland. Beside the classics — pierogi, żurek, bigos — you will find dishes here that do not exist in other regions: placek po zbójnicku, kremówka, or the makowiec specific to Kraków. The city has over 700 years of culinary tradition, shaped over the centuries by the royal court, the burghers, and the Church. Today you can find it in three places: the milk bars in Kazimierz, the markets of Stary Kleparz and Hala Targowa, and the elegant restaurants of the Old Town. This guide shows the 10 dishes you must try — with specific places, prices, and history.

Classics to Start With

1. Obwarzanek Krakowski

Obwarzanek is the symbol of the city — a round bread roll with a hole, sprinkled with salt, poppy seeds, or sesame. Its history dates back to the 14th century, when Kraków bakers received a royal privilege to bake it. The tradition has survived to this day — obwarzanek has been on the EU protected products list since 2010. Around 150,000 are sold in Kraków daily, mainly from the characteristic glass stalls on the Main Square, at Stary Kleparz, and at the railway stations.

Price: 2–3 PLN per piece. The best is freshly baked, still warm — buy it in the morning, before the city wakes up. By afternoon, stall obwarzanki are often stale.

2. Zapiekanka from Okrąglak

The zapiekanka from Plac Nowy is a legend of Kraków street food. It was created in the 1970s, when the round pavilion in the middle of the square (hence the name “Okrąglak”) began serving zapiekanki with mushrooms, cheese, and garlic sauce. Today Okrąglak is a must on every visit to Kazimierz — especially after a night out, because it is open 24/7.

Price: 10–15 PLN for a classic with mushrooms, 15–20 PLN for an extended one (with ham, salami, jalapeño). Weekend queues can reach 30 minutes, but it is worth it — the ritual is part of the experience.

3. Pierogi with Cabbage and Mushrooms

A classic of Lesser Poland cuisine, present on every Kraków table. Thin dough, filling of sauerkraut and dried mushrooms, topped with fried onion. The best are in small family places in Kazimierz — not in the restaurants on the Main Square, where frozen stock is often used.

Price: 15–25 PLN per portion (8–10 pieces). Where: Przystanek Pierogarnia (ul. Miodowa 24), Babcia Malina (ul. Szewska 14), Kuchnia u Doroty (ul. Augustiańska 4).

Kraków Dishes with History

4. Żurek in Bread

Sour soup with rye leaven, served in a hollowed-out bread loaf. The history of żurek goes back to the Middle Ages — it was a poor man's dish that survived to this day in an elegant version. In Kraków, żurek is served with white sausage, egg, and marjoram — sometimes with horseradish for extra heat.

Price: 15–25 PLN. Where: Karczma Koko (ul. Gołębia 8), Bar Mleczny Flisak (Rynek Główny 27), Kuchnia u Doroty. Perfect for cold, autumn days.

5. Duck with Apples

A traditional Kraków dish, known since the 17th century. Roast duck with apples, marjoram, and onion, served with dumplings or buckwheat. This is a restaurant dish — you will not find it in milk bars. It takes time (roasting takes about 2 hours), so book a table in advance.

Price: 60–100 PLN in elegant restaurants. Where: Wierzynek (Rynek Główny 15), Cyrano de Bergerac (ul. Sławkowska 26), Ancora (ul. Bulwarowa 31).

6. Placek po Zbójnicku

A Podhale dish that has firmly settled in Kraków. It is a potato pancake (similar to the Hungarian-style pancake), topped with meat goulash, grated cheese, and sour cream. The name comes from the zbójnicy — mountain outlaws who, according to legend, ate the pancake before their raids for strength and endurance. In Kraków it is served in inns, tourist pubs, and bars — especially those closer to Kazimierz, where the highlander community lived.

Price: 25–40 PLN per portion. Where: Karczma Pod Wawelem (ul. Powiśle 11), Karczma Młyńska (ul. Młyńska 16), Bar Mleczny Flisak.

Cheeses, Pancakes, and Sweets

7. Podhale Bryndza

Sheep cheese from Podhale, on the EU protected products list. In Kraków, available at markets — Stary Kleparz, Hala Targowa — and in regional shops. Served with cranberry, bread, and honey, or as an addition to pierogi (pierogi with bryndza is a classic).

Price: 20–30 PLN per 250 g. Where: Stary Kleparz (best on Saturday morning), Hala Targowa (ul. Grzegórzecka 3).

8. Fuczki

Pancakes made from sauerkraut, fried in a pan, served with garlic sauce or sour cream. A Lemko dish, but settled in Kraków — especially in Kazimierz, where the Lemko community settled after World War II. Simple, cheap, and filling.

Price: 15–25 PLN. Where: bars in Kazimierz, Kuchnia u Doroty, Bar Mleczny na Kazimierzu (ul. Krakowska 10).

9. Kraków Makowiec

A yeast cake with poppy seeds, dried fruit, and icing, present on every Kraków festive table. The baking tradition dates back to the 19th century. In Kraków, makowiec is served at Christmas, Easter, and family celebrations — usually with a cup of coffee or tea. In pastry shops you can buy both a whole cake and individual slices.

Price: 20–35 PLN for a whole cake, 8–12 PLN per slice. Where: Cukiernia Michałek (ul. Starowiślna 25), Cukiernia Wanda (ul. Grodzka 34).

10. Papal Cream Cake

A cake with vanilla cream and icing, known from Wadowice — the hometown of John Paul II, 45 km from Kraków. The Pope was said to love it, hence the name. In Kraków it is available in every pastry shop — served cold, with coffee or tea. A classic kremówka is about 8 cm high and weighs about 200 g.

Price: 8–12 PLN per portion. Where: Cukiernia Michałek, Cukiernia Wanda, and in Wadowice (Cukiernia Klasztorna, ul. Kościelna 4).

Where to Eat — Specific Places

Milk bars (cheapest and most authentic):

  • Milkbar Tomasza (ul. Świętego Tomasza 24) — the most famous milk bar in Kraków, operating since 1970. Lunchtime queues.
  • Bar Mleczny Flisak (Rynek Główny 27) — a milk bar in the heart of the Old Town, lunch 15–25 PLN.
  • Bar Mleczny na Kazimierzu (ul. Krakowska 10) — a classic of Kraków cuisine in home style.

Markets (for shopping and takeaway):

  • Stary Kleparz — the oldest market in Kraków, since the 13th century. Vegetables, cheeses, cold cuts, obwarzanki. Best on Saturday until 1 PM.
  • Hala Targowa (ul. Grzegórzecka 3) — a hall with regional products, cheeses, cold cuts.

Higher-end restaurants (for dinner):

  • Wierzynek (Rynek Główny 15) — the oldest restaurant in Kraków, operating since 1364. Book 2 days ahead.
  • Cyrano de Bergerac (ul. Sławkowska 26) — an elegant French restaurant with Polish accents.
  • Ancora (ul. Bulwarowa 31) — a restaurant with a view of the Vistula, modern cuisine.

Practical Tips

  • Avoid restaurants on the Main Square — they are expensive and touristy. Go 2 streets further, to Kazimierz or Kleparz. There the food is cheaper, better, and more authentic.
  • Milk bars are subsidised by the city — lunch costs 15–25 PLN. Worth using — it is the cheapest way to eat real Kraków food.
  • Markets work in the morning — Stary Kleparz best on Saturday until 1 PM, Hala Targowa on weekdays until 5 PM.
  • Elegant restaurants (Wierzynek, Cyrano) require bookings 1–2 days ahead, especially on weekends.
  • Obwarzanki buy from stalls in the morning — by afternoon they are stale. The stalls on the Main Square and at Stary Kleparz have the freshest.
  • Okrąglak is open 24/7, but the shortest queues are on weekdays before 6 PM.

Kraków is a city to be eaten slowly — like all of Poland in our slow travel approach. Instead of running from attraction to attraction, sit in a milk bar, order pierogi, and simply watch the city live.
