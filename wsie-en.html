Polish Countryside — Still Alive

The Polish countryside is not just an agricultural landscape — it is above all a living culture that has survived despite industrialization and urbanization. Wooden cottages, Orthodox churches, roadside shrines, paper cutouts, embroidery, sculptures — all of this is still present in the daily life of many regions.

Slow travel through the Polish countryside is a journey through ethnographic regions, each with its own history, language (sometimes literally — like Kashubian), traditions, and crafts. It is a journey that is not about ticking off attractions but about understanding a culture that is still alive.

From Podlasie to Kashubia

Every rural region of Poland has its own character. Podlasie is multiculturalism — Orthodox churches, mosques, wooden cottages. The Lemko Region is dramatic history and wooden churches. Kurpie is paper cutouts and white cottages. Kashubia is chëcze cottages, embroidery, and the Kashubian language. Warmia is rural Gothic churches and red brick.

Many of these regions can be explored by bike or on foot — villages are usually close to each other, and the roads between them lead through the most beautiful corners of the region. It is a journey you can plan for a weekend or a whole week.

How to Visit the Polish Countryside?

The rule is one: slow down. The countryside makes no sense if you try to visit it in a hurry. It is worth stopping in each village for a few hours, entering the church, talking to residents, sitting on a bench in front of a cottage and simply listening to how the village lives.

Many rural regions have open-air museums — outdoor museums where you can see what a village looked like a hundred years ago. It is worth visiting them to understand the context. But the real village is not a museum — it is places where people still live, work, and cultivate traditions.

ALL RURAL REGIONS

Which one will you choose?

Podlasie

PODLASIE • 3–4 days

Multicultural Villages

Orthodox churches, Tatar mosques, and wooden cottages in one landscape. Villages where religions and cultures intersect.

Lemko Region

LESSER POLAND • 3 days

Lemko Villages

Wooden churches, shingle-roofed cottages, and traces of a culture that survived despite deportations. Beskid villages with extraordinary history.

Kurpie

MASOVIA • 2–3 days

Kurpie Wooden Villages

White cottages with ornate shutters, paper cutouts, and wood carvings. Villages where folklore is still alive.

Kociewie

POMERANIA • 2 days

Kociewie Villages with Embroidery

Colorful embroidery, wooden churches, and villages where traditional holidays are still celebrated. A region next to Kashubia.

Warmia

WARMIA-MASURIA • 3 days

Warmian Villages with Gothic

Rural Gothic churches, Warmian red-brick cottages, and roadside shrines. A region where the village meets history.

Kashubia

POMERANIA • 3 days

Kashubian Villages with Chëcze

Thatched wooden cottages, Kashubian embroidery, and bilingual village signs. Villages where Kashubian is still spoken.

Low Beskids

LESSER POLAND • 3 days

Abandoned Lemko Villages

Villages that disappeared from the map after 1947. Today only cottage foundations, old orchards, and lonely churches remain.

Roztocze

LUBLIN REGION • 2–3 days

Roztocze Villages with Mills

Wooden water mills, thatched farmsteads, and villages where time flows more slowly than anywhere else.

Łowicz Masovia

MASOVIA • 2 days

Łowicz Villages with Striped Fabrics

Colorful striped fabrics, paper cutouts, and floral motifs. Księżak villages where folklore is still a living part of everyday life.

Sudeten Villages

LOWER SILESIA • 3 days

Sudeten Villages with Weaving Mills

Villages where weavers' houses have stood side by side since the 18th century. The Sudetes, where industrial history meets the mountains.
